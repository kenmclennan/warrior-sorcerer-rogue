**Section:** Chapter 8 / Equipment
**Pages:** 183-227
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Equipment agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

## Summary

Chapter 8 is the largest single chapter in the source at 45 pages, covering money, weapons, armor, shields, artillery, vehicles, poisons, books/grimoires, medical equipment, and general object rules. As flagged in `notes/source-structure.md`, roughly 70% is out of scope for WSR's ancient-world setting: all modern firearms, energy weapons, explosives, modern vehicles, advanced armor, and sci-fi/cybernetic gear are dropped on sight. The remaining 30% - primitive and historic melee and missile weapons, ancient-and-medieval armor and shields, mundane ancient-world gear, poisons, and ancient vehicles (chariot, rowed ship, mount) - is mostly excellent and usable with only minor modification. The wealth/economy system is abstract (five Wealth Level tiers: Destitute/Poor/Average/Affluent/Wealthy) with no specific coin names or ancient-world currency tables; this requires WSR design. Grimoire/spellbook rules embedded in the "Books" subsection are directly relevant to the Sorcery chapter. The artillery subsection contains a genuinely useful WSR entry: ancient siege engines (ballista, catapult, springald, trebuchet, siege arbalest) with full stats - a surprise given how little of the chapter is kept.

## Canonise / Modify

### Money and Economy

**Canonise (framework): Wealth Level system** (p. 183). The five-tier abstract wealth system (Destitute / Poor / Average / Affluent / Wealthy) with corresponding Item Value tiers (Cheap / Inexpensive / Average / Expensive / Priceless) works cleanly for WSR. It avoids precise coin accounting, which suits the pulp pace of design.md §3.1.

**Modify (flavor): replace modern examples with ancient-world ones.** The example professions used (pilot, astronaut, noble) need replacement with WSR-appropriate profession examples (mercenary, caravan guard, court mage, merchant). Status skill tie-in is fine as written.

**Canonise: Equipment Quality Modifiers** (p. 186). Inferior (-20%), Average (none), Superior (+20%) to skill rolls is clean and setting-agnostic. Canonise as-is. Applies to weapon quality (a masterwork blade), tool quality, armor quality, etc.

**Canonise: Repairing Equipment** (p. 190). Craft/Repair skill roll to restore hit points. Simple and setting-agnostic.

**WSR Design Required: Currency.** The source provides no ancient-world currency table - no coin names, no exchange rates between denominations. The Wealth system substitutes for currency accounting, but players will want some flavor (gold talent, silver drachm, bronze obol, etc.). This is a WSR original design task. See Open Questions.

- Scale: overlapping metal scales - very ancient-world (Egyptian, Assyrian, early Greek). Keep. Note that Scale and Lamellar are both AV 6 and similar Burden/ENC; they represent different construction traditions.
- Cuirbouilli: boiled leather molded to the body, often ornamental. Very appropriate for Leiber-style city fighters and rogue-types who want armor that looks like clothing.
- Ring: soft backing with metal rings sewn on. AV 5 at Light burden - a useful middle ground. Ancient-world appropriate.
- Heavy Clothing: AV 1, no penalties. Useful for travel and when actual armor is inappropriate socially.
- Layering Armor rule (p. 209): keep. Soft armor can be worn under hard armor for additive AV. Layering soft leather under scale is genre-appropriate. Rule: layer soft armor normally; layering anything else under hard armor triples the ENC of the lesser piece.
- Putting Armor On / Taking Armor Off rules (p. 209): keep. 1 round per AV point to put on (simplified: chain takes 3 rounds, plate takes 8). Removing is faster: 3 AV per round, 4 AV per round with Agility success.
- Armor by Hit Location (Option) (p. 209): this is flagged as an option and is incompatible with the Major Wounds system we've adopted (decisions.md). Drop from main rules. Note in GM Book as a variant for groups using hit location flavor.
- Helmets by hit location (p. 209): when hit location is used, helmets have their own AV values. Ancient/Medieval Heavy Helmet = 8, Light Helmet = 4. Note as an optional adjunct to the hit location flavor rule.
- Armor Encumbrance (ENC) formula (p. 208): keep. ENC by SIZ - standard SIZ 11-15 uses listed ENC; smaller characters use 0.8x, larger 1.2x. The per-hit-location ENC fractions (Head 1/10, Arm 1/10 each, Chest 1/5, Abdomen 1/10, Leg 1/5 each) are referenced only if using hit location armor variant.

### Books and Grimoires (pp. 221-222)

**Canonise: the full Books section** with WSR-specific emphasis on grimoires.

The source distinguishes four book types:
- **Instructional Books:** teach a skill (experience check when read successfully, or a default skill increase). Canonise. Ancient-world: clay tablets, papyrus scrolls, wax tablets.
- **Lore Books:** specialized knowledge, private diaries, scholarly overviews. Reading provides automatic skill increase. Can also cause SAN loss (not relevant for WSR as written; the SAN mechanic is a separate chapter decision). Canonise without the SAN loss trigger.
- **Reference Books:** encyclopedias, dictionaries. Augment Knowledge skills; require double the read-time. Canonise.
- **Spellbooks (Grimoires):** "books, scrolls, tablets, hides, scribed stones, knotted ropes, or other means used to convey the information required for a magician or sorcerer to learn magic spells or store spells they already know, or containing secret information about magical beings or demons." Canonise in full. This definition is broad enough to cover all ancient-world sorcerous lore formats. Cross-reference with Sorcery chapter (grimoire-based learning is central to that system). The formats listed here (scrolls, tablets, hides, scribed stones, knotted ropes) are all ancient-world appropriate and should be reflected in the WSR Sorcery chapter's description of grimoires.

**Modify:** The "Books as Objects" physical properties (SIZ 0-2, AV 0-4) are useful as GM tools. Reframe the description for WSR: papyrus scrolls (SIZ 0-1, AV 0), clay tablets (SIZ 1-3, AV 2-4 - durable but fragile if dropped), wax tablets (SIZ 1, AV 1). The source's iron-bound book (AV 6) translates to a stone-covered tablet or metal-cased reliquary.

**Book placement note:** The full grimoire rules (how a sorcerer acquires and uses a grimoire, POW cost to copy spells, secrecy mechanics) live in the Sorcery chapter. This Equipment chapter entry establishes the physical object rules only.

### Other Equipment - General Rules (pp. 186-190, 224-227)

**Canonise:**

- **Skills and Equipment table** (pp. 186-187): cross-reference of skills to relevant equipment types. Keep the ancient-world applicable rows: Appraise, Art, Climb, Craft, Disguise, Fine Manipulation, First Aid, Knowledge, Language, Literacy, Medicine (herbalist materials), Navigate (maps, astrolabe), Repair, Research, Teach. Drop: Gaming (cards/dice can stay with reframing), Science (instruments), Technical Skill.
- **Equipment with Characteristics** (pp. 187-188): general rules for assigning STR/DEX/SIZ/POW/INT to objects. Canonise for magic items (artifacts) and siege weapons. Particularly useful for artifacts with POW reservoirs (cross-reference Sorcery chapter Equipment with Sorcery rules, pp. 188-189).
- **Equipment with Sorcery / Artifacts** (pp. 188-189): a piece of equipment with sorcery spells is called an artifact. Rules: creator sacrifices 1 point of permanent POW per sorcery spell; artifact has a PP reservoir equal to creator's normal PP maximum; artifact goes inert at 0 PP; can store up to double max PP; PP recharged by moving 1 PP from body to artifact per combat round; artifact HP = SIZ; if destroyed, any POW invested is lost. **Canonise in full.** This is the primary mechanism for magical items in WSR. It integrates cleanly with the Sorcery chapter's PP-cost model. Player-created artifacts will be rare (permanent POW cost is steep) but genre-appropriate (Elric's Stormbringer, the sorcerer's bound weapon).
- **Equipment with Requirements** (p. 190): characteristics-based minimums (STR/DEX for weapons, SIZ for armor) and skill-based requirements. Canonise. The weapon tables already list STR/DEX minimums.
- **Repairing Equipment** (p. 190): Repair or Craft skill roll restores damaged HP. First Aid analogy. Canonise.
- **Damage to Inanimate Objects** (pp. 224): SIZ = HP for objects; armor value based on composition; object destroyed at 0 HP if smaller than human-sized. Canonise. Useful for doors, walls, locks, chains - all genre-relevant. The "human-sized hole" rule (larger objects get a hole punched through) is evocative and keeps.
- **Armor Value of Substances** (p. 224): glass (1), hardwood (6), concrete (9). Keep the generic material entries; drop the "3 cm bulletproof glass" (22) and "3 cm steel plate" (28) as those specific measures are less meaningful, though the values are fine GM references.
- **General Qualities of Objects / SIZ of Common Objects** (pp. 224-226): tables of SIZ and HP for common objects. The SIZ table is extensive (pp. 225-226) and covers abstract SIZ-to-weight conversion. Partially keep as a GM reference tool; trim to the non-modern entries or present as a GM Book appendix.

- Disguise Kit (p. 223): keep. Ancient-world appropriate (skin dyes, wigs, costume changes). Used with Disguise skill.

## Defer

- **Starting Equipment with Powers** (pp. 184-185): the rules for beginning play with powered items (magic items, psychic artifacts, superpowered gadgets) are mostly dropped (Mutations, Psychic, Superpowers). The Sorcery portion is relevant: beginning play with an artifact requires sacrificing 1 permanent POW per sorcery spell in the artifact, plus 1 permanent POW per level of spell capacity. This interacts with the character creation chapter. Defer to the Sorcery chapter for the final ruling on whether PCs can begin play with artifacts and at what cost.
- **Making Equipment** (pp. 185-186): rules for crafting items (1 hour per SIZ for simple, 1 day per SIZ for average, 1 week per SIZ for complex). The SIZ-based time framework is potentially useful for ancient-world craft (how long to forge a sword, build a small boat). Defer to the GM Book as a craft-time reference.
- **Charges or Limited-Use Equipment** (p. 190): the rule that equipment runs on charges is primarily relevant to firearms and powered devices. In WSR, the relevant case is artifact PP depletion (already handled by the artifact rules) and blowgun dart supply. Defer any charge mechanics to specific equipment entries rather than a general rule.
- **Encumbrance system** (referenced throughout): the ENC values in the armor and weapon tables presuppose an encumbrance system. The source's system (ENC = fatigue loading) is referenced but not fully reprinted here. Defer to the Characters chapter for the WSR encumbrance decision - whether to use full encumbrance tracking, a simplified "slots" approach, or narrative encumbrance only.
- **Mundane Gear List (adventuring equipment):** the source provides almost no mundane gear list - no rope, torches, waterskin, salt, food rations, tools, lamps, oil, sacks. The torch appears as an improvised weapon but not as mundane gear. This is a significant gap. See Open Questions.
- **Artifact creation costs and POW economy:** the artifact rules establish permanent POW cost, but interaction with the sorcery chapter's existing POW cost model (PP sharing with Fate Points, per decisions.md 2026-05-09) needs explicit design. Defer to the Sorcery chapter.

## Open Questions

1. **Currency and economy - design required.** The source provides no ancient-world currency table. The Wealth Level abstraction (Destitute/Poor/Average/Affluent/Wealthy) handles transactions mechanically, but WSR needs flavor coin names and a rough exchange structure to answer "what does my mercenary get paid?" and "how much does a sword cost in silver?" Recommendation: design a simple three-denomination system (bronze obol / silver drachm or shekel / gold talent or stater) mapped to the Cheap/Inexpensive/Average/Expensive tiers. This is a WSR original design task; the source gives no help here.

2. **Mundane adventuring gear - entirely absent.** No rope, torches, waterskin, backpack, oil, rations, salt, papyrus, wax tablets, grappling hook, lantern, etc. The source provides no mundane gear list at all. This is the largest content gap in this chapter for practical play. WSR needs to design this list from scratch, with items, rough Wealth values, and ENC. It should feel like an ancient-world market: the things Conan buys in Shadizar before descending into the catacombs. Player Book material.

3. **Additional mounts and beasts of burden.** The source provides Horse and horse-drawn vehicle stats, but no mule, camel, elephant, ox, or war elephant. The Creatures chapter may provide some of these in the Natural Animals table (pp. 264-266); check when those notes are written. If not present there, WSR needs to either extrapolate from the Horse stats or design new entries. A camel is essential for desert settings; a war elephant is genre-appropriate (Hannibal, Alexander, etc.).

4. **Poison list expansion.** The source's poisons list is functional but thin. Genre-specific additions worth designing: spider venom (appropriate for dungeon settings), snake venoms beyond cobra and rattlesnake (mamba, viper), plant-based paralytic (henbane, nightshade), mineral poison (white arsenic vs realgar), delayed-onset contact poison. This is a modest expansion, appropriate for GM Book treasure/hazard tables.

5. **Encumbrance decision.** The armor and weapon tables all list ENC values, which only matter if WSR uses an encumbrance system. The source's system ties ENC to the Stamina/Fatigue rules. If WSR adopts a simplified encumbrance approach (e.g., a "load" system by category rather than precise ENC tracking), the ENC column in the tables becomes guidance rather than a tracked number. This needs a decision before drafting the Player Book weapon tables.

6. **Weapon naming for cultural fit.** Several weapon names in the historic table are culturally specific in ways that conflict with WSR's non-specific ancient-world frame: Katana, Wakizashi, Naginata (all Japanese), Rapier (Renaissance European), Sword Cane (18th century). Decision: rename to generic ancient-world equivalents, or keep the names and note that they represent the weapons of specific distant cultures that a PC might acquire on travels. The stats are fine either way; this is a flavor and setting-consistency question.

7. **Signature Equipment mechanic.** Design.md §3.1 flags "signature equipment" as a tool for character distinctiveness. The source provides no native mechanism for this. The Equipment Quality system (+20% for superior tools) is the closest analogue, but it doesn't produce the genre's "Conan's broadsword" or "the Grey Mouser's rapier Scalpel" feel. Options: (a) allow a PC to designate one weapon as their signature weapon, gaining a persistent +10% bonus when using it, at the cost of being at -10% with any other weapon of that class; (b) create a "bonded weapon" rule tied to the character's background. This is a WSR original design task. Flag for the Characters chapter.

8. **Ancient artillery in play.** The siege weapon stats are a genuine find, but their use in play needs guidance. When do PCs encounter siege engines? As defenders (manning a catapult on a city wall), as attackers (directing slaves to operate a ballista), or as targets (fleeing a catapult barrage)? A brief GM Book section on siege scenarios - perhaps a page - would make these stats usable.

## Watch-Items

### Character Distinctiveness (design.md §3.1)

**Equipment level: moderate support.** The weapon choice creates meaningful combat differentiation (a spear specialist plays differently from a sword specialist; a crossbow user differs from a bow user). Equipment Quality (+20% superior) supports the "I take care of my blade" characterization. But the source has no signature equipment mechanic, no family heirloom rule, no "this weapon has a name" framework. The Sorcery artifact rules (permanent POW investment) create a form of distinctive equipment for sorcerers - an artifact sword or ring that the sorcerer has literally sacrificed part of their soul for is distinctively theirs. Non-sorcerers need an equivalent. See Open Question 7.

### Party Cohesion (design.md §3.1)

**Equipment level: no support.** Nothing in this chapter creates party cohesion. No shared equipment rules, no party wealth pool, no "complementary weapons" bonuses. The closest native element: a PC could carry the net or lasso to create a two-PC entangle-then-attack combination, but this is emergent from the rules rather than designed. Flag as a gap that must be addressed elsewhere (Characters chapter or a dedicated "working together" rules section).

### Mook Rules (design.md §2.3, §3.3)

Not applicable to this chapter. The equipment chapter has no implications for mook handling.

### Sorcery Cost (design.md §2.2, §3.3)

**Artifact rules are directly relevant.** The Equipment with Sorcery / Artifacts section establishes that artifacts use PP from their own reservoir. When that reservoir is depleted, the artifact is inert. If a sorcerer recharges the artifact during play (1 PP per combat round transferred from their body), they are depleting their own PP - which is shared with Fate Points (decisions.md 2026-05-09). Sorcerer + artifact creates a three-way PP competition: spell costs, Fate Point costs, artifact recharging. This is genre-appropriate (sorcery is costly) but needs explicit rules language in the Sorcery chapter noting that the artifact's PP pool is separate from the sorcerer's body pool, and that recharging costs body PP.
