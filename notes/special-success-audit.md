# Special Success Audit - BRP UGE

**Audit question:** Where in BRP UGE does a Special Success have mechanical effect? Are those effects combat-only, or do they extend into non-combat contexts (skills, social, perception, healing)? This determines whether replacing the two-tier success ladder (Special + Critical) with a single matching-dice critical mechanic is a clean substitution or requires adaptation work.

---

## Definition

**Source:** p. 128, Chapter 5: System

A Special Success occurs when the roll is equal to or less than **1/5 of the skill rating** (rounding normally). It is a higher tier than ordinary success but lower than Critical (1/20 of skill rating). The Skill Results Table on p. 128 provides pre-calculated thresholds for skill ratings from 01-05 through 116-120 and beyond.

The master definition of what a special success means for skill rolls (non-combat) appears on p. 34, Chapter 3: Skills, under "Describing Results of Successes":

> A **special success** has double the effectiveness of a regular success and/or provides additional information about the subject.

For weapon skill rolls in combat, the definition defers to the Attack and Defense Matrix and the weapon-type special damage tables in Chapter 6: Combat.

The System chapter (p. 130) also notes that on Resistance Rolls, the GM may choose to "characterize results more granularly in the cases of a special or critical success... using the guidelines for levels of success and failure with skills." Resistance rolls themselves have no built-in special success effect - any effect is at GM discretion using the skill guidelines.

## Combat uses

All uses where a Special Success has a defined mechanical effect within combat or directly on combat resolution. Every entry here involves a weapon skill, a combat-phase defensive skill, or a special damage interaction.

1. **Attack roll - weapon-type special effect (p. 142-160, Attack and Defense Matrix).** A Special Success on an attack roll triggers the weapon's special damage type - Bleeding (slashing), Crushing (blunt, doubles damage modifier and forces Stamina roll vs. stun), Impaling (thrusting, doubles rolled damage dice, weapon lodges), Entangling (whip/net/chain, immobilizes), or Knockback (unarmed/shield, SIZ resistance roll, prone result). This is the primary combat special success mechanic and the reason the two-tier system was designed.

2. **Dodge roll - downgrade attack result two steps (p. 37, Dodge skill; Attack and Defense Matrix).** A special success on a Dodge roll downgrades the incoming attack's success level by two steps (e.g. a Special attack becomes a Failure). A critical success on Dodge downgrades by three steps. This is the main defensive payoff for the special/critical distinction.

3. **Parry roll - Attack and Defense Matrix (pp. 142-160).** The Attack and Defense Matrix cross-references attack and parry success levels to produce varied outcomes (e.g. special attack vs. successful parry = attacker does half damage; critical attack vs. special parry = attacker does no damage, defender's weapon damaged). The matrix produces distinct results for every combination of special/critical on attack vs. parry.

4. **Grapple - automatic maintenance and +3 characteristic bonus (p. 40).** A Special Success on a Grapple attack roll means: (a) the attacker automatically succeeds their next Grapple roll to maintain the hold, and (b) the attacker gains a +3 characteristic bonus on all resistance rolls for grapple effects made this round. Grapple is explicitly a combat skill.

5. **Martial Arts - stacked special on combat (p. 45).** Martial Arts is always used alongside a combat skill (Brawl, Melee Weapon, Missile Weapon, or Grapple). On a Special result, the additional base damage roll from Martial Arts is added to whatever special result the combat skill's special success would generate. This is a pure combat interaction.

6. **Throw (in combat) - weapon-type special damage (p. 53, Throw skill).** A Special Success on a Throw roll means the thrown object "does the special damage type as appropriate to its shape." The Throw description explicitly cross-references "Special Successes" (the combat chapter section). This applies when throwing in combat (improvised weapon, knife, javelin not using Missile Weapon).

7. **Shield - crushing special results (p. 50, Shield skill; p. 142-160).** Shields can be used offensively ("Shields do crushing special results, can cause knockbacks, and can be used for aimed attacks"). The shield's special success on an attack roll generates a Knockback or Crushing result as appropriate.

8. **Knockout Attacks - special/critical effects apply (p. 90, Spot Rules).** The Knockout Attack spot rule states: "Special/critical effects (extra damage, bypass armor) apply" to knockout attacks. Slashing and Knockback special effects are specifically excluded from the knockout context, but other special effects (Impaling, Crushing) do apply when attempting a knockout.

9. **Stunning or Subduing - stun weapon special (p. 114, Spot Rules).** When using a stun weapon: "special = max rolled" damage for determining stun duration. This is a combat sub-rule.

**Combat use total: 9 distinct mechanical effects** (Attack/weapon-type special, Dodge downgrade, Parry matrix, Grapple auto-maintenance/bonus, Martial Arts stacking, Throw special damage, Shield special, Knockout application, Stun weapon special).

## Non-combat uses

All uses where a Special Success has a defined mechanical effect outside combat resolution. "Non-combat" means the skill is not a weapon skill, not a combat-phase defensive skill, and the effect does not involve the Attack and Defense Matrix.

1. **Cooperative Skill Rolls - helper's Special gives +30% (p. 130, System chapter).** When assisting another character with a cooperative skill roll, a helper who rolls a Special Success modifies the lead character's skill by +30% (vs. +20% for an ordinary success, +50% for a critical, -10% for a failure). This applies to any skill in any context - exploration, social, craft, knowledge - wherever cooperative rolls are used. This is the only explicit non-combat mechanical value for a Special Success defined in the System chapter itself.

2. **First Aid - heals 2D3 HP instead of 1D3 (p. 39, First Aid skill).** The First Aid skill has an explicit effects table. On an ordinary success, one of the options is heal 1D3 HP to a wound. On a Special Success, that becomes heal 2D3 HP. On a Critical, it becomes 3+1D3 HP. First Aid is the primary immediate post-combat triage skill and is used outside combat context (it cannot normally be used during combat). This is a direct, numerical, non-combat effect.

3. **Medicine - injury healed by 2D3 HP, or 1D3 characteristic points (p. 46, Medicine skill).** The Medicine skill effects table specifies: Special = "an injury is healed by 2D3 hit points, or 1D3 characteristic points are recovered." (Ordinary success: choose from a list of effects including 1D3 HP healing or stabilization. Critical: 2D3 HP or 1D3+1 characteristic points.) Medicine is used between adventures or in downtime contexts, not during combat.

4. **Teach - special success improves skill training to 1D6 (p. 52, Teach skill).** The Teach skill effects are: ordinary success = teaches as per normal training rules; special success = improves skill training to 1D6 (an explicit improvement over the normal training outcome); critical = 1D6+2. This is a pure between-sessions, non-combat context - it governs skill training and character improvement.

5. **Jump - increases jump distance by 50% (p. 42, Jump skill).** A Special Success on a Jump roll increases the jump distance or height by 50% (a Critical doubles it). Additionally, Jump reduces falling damage: "jump can also reduce falling damage by 1D6 per level of success (normal success -1D6, special -2D6, critical -3D6)." The jump-distance increase applies in any context (crossing a gap, leaping in a chase, vaulting an obstacle). The falling damage reduction is also available outside combat (falling from a ledge). Some uses of Jump occur in combat (leaping at an opponent), but the skill has no entry in the Attack and Defense Matrix - it is a Physical skill used in both contexts.

6. **Appraise - additional information (p. 35, Appraise skill).** "A special or critical yields additional information." The source does not specify the exact nature of the information beyond this general statement. In practice this means: ordinary success = knows the approximate worth; special/critical = more precise or additional details (provenance, unusual properties, hidden value). No numerical bonus, but a defined qualitative step-up in outcome. Pure knowledge/social context.

7. **Art - quality and prestige (p. 35, Art skill).** "A critical or special success yields a timeless classic of considerably more value and prestige." Defined qualitative improvement in the artwork's outcome and social reception. No numerical bonus. Pure creative context.

8. **Fine Manipulation - time reduction and concealment (p. 39, Fine Manipulation skill).** "Superior successes take less time, make it easy in the future, show no signs of tampering, or some other similar benefit." The source uses "superior successes" rather than specifying the threshold explicitly, but this is standard BRP shorthand for special/critical. The benefits are: the task is completed more quickly, future attempts at the same task become easier, or evidence of tampering is eliminated. These are practical non-combat consequences (picking a lock, disabling a trap). No fixed numerical value but defined functional effects.

9. **Fly - +10% bonus to next Dodge, Fly, or attack roll in aerial combat (p. 40, Fly skill).** A Special Success on a Fly roll gives a +10% bonus to the next Dodge, Fly, or attack roll in aerial combat (Critical gives +25%). This is partly combat-adjacent (the attack roll bonus applies in aerial combat), but the Fly skill itself is used primarily in non-combat contexts. In WSR this skill is dropped entirely (design.md §1.3 - no flying PCs). Included for completeness but irrelevant to WSR.

10. **Gaming - greater benefits (p. 40, Gaming skill).** "Superior successes reap greater benefits and perhaps even improves one's status." Qualitative outcome improvement in gambling or games of skill. No fixed number but the status improvement is a defined non-combat consequence. Pure social/leisure context.

11. **Psychotherapy - SAN gain 1D3 (p. 48, Psychotherapy skill).** Special = SAN gain of 1D3 (vs. 1D3-1 on ordinary success, 1D3+1 on critical). Explicit numerical bonus in a non-combat context. However, WSR drops both SAN mechanics and the Psychotherapy skill entirely (design.md §1.3, source-6-skills.md Drop section). Included for completeness; irrelevant to WSR.

**Non-combat use total: 11 distinct uses** (Cooperative rolls +30%, First Aid 2D3 heal, Medicine 2D3 heal/char recovery, Teach training improvement, Jump distance/fall reduction, Appraise information, Art quality, Fine Manipulation efficiency/concealment, Fly bonus [WSR-dropped], Gaming outcome, Psychotherapy SAN [WSR-dropped]).

**Non-combat uses relevant to WSR after applying WSR's drops:** 9 (removing Fly and Psychotherapy/SAN, both of which WSR eliminates).

---

## Verdict

**MOSTLY COMBAT, but also a few non-combat uses.**

The combat application is clearly the designed home of the Special Success mechanic - the weapon-type special effects (Bleeding, Crushing, Impaling, Knockback, Entangling) are the entire point of the tier, and the Attack and Defense Matrix is built around the special/critical distinction for both offense and defense. This is where most of the system's textural richness lives.

However, several non-combat skills have explicit special-success mechanics with real numerical consequences - not just vague "better results" but specific numeric upgrades:

- **First Aid**: 2D3 instead of 1D3 HP healed - a genuine in-play difference
- **Medicine**: 2D3 HP or 1D3 characteristic points recovered - affects long-term recovery
- **Teach**: 1D6 skill improvement vs. normal training outcome - affects character growth
- **Cooperative Skill Rolls**: +30% vs. +20% to lead's skill - affects any cooperative task
- **Jump**: +50% distance and -2D6 fall damage vs. -1D6 - affects exploration and chases

These five are mechanically concrete. The remaining non-combat uses (Appraise, Art, Gaming, Fine Manipulation) are qualitative steps - "more information," "better quality" - that any GM would adjudicate similarly under a "you rolled an exceptional success" framing regardless of which mechanic produced it.

The verdict is not COMBAT-ONLY because First Aid, Medicine, Teach, and the Cooperative Roll bonus have hard numbers attached that change outcomes meaningfully.

---

## Recommendation

The proposed replacement (matching-dice critical + weapon-type tables) is clean for all combat uses and for the qualitative non-combat uses. Four non-combat uses need explicit adaptation:

**1. First Aid** (p. 39)
Current: Special = 2D3 HP. Proposed adaptation: a "double" on the percentile dice (both dice show the same digit, e.g. 33, 44) when rolling First Aid is a critical result - treat it as the special-success bonus (2D3 HP). This preserves the graduated outcome without the separate special-success threshold. Alternatively: First Aid always heals 1D3, but the GM describes the triage as exceptional and narrates the broader stabilization - remove the numeric tier entirely, relying only on success/fail/critical. The simplest WSR approach: two tiers only (success = 1D3, critical = 2D3), which maps cleanly to the new system.

**2. Medicine** (p. 46)
Current: Special = 2D3 HP or 1D3 characteristic points. Proposed adaptation: same logic as First Aid - collapse to two tiers (success = 1D3 HP or stabilization options, critical = 2D3 HP or 1D3 characteristic points). The critical threshold in the matching-dice model (double on percentile roll) replaces the 1/5 threshold. Functionally identical outcome distribution.

**3. Teach** (p. 52)
Current: Special = 1D6 skill improvement (ordinary success = normal training, special = 1D6 gain, critical = 1D6+2). Proposed adaptation: collapse to two tiers. Ordinary Teach success = standard training rules as written. Critical Teach success (matching dice) = 1D6 improvement. Drop the middle tier; critical is now the only way to get the boosted improvement. This may slightly reduce the frequency of 1D6 gains (since critical is rarer than special), which is acceptable given WSR's slow-growth philosophy (design.md §4.5).

**4. Cooperative Skill Rolls** (p. 130)
Current: helper's Critical +50%, Special +30%, Success +20%, Failure -10%, Fumble -50%. Proposed adaptation: collapse to three tiers: Critical +50%, Success +20%, Failure -10%, Fumble -50%. Remove the special-success +30% tier; helpers either succeed normally (+20%) or roll a critical (+50%). This is a slight loss of granularity but does not change the cooperative mechanic's fundamental logic. The +20% vs. +30% distinction is small enough that its absence is negligible.

**5. Jump** (p. 42)
Current: Special = +50% distance and -2D6 fall damage. Proposed adaptation: collapse to two tiers. A critical Jump (matching dice) = double distance and -3D6 fall damage. An ordinary success = listed distance and -1D6 fall damage. The +50%/-2D6 middle tier disappears. Given that Jump's primary use in WSR is exploration and chase contexts (not tracked precisely in theatre-of-mind play), the loss of the middle tier is negligible in practice - GMs will describe "you leap farther than expected" for any particularly good roll.

**Summary:** The non-combat adaptations are all simple collapses from three tiers (fail/special/critical) to two (fail/critical). No new mechanics are needed. The weapon-type critical tables in the proposed Rolemaster-style replacement would handle all combat uses directly.
