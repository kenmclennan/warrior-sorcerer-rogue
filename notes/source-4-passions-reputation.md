# Source Notes: Passions & Reputation (Chapter 10, pp. 253-258)

**Source:** BRP UGE ORC Content Document, pp. 253-258
**Sections:** Passions (Option) pp. 253-255; Reputation (Option) pp. 256-258
**Reviewed:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

Passions are emotional bonds - Love, Hate, Fear, Devotion, Loyalty, Honor - rated on D100 like skills and rolled to produce Inspiration (a bonus to a chosen skill) or Despair (a penalty and temporary incapacitation). They grow through successful use and can be reduced voluntarily or through fumbles. Characters start with three passions at chargen (one at 80%, two at 60%) and can gain new ones during play. Reputation is a separate fame/notoriety score that only goes up; it is gained through notable and heroic deeds at the GM's discretion and used in two ways: identifying someone (rolling against their Reputation to see if an NPC recognizes them) or impressing others (augmenting Communication skills). Both systems are structurally clean, clearly optional in BRP UGE, and almost entirely compatible with WSR's tone without alteration. They represent the single strongest genre-alignment cluster in the entire Chapter 10 optional-rules set.

## Canonise

**Passions**

- **The six core passion types (Love, Hate, Fear, Honor, Devotion, Loyalty) with their sub-types.** Each maps directly to the personal-codes framing in `design.md` §2.5 ("Every PC has lines they will not cross, debts they pay, oaths they keep. These are personal, not universal.") and the pulp personal-stakes framing in §1.2 #1 ("PCs have their own motivations: wealth, revenge, escape, glory, a particular love or grudge"). These six exhaustively cover sword-and-sorcery character drivers.

- **Inspiration mechanic (roll against passion rating to augment a skill).** Produces exactly the pulp "hero driven by his oath / hatred / love overcomes impossible odds" moment described in `design.md` §1.2 #1 and §4.2 ("When to swear a vow"). Critical (+50%), Special (+30%), Success (+20%) bonuses for duration of the task; one attempt per scene. Keep as-is.

- **Despair table (Fumble result: -1D10% to passion, overcome by despair for 1D10 rounds to 1D3 days depending on roll).** The downside risk is what makes Inspiration a real choice, not a free buff. Directly supports `design.md` §4.4 ("A loss looks like survival at unbearable cost"). Keeps despair duration short enough not to break combat scenes but meaningful enough to sting.

- **Failure penalty (-10% to all further rolls in scene).** The middle-failure outcome. Appropriate; already small enough not to be a session-stopper.

- **Passion improvement via experience check (same as skills).** Integrates cleanly with BRP's skill-improvement chassis. No new machinery needed. Passions feel more "lived in" as they grow. `design.md` §4.5 says growth happens in experience; passions growing through use is exactly that.

- **Passion loss via fumbled Inspiration roll (-1D10%).** Passion extinguished at 0% and cannot return without extraordinary circumstances. "Passion exhausted" is a strong genre beat - the man whose hatred finally burned itself out, the soldier who lost his faith. `design.md` §4.4 (survival at unbearable cost).

- **Starting passions: player-chosen, one at 80% and two at 60%.** Clean, fast, and player-authored. Directly produces character distinctiveness without classes (`design.md` §3.1). The player defines *what* the passion is; the choice characterizes immediately.

- **Gaining passions in play (new passion starts at 60%, or higher based on circumstances).** Keeps passions alive as a campaigning resource across sessions. `design.md` §4.5 ("recurring elements knit sessions together: a vow they have not yet been able to keep").

- **Mandatory rolls at 80%+ (GM may require roll when character acts contrary to a deeply-held passion).** This is the teeth of the system. A Hate at 95% is not just a flavour note; it constrains behaviour. `design.md` §2.5 ("anti-heroes with personal codes") - a code that costs nothing is not a code. The two exit ramps (reduce passion below 80%, or oppose with a conflicting passion) are sensible safety valves.

- **Conflicting passion resolution via opposed roll.** Character with two high-rated conflicting passions (Loyalty to guild vs Love of rival) can resolve tension mechanically. Strong party-cohesion and drama tool. `design.md` §3.1 ("Provide hooks for party cohesion - mutual debts").

- **Voluntarily reducing a passion.** The mechanic for letting a passion fade by choice (roll as experience check; if not tested, reduce by 1D6) is subtle and correct. The man who has largely forgiven his old enemy; the exile who stops caring about the homeland. Supports `design.md` §4.2 ("when to break a vow").

- **Demonstration and Support (using Loyalty/Love/Devotion to convince others they share the same cause).** The social leverage application of passions. Adds a way passions create party bonds: if you can roll your Loyalty to persuade your companion their duty aligns with yours, passions bind PCs together as well as defining them. `design.md` §3.1 (party cohesion), §4.2 (what players decide at the larger level).

**Reputation**

- **Reputation as a one-way-ratchet score (only increases, never decreases; GM tracks through deeds).** Fits `design.md` §1.3 ("Zero-to-hero arcs rejected - growth happens in experience and reputation, not raw power") perfectly. A rising-only score means old accomplishments are permanent weight the PC carries, not a resource to spend and replenish.

- **Reputation Gains table (Notable +1D3, Extraordinary +1D6, Heroic +2D6 or more) with illustrative deed examples.** The three-tier structure is clean. Many of the BRP examples are directly genre-appropriate for WSR: swearing an oath of import, defeating a superior foe, earning a major enemy, kin-slaying, breaking a major oath, a spectacular defeat. Keep all three tiers; cull the modern-context examples (blogs, media, celebrities) in our output.

- **Identifying Someone (roll against Reputation to see if NPC recognizes the character).** The social existence of a famous/infamous PC in the world. A character can be recognized by strangers, or can attempt to learn who an NPC is by rolling against the NPC's Reputation. `design.md` §4.5 ("PCs grow in reputation; old enemies return, old patrons reveal new agendas").

- **Impressing Others (Reputation roll augments Communication skill - same success-level table as Inspiration).** "Do you know who I am?" as a mechanical tool. Critical (+50%), Special (+30%), Success (+20%), Failure (-20%), Fumble (-50%) to the Communication skill used. The fumble penalty (notoriety working against you, or a disastrous case of mistaken identity) is exactly right for the genre.

- **Reputation can be gained for notoriety as well as heroism (behaving dishonorably, failing spectacularly, committing heinous acts also generate Reputation).** Critically genre-aligned. Elric's kinslaying is his most defining reputation event. The system explicitly notes "it's possible to gain Reputation for an act and lose Honor for it at the same time" - this sentence is the core of the anti-hero framing in `design.md` §1.2 #2.

- **Tracking Reputation sources (player records what they are famous for, not just the number).** The label-what-it-is-for convention means a character might have Reputation 45% earned entirely from acts of infamy. The "what you are known for" record is a campaign-history tool. `design.md` §4.5 (sessions accumulate weight; old deeds come due).

## Drop

**Passions**

- **The example of Hate motivated by racism/bigotry** (p. 253 mentions "Hatred unfortunately motivates many people, whether through racism, bigotry..."). The mechanic is kept; this illustrative framing is not reproduced. `design.md` §3.2 ("The source's prejudices do not survive the port"). Replace with genre-appropriate examples: blood feuds, betrayals, the enmity of a former patron.

**Reputation**

- **Modern-context Reputation Gain examples** (blogs, popular music, celebrity, newspaper coverage, being the subject of a documentary or popular book). These are genre-alien. Drop from our example list entirely; the deed categories (Notable / Extraordinary / Heroic) and their tier structure are kept. `design.md` §1.2 #3 (ancient-world setting). Replacement examples for WSR's example list: carried by word of mouth, minstrel's song, temple decree, a rival's boast that backfired.

- **"Someone can always try to deny who they are, but this grows increasingly difficult in more modern settings"** (p. 258, last paragraph of Identifying Someone). The "more modern settings" caveat is irrelevant in WSR's ancient world, where identity is verified by witnesses, marks, and lineage. Omit the sentence; the core "deny identity" possibility is kept (it is genre-appropriate - a disguised Conan is a recurring Howard trope).

## Modify

**Passions**

- **Honor's Dishonor table.** Canonise the concept and the table structure, but revise specific entries for WSR's ancient-world anti-hero frame. Several entries are quasi-chivalric and feel more Pendragon than sword-and-sorcery: "Attacking an unarmed foe" (-5%) is contextually fraught for characters whose code may explicitly permit it; "Refusing to offer mercy when it is deserved" (-5%) implies a universal mercy standard that conflicts with `design.md` §1.2 #2 (anti-heroes with personal codes). **The fix:** Honor in WSR is explicitly *personal*, not universal. The Dishonor table becomes a set of illustrative examples, not a mandatory ledger. Each PC's Honor passion should have a brief player-authored list of their personal dishonorable acts - with the BRP table as a default scaffold for players who want it. The core mechanic (Honor is a skill-rated passion that can be inspired or lost) is unchanged. `design.md` §2.5 ("their own individual code of honor should be developed").

- **Devotion (deity) passion.** The passion type is canonical; the framing needs a WSR filter. In BRP, Devotion is described in terms of sincere faith and a "small measure of courage" - this is too mild for WSR's gods, who are alien, demanding, and dangerous. **The modify:** Devotion in WSR is not gentle faith; it is a binding. Mechanically identical, but the text framing should reflect that devotion to a dark god is as valid as devotion to a protecting ancestor spirit, and both exact costs. `design.md` §2.2 (magic as transaction with things beyond the world).

- **Starting passion assignment (one at 80%, two at 60%).** The values are fine; the process needs a WSR chargen hook. **The modify:** In the WSR Player Book chargen flow, passions are chosen at chargen as part of the character concept step, after background/culture is chosen but before skill allocation. Culture may suggest or constrain passion types (a nomad character is more likely to have Loyalty (community) than Devotion (city temple)). This is a presentation/integration change, not a mechanical one.

- **Passion list extensibility.** BRP notes that Distrust, Respect, Greed can also be passions. WSR should give a short curated WSR-specific extension list rather than leaving it open-ended: **Greed** (Leiber's "Grey Mouser" archetype), **Vengeance** (more specific than Hate - focused on a single wrong to be righted), **Dread** (a cosmic or sorcerous dread distinct from Fear of a specific individual). These slot under the existing Inspiration/Despair mechanic without modification. `design.md` §1.1 (Leiber's picaresque driver), §1.2 #1 (personal stakes).

**Reputation**

- **Reputation Gains table - example lists.** Cull and rewrite the illustrative deeds for the ancient-world context (see Drop above). The tier structure and dice values (Notable +1D3, Extraordinary +1D6, Heroic +2D6+) are kept exactly. **WSR-specific examples to add:** for Heroic: "defeating a sorcerer or supernatural being," "leading a warband to a decisive victory," "surviving a widely-witnessed doom." For Notable: "besting a known warrior in public combat," "breaking a city gate single-handed," "surviving a cursed place." `design.md` §1.2 #1 and §1.2 #5 (exceptional but mortal heroes).

- **Reputation Modifiers (referenced twice in the source text but the actual table is not on these pages - the source text says "see Reputation Modifiers" without printing the table on pp. 256-258).** This table must be located in the source (likely nearby, possibly on a page before or after this range). Flag for follow-up read. **For WSR:** the modifier framework should account for cultural scope - a warrior famous in one city-state may be unknown on another continent. Scope modifiers (local, regional, legendary) would serve the episodic structure of WSR better than a flat score. `design.md` §4.1 (sessions are short stories; scope is local).

## Defer

- **Integration with Allegiance (Chapter 10, pp. 250-252).** Devotion passion and Allegiance mechanics may overlap or reinforce. The Allegiance system (divine patronage, intervention) is a separate read; decisions about whether Devotion passion + Allegiance co-exist, merge, or one is dropped in favour of the other are deferred to the Allegiance notes. `design.md` §3.3 (open questions deferred to chapter-time).

- **Integration with Sanity / Corruption.** Fear passion and a potential Corruption track (modified Sanity, see source pp. 258-261) may interact. A character with a very high Fear passion toward sorcery who is exposed to cosmic horror - does the Corruption track subsume the Fear response, or do they stack? Defer to Sanity/Corruption notes. `design.md` §3.3.

- **Reputation Modifiers table location.** The source text references "Reputation Modifiers" on pp. 256-258 without printing the full table there. Possible the table is in Chapter 9 (the optional rules checklist section) or nearby in Chapter 10. Requires a targeted re-read before the WSR Reputation section can be written. Defer to a follow-up source read pass.

- **Passion intersections with the magic chapter.** A sorcerer's Devotion to their patron demon or dark power could operate mechanically as a Devotion passion - this would create a cross-chapter link (passion system + sorcery cost mechanic). Defer until the sorcery chapter notes are written and the cost framework is decided. `design.md` §3.3 (sorcery cost mechanic deferred).

## Open Questions

1. **Chargen placement in Player Book.** Passions clearly belong in the Player Book chargen chapter. The question is whether they get a dedicated sub-section or are integrated into the "defining your character" step alongside backgrounds. Given they are rated like skills and started at chargen, they should appear in the skill-allocation section or immediately after it, with a clear note that they are required (not optional) in WSR. Are they required? This needs a decision: BRP marks them optional; WSR almost certainly should make them mandatory for the personal-stakes promise to work. Recommend: mandatory, minimum two passions at chargen.

2. **Reputation placement.** Reputation is primarily a campaigning mechanic and its primary user is the GM (who awards gains). But players need to know how to use it in play (Identifying Someone, Impressing Others). Candidate for both books: Player Book gets the using-Reputation mechanics; GM Book gets the awarding-Reputation guidance and the deed examples table. Is a split treatment acceptable, or should Reputation live in one book with cross-references?

3. **Starting Reputation.** BRP Reputation section says nothing about starting values. Do PCs begin with 0 Reputation? Or do backgrounds/cultures grant starting Reputation (a character from a noble family might start with Reputation 10% from family name alone)? This needs a decision before chargen is written.

4. **Passion and Reputation interaction at chargen.** BRP notes passions can be modified based on culture/origin. WSR should define whether the culture/background step at chargen adjusts starting passion values or types, and whether any backgrounds grant starting Reputation. This is a chargen design question, not a source-material question - but it must be answered before writing the Player Book chargen chapter.

5. **Minimum passions.** Should WSR enforce a minimum number of passions? One each of Love, Hate or Fear, and Loyalty would strongly encode the sword-and-sorcery character profile. Or is three player-chosen (as BRP suggests) sufficient? The risk of fully free choice is a PC with three mild Devotion passions and no interpersonal stakes - structurally wrong for the genre.

6. **Reputation Modifiers table.** The source references this table but the pages reviewed (256-258) do not contain it in full. A follow-up read is needed before the Reputation sub-system can be fully extracted.

## Watch-Items

### Character distinctiveness

**Strong match - canonise core.** Passions are the primary character-distinctiveness mechanism for WSR. In a classless percentile system, two warriors with the same skill spread are otherwise identical; a warrior with Hate (serpent cult) 85% and Loyalty (mercenary company) 60% is immediately distinct from a warrior with Love (family) 80% and Honor 75%. The three-passion chargen requirement (one high, two medium) with player-authored targets produces unique characters without classes, backgrounds, or trait lists. This directly addresses `design.md` §3.1 watch-item #1. Passions are not supplementary distinctiveness - they *are* the distinctiveness mechanic for WSR. The skill system and cultural background narrow the character; the passions individualize them.

### Party cohesion

**Strong match - multiple mechanisms.** Three distinct cohesion hooks found:

1. **Shared passions.** Two PCs with overlapping passions (both have Hate (the Serpent Throne)) have an immediate in-world bond and can potentially inspire each other. The Demonstration and Support mechanic (rolling Loyalty or Love to convince a companion they share the cause) is a direct mechanical hook for party action.

2. **Conflicting passions creating drama without fracture.** The opposed-passion resolution mechanic (PC with Loyalty to party vs Loyalty to guild uses opposed roll to determine which wins) means intra-party tension is mechanically handled without requiring the GM to adjudicate fiat. This prevents passion conflicts from becoming party-splitting but keeps them dramatically live.

3. **Reputation as shared currency.** PCs who witness each other's great deeds gain a Reputation knowledge base - they can roll against each other's Reputation scores. A party that has adventured together is a party that can credibly invoke each other's fame when negotiating with NPCs. Shared Reputation history is a social cohesion layer that grows naturally with play.

Watch-item #2 (party cohesion) is addressed by Passions + Reputation together more completely than any other Chapter 10 optional rule.

### Mook rules

No match. Neither Passions nor Reputation contain any streamlined-combat or minion-handling content. Watch-item #3 remains unresolved.

### Sorcery cost

Partial potential match, but not in these pages. Devotion passion could serve as a PC's binding cost to a patron power (sorcerous or divine) if the magic chapter uses it. This is a cross-chapter integration possibility, not a direct cost mechanic. Watch-item #4 remains open; defer to sorcery chapter notes.

## Book Placement

**Passions - Player Book (primary), GM Book (reference)**

Passions are a player-facing, chargen-integrated, in-session mechanic. The full rules - passion types, starting values, Inspiration/Despair table, Mandatory Rolls, gaining passions in play, voluntarily reducing, improvement - belong in the Player Book as a dedicated sub-section of the character chapter. A brief cross-reference note in the GM Book (covering when to call for mandatory rolls and how to introduce new passions in play) is appropriate, but the full mechanical text lives in the Player Book.

**Reputation - Both books, split treatment**

Player Book gets: Using Reputation (Identifying Someone, Impressing Others, the success-level modifier table). These are in-play player-facing mechanics.

GM Book gets: Gaining Reputation (the full deed examples table across all three tiers, guidance on when to award gains, the Reputation Modifiers table once located). These are GM-discretion tools and campaign-pacing decisions. The GM Book section should also cover Reputation scope (local vs regional vs legendary) once the modifier table is found and the scope question is resolved.
