# Source Notes: Magic (BRP UGE pp. 56-70)

**Section:** Chapter 4 / Powers / Magic (first sub-section)
**Pages:** 56-70
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Magic agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Recommendation

**Option B: Keep as hedge magic / folk magic - but tightly scoped, renamed, and mechanically separated from Sorcery.**

The source Magic system and WSR's Sorcery are structurally similar (both PP-driven, both grimoire-based, both use a memorization cap at INT/2) but tonally opposite in almost every respect. Sorcery is automatic, alien, and grimoire-gated by high POW; Magic requires a skill roll, is learnable by anyone, and frames its practitioners as "magicians" with "wands and staffs" - a quasi-D&D wizard idiom that cuts against the WSR tone.

However, three things argue against dropping Magic entirely:

1. **Tonal niche exists.** The genre genuinely has a lower tier of magic users: the cunning-woman with her herbal charms, the tribal shaman who reads omens, the hedge-priest who wards a door and speaks to animals. Solomon Kane encounters these figures; so does Conan. They are not Sorcerers - they do not make pacts with demons or risk their souls - but they are not mere mundanes either. BRP Magic, stripped of its D&D-wizard framing, can fill this niche.

2. **Spell list has some genuinely useful entries.** Several Magic spells have no Sorcery equivalent and are tonally appropriate for WSR's setting: Dark/Light, Illusion, Vision, Perception, Change, Ward. These can be reframed as hedge-charms rather than wizard spells.

3. **The skill-roll mechanic is the differentiator that matters.** Sorcery never fails (no roll to cast); Magic can fail. This is the defining distinction: Sorcery is a transaction with something beyond the world; hedge magic is a skilled human practice that sometimes doesn't work. That distinction is dramatically useful and genre-coherent.

**The critical constraint:** If hedge magic is kept, it must be mechanically marked as lesser than Sorcery in every respect: lower social status, smaller spell list, no demon summoning, no permanent-POW costs (because it never goes that deep), and visually quieter. It should feel like the difference between a village wisewoman and Elric.

**What to drop from the Magic system even if hedge magic is kept:** the Familiar sub-system, the Wizard's Staff sub-system, the elemental conjuring spells, all combat-damage spells (Blast, Fire, Frost, Lightning, Wounding), and every utility spell that implies a D&D wizard rather than a cunning-folk practitioner. This reduces the spell list from 30 entries to roughly 8-10 usable ones.

**Alternative argument for Option A (drop entirely):** If the design goal of §3.1 ("focused, opinionated extraction") is applied strictly, one magic system is better than two. Sorcery already handles the full range from minor dark arts to great demon-binding. Adding hedge magic risks blurring Sorcery's identity as the extraordinary, corrupting thing. This is a legitimate position and the design call belongs to the user.

**My recommendation stands as Option B** because the genre supports the distinction and the usable spell list is small enough that hedge magic would not crowd the book. But it is a close call and I flag the counter-argument explicitly.

## Summary

BRP Magic is a skill-based casting system: each spell is a separate skill (starting at INT x 1), casting costs PP equal to the spell's level, and a percentile roll determines success. Failure wastes 1 PP; fumble loses all intended PP and bars re-casting for one round. The system has a memorization limit (INT/2 spells, same as Sorcery's), a grimoire requirement (spellbook), and a familiar sub-system (bound animal, costs 1 permanent POW). A wizard's staff sub-system (also costs 1 permanent POW) provides a PP reservoir and a combat weapon. Magic can be learned by non-magicians at a reduced INT cap (INT/4 spells, no grimoire creation, no research). Becoming a full magician requires apprenticeship to a skilled wizard, taking (30 minus INT) months.

The spell list has 30 named entries plus Ward (which also appears in the Sorcery section). Professions with formal access: Occultist, Priest, Shaman, Wizard. Spells are learned via grimoire study, instruction, or research (6 months of INT+POW rolls).

Key contrast with Sorcery (pp. 87-102):
- **Magic:** skill roll required, any character eligible, PP cost per level, failure possible, social framing is "wizard/magician" (staff, robe, familiar)
- **Sorcery:** no skill roll, POW 16+ required, PP cost per level, automatic success, social framing is dark pact-maker

## Canonise

These spells are compatible with a hedge-magic framing and have no close Sorcery equivalent. They are small, quiet, and appropriately limited.

- **Dark (1).** Absolute darkness, 3m radius + 3m/level, 15 minutes. An ancient-world cunning-person who can call true darkness has a genuine power - useful for cover, escape, ritual atmosphere. Genre-compatible. Rename candidate: "Night-Shroud" or "Veil of Dark." In WSR the visual drama is implicit (§2.2).

- **Light (1).** Illuminates 3m radius/level, 15 minutes. The counterpart to Dark. Both together form a minimal light-manipulation toolkit appropriate to folk magic. Low cost, low drama, useful at the table. Keep as-is tonally.

- **Illusion (1).** Visual illusion, 3 SIZ/level, 15 minutes. The cunning-woman who crafts illusions to frighten enemies or disguise a camp is a genuine genre figure. Detect via Idea roll or POW vs INT resistance. No sound/scent. This is a humble, limited version of deception magic - appropriate to hedge magic's register. Note: Sorcery has Liken Shape (shapeshifting) but not pure illusion. This fills a gap. Keep.

- **Perception (1).** Detects nearest example of a specified thing (traps, gold, magic, secret doors, unfamiliar thoughts) within 60m, instantaneous. At 1 PP/level this is expensive for what it does. Reframed as hedge magic, it reads as divination: a shaman who can feel where danger lies, a wisewoman who senses hidden things. Genre-appropriate as a limited divination charm. Keep. Rename candidate: "The Cunning Sense" or "Second Sight."

- **Vision (1).** Remote viewing of familiar places (100m) or unfamiliar (10m); each additional level adds +1 day into the past. Touch an object for psychometric visions. 10 combat rounds per casting, caster immobile during. This is the most genre-resonant spell in the entire Magic list. Object-reading, remote viewing, glimpsing the past through old things - this is exactly the kind of low-key but unsettling folk magic that fits the WSR world's deep-past-breathing aesthetic (design.md §2.4). Rename candidate: "The Watcher's Eye" or "Reading the Old Ways." Keep.

## Drop

The following are dropped regardless of whether hedge magic is kept:

- **Heal (3).** Mandatory drop per design.md §1.3 ("No healing magic") and §2.2 ("No healing magic"). Do not canonise in any form.

- **Blast (3), Fire (3), Frost (3), Lightning (3), Wounding (3).** All are direct-damage combat spells. Hedge magic does not throw fireballs or lightning bolts; that is Sorcery's domain. These spells also require a 3 PP/level cost that is expensive relative to the hedge-magic register. Dropping all five keeps hedge magic clearly inferior in raw destructive capacity to Sorcery.

- **Conjure (type) Elemental (1).** Sorcery handles elemental summoning; a second entry point for summoned creatures would blur the distinction. Drop.

- **Control (3).** Mind control. Sorcery has Muddle (disorient) and Inescapable Bonds (immobilize); adding a third mind-control vector from hedge magic would make the topic crowded. Drop.

- **Diminish (1), Enhance (1).** Characteristic manipulation. Sorcery handles these better (Characteristic Enhancement spells, Bolster the Soul, etc.). Duplicating in hedge magic serves no purpose. Drop.

- **Dull (1), Sharpen (1), Protection (1), Resistance (1).** Tactical buffs and debuffs. Sorcery covers weapon enhancement (Hell's Razor, Sorcerous Armor). These hedge-magic versions are lower-grade versions of the same thing. Drop in favor of Sorcery's versions.

- **Lift (1).** Telekinesis. D&D wizard utility with no genre resonance. Drop.

- **Seal (1), Unseal (1).** Object-bonding utility. Analogous to Make Fast (dropped from Sorcery for the same reason - flavorless per §2.2). Drop.

- **Teleport (1).** Instantaneous transport. Entirely wrong register for sword & sorcery. Drop.

- **Wall (1).** Tactical barrier. Explicitly grid-dependent in its examples; anti-theatre-of-mind per design.md §3.1 and §3.2. Drop.

- **The Familiar sub-system (pp. 58-59).** The familiar mechanic (3 months of ritual preparation, permanent POW cost, bound animal) is mechanically elaborate and tonally ambiguous. In the BRP framing it explicitly mirrors a D&D wizard's familiar. The genre does have animals associated with sorcerous figures (Elric's birds, various shamanic totems) but these are narrative rather than mechanical. The familiar's mechanical functions (PP reservoir, sensory sharing, telepathic command) overlap with Sorcery's demon-binding sub-system and a bound demon can fill the same narrative role with more genre weight. Drop the familiar system; note that a WSR GM can roleplay a shaman's totem animal without mechanics.

- **The Wizard's Staff sub-system (pp. 59-60).** The staff as a PP reservoir (costs 1 permanent POW, stores up to POW-1 points) is a clean mechanic, but the framing - "a sign of authority, skill, and mastery," "a wizard is referred to as a wizard" - is exactly the D&D-wizard register WSR is rejecting per §1.3 ("Become D&D in disguise"). The PP-reservoir concept is already present in Sorcery's Brazier of Power (more tonally appropriate: it's a fixed object in a sorcerer's sanctum, not a portable prop). Drop the staff sub-system.

- **Becoming a Magician / Use of Spells by Non-Magicians.** If hedge magic is kept, the social structure of "becoming a magician" via apprenticeship (30 minus INT months) will be redesigned for WSR's setting. The formal magician/non-magician distinction and its specific rules (non-magicians limited to INT/4 levels, cannot research, cannot create grimoires) are a BRP UGE structure that may not survive wholesale. Defer to chapter design.

## Modify

These spells survive with reframing, renaming, or mechanic adjustment:

- **Change (1).** Polymorph: transform animal-to-animal, vegetable-to-vegetable, mineral-to-mineral. 3 SIZ/level, 15 minutes, 30m range. Resistance roll if unwilling. The scope limitation (same general type) keeps it grounded: this is not D&D polymorph. A cunning-woman who can turn a rat into a crow, or a trapped PC into a smaller animal for escape, is genre-appropriate. However: the "same type" restriction means Change cannot produce the more dramatic shapeshifts of sword & sorcery (a sorcerer becoming a wolf would require Sorcery's Liken Shape instead). In hedge-magic framing, Change becomes a more limited charm: animal-to-animal and object-to-object only, caster-touch, used for camouflage and escape rather than transformation drama. Rename candidate: "Shape-Shift" or "The Cunning Change." Conditionally keep if hedge magic is adopted; would need WSR-flavored description.

- **Countermagic (1).** Reflects spells back at the caster; must overcome incoming spell's level. Duration 5 minutes. This is a protective ward mechanic. In hedge-magic framing it reads as a warding charm - "no ill magic shall touch you while this holds." The reflection mechanic (the spell turns back on its sender) is tonally dramatic: it implies that the hedge-witch's ward is not brute force but redirection. Keep with WSR-flavored rename. Candidate: "The Ward Against Ill" or "Reflection Charm."

- **Dispel (1).** Removes spell effects; can interrupt a spell being cast; can banish minor summoned creatures. In hedge-magic framing: banishing. The cunning-person who can send minor supernatural beings away, or undo another caster's work, has a clear genre precedent (the exorcist, the counter-witch). Note that this has power over Conjured Elementals and "minor demons" - in WSR terms, this would interact with Sorcery's summoned creatures. Worth keeping as a hedge-magic capability: the wisewoman's banishment charm works against lesser things but would fail against a true bound demon. Define this distinction clearly at chapter time. Conditionally keep.

- **Invisibility (1).** Touch, 3 SIZ/level, 15 minutes. Maintaining requires no fighting, injuring, using powers, or similar distractions. Attacks against invisible targets at 1/5 skill. This is a classical folk-magic ability (the cloak of concealment, the grey-man's shroud) and genre-appropriate for a cunning-rogue figure. However: the mechanic breaks invisibility on any action other than walking, so it functions as a stealth charm rather than a combat tool. That limitation is appropriate to hedge magic's register. Sorcery's Cloak of Night covers stealth augmentation but is not full invisibility. Conditionally keep if hedge magic is adopted; rename to something less D&D-adjacent. Candidate: "The Unseen Passing" or "Grey-Cloak."

- **Speak to Mind (1).** Mental communication, 1 target/level, 10 combat rounds. Language-independent; non-intelligent targets make Idea rolls to understand. In hedge-magic framing: spirit-speech. The shaman who communicates with animals, or the wisewoman who speaks across distance to a bound familiar (if the familiar system were kept, which it is not) - this has genre precedent. In the absence of familiars, this becomes a charm for cross-species communication or, at the GM's discretion, speaking with spirits. Conditionally keep if hedge magic is adopted. Rename candidate: "The Speaking Charm" or "Beast-Speech."

## Defer

- **Whether hedge magic requires a separate PC type / background.** BRP Magic is accessed by Occultist, Priest, Shaman, and Wizard professions. In WSR's classless, background-based system, the question of who can access hedge magic and how it is distinguished from Sorcery is a chapter design call. Two options: (a) any character can access hedge magic through a specific background or cultural origin; (b) hedge magic is a GM-only tool (NPC wisewomen, tribal shamans) not available to PCs. Option (b) would mean this section has zero player-facing content and might not justify its own chapter section. Defer.

- **Skill governing Magic spells.** In BRP, each spell is its own skill at INT x 1. For WSR hedge magic this is probably too granular - a cunning-person would have a single "Hedge Magic" or "Cunning Craft" skill rating rather than individual skill percentages per spell. But this is a chapter-design decision touching the broader skills redesign. Defer to the chapter design phase.

- **Starting access and spell count for hedge magic.** BRP specifies 4 spells (Normal), 6 (Heroic), 8 (Epic), 10 (Superhuman) at INT x 1. If hedge magic is kept as a narrow folk tradition, the WSR version should have fewer starting spells and possibly a lower cap. Defer to chapter design.

- **Interaction between hedge magic and Sorcery's Dispel / Undo Sorcery.** The source's Dispel spell (from Magic) can banish "lesser summoned creatures (elementals or minor demons)." The source's Undo Sorcery (from Sorcery) operates against sorcery spells. In WSR these systems would need clear jurisdiction rules: does Dispel (hedge) work against Sorcery effects? Does Undo Sorcery work against hedge charms? Needs a call at chapter time.

- **The Ward spell appears in both Magic (p. 69) and Sorcery.** The Magic Ward (permanent, 3 PP/level, Countermagic + Blast combination around a perimeter) and the Sorcery Ward (3 PP, magical alarm) appear to be different spells with similar names. In WSR terms, if both systems are kept, one would need a different name to avoid confusion. Defer.

## Open Questions

1. **Is hedge magic for PCs or GM-NPCs only?** If hedge magic belongs only to NPCs (wisewomen, shamans, exorcists), the section needs very little rules space - a brief note that these figures exist, plus the GM description of their abilities. If it is PC-accessible, it needs chargen hooks, a skill framework, and spell descriptions. This is the defining question.

2. **What is the social register of hedge magic in WSR's ancient world?** Is it common (village-level practitioners everywhere) or rare (a handful of surviving traditions)? This shapes how often PCs encounter hedge magicians and whether it is an accessible PC path.

3. **Does hedge magic interact with the PP pool in the same way?** The decisions log (2026-05-09) establishes that Fate Points and Sorcery share the PP pool. If hedge magic also uses PP, hedge magicians have the same Fate Point tension as sorcerers, which may or may not be desirable. If hedge magic has its own separate small pool, it breaks the established shared-pool principle. Needs a call.

4. **Rename / rebrand.** "Magic" and "magician" are the wrong words for WSR. The hedge-magic tradition needs a name that fits the ancient-world aesthetic: "cunning craft," "the old ways," "hedge-art," "wise-working," "charm-work." Naming is deferred to chapter drafting.

5. **Can Sorcerers use hedge-magic spells and vice versa?** In BRP these are separate skill families. In WSR, if kept, they should probably remain separate to preserve the tonal distinction. But this needs explicit ruling.

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

**Moderate, but duplicative.** If hedge magic is kept as a PC option, it creates a second magical archetype alongside the Sorcerer - the Cunning-Person who does not traffic with demons but can see hidden things, ward a threshold, and slip past guards unseen. This is a distinctive character type that does not exist in the current Sorcery section. However, the mechanical distinctiveness is relatively thin: hedge magic is a skill-based system where Sorcery's mechanics are more elaborated. The distinctiveness mostly comes from *flavor and fiction* rather than mechanics. If character distinctiveness is the primary goal, hedge magic's contribution is smaller than it first appears. Against: this may mean hedge magic does not pull its weight in the book and would be better handled by background/culture flavor text.

### Party cohesion

**Low contribution.** The Magic system has no party-binding mechanics equivalent to Sorcery's Chain of Being. The familiar and staff sub-systems (both dropped) were the only mechanics that implied collaborative play. The surviving hedge-magic spells (Dark, Light, Illusion, Perception, Vision) are almost entirely solo/support tools. A hedge-magic character in a party contributes divination and concealment but creates no mechanical interdependence. This is a gap, not a blocker - party cohesion is a design goal for the whole system, not the magic chapter alone.

### Mook rules

**No match.** The Magic section has no mook or minion mechanics. The Familiar system (dropped) was the closest analogue. No new findings here.

### Sorcery cost

**Useful contrast.** The Magic system's skill-roll mechanic (fumble loses all PP, failure loses 1 PP, success costs PP equal to spell level) creates a cost structure that differs from Sorcery's automatic success. If the sorcery cost design call (source-1-sorcery.md, Defer section, Options A-F) trends toward a hybrid model, the Magic system's failure risk provides a ready precedent: hedge magic fails visibly and costs PP on failure; Sorcery always works but may cost more in other ways. This distinction reinforces the "hedge magic is lesser" register without requiring a separate mechanic.

## Book Placement

**If hedge magic is kept:**

- **Player Book:** A brief section on the cunning-folk tradition, the 6-10 surviving spells with WSR-flavored descriptions, the skill framework for acquiring and improving spells, and the distinction from Sorcery. Probably 2-3 pages maximum.
- **GM Book:** Guidance on crafting NPC wisewomen, shamans, and hedge-practitioners. The interaction rules between hedge magic and Sorcery (Dispel jurisdiction, Ward naming conflicts). Notes on the social register of cunning-craft in the GM's setting.
- **Not needed:** The familiar sub-system, the staff sub-system, the Becoming a Magician apprenticeship rules (simplified to a single note about study time), the power-level starting-spell tables.

**If hedge magic is dropped (Option A):** No Magic chapter section at all. A brief note in the Sorcery section's GM Book content acknowledging that lesser practitioners exist in the world as flavor, without game mechanics.
