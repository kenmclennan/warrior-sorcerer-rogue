# Rolemaster Arms Law & Claw Law - Critical Table Research

**Source:** Arms Law & Claw Law, 2nd Edition (Iron Crown Enterprises, 1989). Stock #1100.
**Pages read:** 1-20 (TOC, intro, full combat resolution rules); 79-89 (all critical strike tables).
**Purpose:** Evaluate the Rolemaster critical hit table format as a model for WSR weapon-type critical tables.
**Note:** All table content below is paraphrased. Arms Law & Claw Law is copyright Iron Crown Enterprises. No verbatim reproduction.

---

## Summary

Arms Law & Claw Law (AL&CL) is Iron Crown Enterprises' standalone combat supplement, originally published in 1980 and revised in 1989. It is one of three cornerstone books of the Rolemaster system (alongside Spell Law and Character Law & Campaign Law) but is also explicitly designed for use with other FRP systems - a boxed note in every section explains how to integrate it without the full Rolemaster chassis.

The book provides: individual attack tables for 30 weapons (each weapon has its own table), 10 animal attack tables (Claw Law section), 3 martial arts attack tables, a fumble table, and 10 Critical Strike Tables covering damage types and creature categories.

The combat resolution chassis works like this: roll d100 (open-ended high), add Offensive Bonus, subtract Defensive Bonus, cross-index the net result against the target's Armor Type (1-20) on the weapon's Attack Table. The Attack Table cell reads something like "22EP" - meaning 22 concussion hits plus an E-severity Puncture critical. The player then rolls d100 on the Puncture Critical Strike Table, looks up the E column, and reads a result. That result is the actual wound narrative and mechanics.

---

## Critical Table Structure

### Damage Types (Critical Types)

AL&CL has **five human-scale critical types**, each with its own Critical Strike Table:

1. **Slash** - slashing weapons (swords, axes, daggers)
2. **Puncture** - piercing and thrusting weapons (spears, arrows, crossbow bolts)
3. **Krush** - blunt/crushing weapons (maces, hammers, clubs)
4. **Grapple** (Claw Law) - grappling and grab attacks
5. **Unbalance** (Claw Law) - knockdown/imbalance attacks

Plus two creature-category tables that replace the above for large targets:

6. **Large Creature Critical Strike Table** - for Trolls, Giants, Demons, Undead
7. **Super-Large Creature Critical Strike Table** - for Dragons, Dinosaurs, most powerful Demons

And four Claw Law tables for non-humanoid combat types:

8. **Martial Arts Striking** - unarmed strikes
9. **Martial Arts Sweeps & Throws** - takedowns
10. **Tiny Animal** - attacks from small creatures

### Severity Bands

Every human-scale Critical Strike Table has **five severity columns: A, B, C, D, E**.

- A = least severe (graze, minor wound)
- E = most severe (catastrophic, frequently fatal)

The band is determined by the letter in the attack table result (e.g., "22EP" = E-severity Puncture critical).

### Row Structure

Each Critical Strike Table has **approximately 20 result rows**, grouped by d100 roll range:

- 01-05
- 06-10
- 11-15
- 16-20
- 21-35 (wider band)
- 36-45
- 46-50
- 51-55
- 56-60
- 61-65
- 66 (single result, notably dramatic)
- 67-70
- 71-75
- 76-80
- 81-85
- 86-90
- 91-95
- 96-99
- 100 (single result, maximum effect)

That is 19 distinct result rows per column, times 5 columns = **95 distinct results per table**.

The Large Creature and Super-Large Creature tables use a different structure: columns are weapon type categories (Normal, Magic, Mithril, Holy Arms, Slaying) rather than severity bands, and the row ranges are broader (e.g., 01-05, 06-10, 11-20, 21-30, 31-40, 41-50, 51-65, 66, 67-70, 71-80, 81-90, 91-95, 96-98, 99-100, 101-150, 151-175, 176-200, 201-250, 251+). These tables handle open-ended high rolls far beyond 100.

## Result Format

Each cell in a Critical Strike Table is a **1-4 sentence narrative result** containing:

1. A **description of the wound** in vivid physical terms ("slash across the calf," "blade drives through the kidney," "blow shatters the cheekbone")
2. **Concussion hit bonus** (additional HP damage beyond what the attack table gave): expressed as "+N hits" or "+N hits/round" (bleeding)
3. **Combat penalty**: expressed as "foe is at -X" (modifier to all rolls), "foe must parry next round," "stunned N rounds," "unable to parry N rounds," "foe is at -X and is stunned"
4. **Condition flag**: "down and out," "dies in N rounds," "dies immediately," "knocked unconscious," "paralyzed"

Some results include **initiative effects** ("you receive initiative next round") and **carry-over bonuses** ("add +10 to your next swing").

Example format (paraphrased from Slash table, row 71-75, column C):

> A slash severs muscle and tendon in the lower leg. Foe is stunned for two rounds and cannot parry. Bonus hits apply. Foe fights at a significant penalty.

Example format (paraphrased from Slash table, row 100, column E):

> A devastating strike immediately destroys all vital organs. All effects are applied instantly. Foe expires.

The results are **prose**, not codes - each is a readable sentence or two. There is no abstract shorthand; every result is written out in full. The GM reads or paraphrases it aloud at the table.

## Sample Table: Slash Critical Strike Table (Paraphrased)

The Slash table (8.1.1) is the most referenced human-scale table. Full paraphrase of its texture across all five bands:

**Band A (least severe) - character and flavor:**
Low rolls (01-20): minor initiative shifts, tiny hit bonuses (1-2), mandatory parry next round. The A column is almost entirely about disruption rather than damage - the foe is inconvenienced, not hurt. Mid-range rolls (21-65): minor wounds to extremities (calf, forearm, thigh, shoulder), 1-3 HP ongoing bleed, combat penalties of -10 to -30. High rolls (66-99): A-severity criticals at the high end can still stun for 2-3 rounds, cause minor permanent effects (a scar), disarm. Row 100 at A: a calf wound that prevents parrying and causes 1 HP/round bleed. Even the worst A-result is survivable and not fight-ending.

**Band B - escalating consequences:**
Low rolls: 1-4 bonus hits, parry forced. Mid (36-65): minor to moderate wounds with stun (1-2 rounds), ongoing hits per round (1-3), penalties of -10 to -25. High (66-80): weapon-arm wounds that disable shield use, chest wounds with ongoing bleed, moderate stun with parry restriction. Near-100: head wounds causing unconsciousness or death in several rounds - not instant but urgent.

**Band C - fight-altering:**
Low rolls: 3-6 bonus hits, stun 1 round. Mid: wounds that combine stun + parry-restriction + ongoing bleed simultaneously - the foe is fighting at multiple penalties and bleeding. High (81-90): back and spine blows that knock out for multiple rounds, organ-area wounds with death-in-rounds. Row 100: destroy foe's eyes and stun for 30 rounds.

**Band D - severe, frequently fatal:**
Low: 2-4 bonus hits, mandatory parry. Mid: amputations (weapon arm made useless), major bleed (3-6 HP/round), stun for 4-6 rounds combined with inability to parry. High: severed arteries, death-in-rounds results become common (3-9 rounds), foe collapses and is dying. Row 100: a heart strike - dies instantly, weapon lodged.

**Band E (most severe) - overwhelmingly lethal:**
Low (01-15): 3-5 bonus hits - Band E's floor is already Band C's ceiling. Mid: foe's limb is severed or permanently destroyed, ongoing massive bleed, death-in-rounds. High (66+): nearly every result kills within 1-6 rounds or instantly. Row 66: spine destroyed, paralyzed permanently. Row 91-95: spine destroyed, paralyzed from neck down permanently. Row 96-99: head strike, brain destroyed, life is "made difficult." Row 100: groin strike destroys all vitals, dies instantly. Band E at any roll above 50 is effectively a death sentence unless the target has magical intervention.

## Lethality Calibration by Band

| Band | Low roll (01-20) | Mid roll (36-65) | High roll (81-99) | Roll of 100 |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|------------|
| A | Initiative shift, 1-2 hits | Minor extremity wound, -10 to act | Scar, 2-3 round stun | Nasty bleed, won't die |
| B | 2-4 hits, forced parry | Moderate wound, stun 1-2 rounds, -20 | Head wound, unconscious risk | Death in 6 rounds |
| C | 3-6 hits, stun 1 round | Multi-penalty wound (stun + parry + bleed) | Spine blow, out for rounds | Eyes destroyed, 30-round stun |
| D | 2-4 hits, parry forced | Arm useless or major bleed 3-6/rd | Severed artery, dying | Instant death (heart) |
| E | 3-5 hits (floor = B-C) | Limb severed or permanent disability | Death in 1-3 rounds, always | Instant death (vitals destroyed) |

**Key finding:** Bands A and B are fight-altering but rarely fight-ending. Band C is where the fight frequently ends for NPCs (not immediately but within rounds). Band D ends fights for everyone including tough PCs. Band E at rolls above 50 is almost always a death sentence. The system is deliberately and relentlessly lethal at the high end.

There is **no "safe" band E result**. Even a low roll (01-05) on Band E delivers bonus hits equivalent to a solid Band B or C result. The bands are not symmetric - the floor of E is higher than the ceiling of A.

## WSR Fit Assessment

### Does it suit WSR's "muscular, decisive, brutal" aesthetic? (design.md §2.3)

**Yes, strongly.** The Rolemaster critical table format is exactly the kind of content design.md §2.3 describes: wounds matter, encounters cost something, the combat aesthetic is visceral and consequential. The narrative descriptions are evocative and genre-appropriate - "drives bone through brain," "severs carotid arteries," "slashes tendons and crushes bone in the shield shoulder." These read as the kind of results that appear in Howard's prose.

The per-wound narrative also lands cleanly in WSR's "simulationist underneath, pulp on the surface" framing (design.md §2.3): the detail is there in the result text, but nothing requires a grid or positional tracking to resolve. The GM reads the result, everyone understands what happened.

### Does it preserve theatre-of-mind? (design.md §3.1)

**Yes, completely.** Every result in the critical tables is a self-contained prose description that needs no map, no miniature, no measurement. Results reference "foe's shield arm," "foe's lower back," "foe's calf" - all resolvable by imagination. The stun/parry/penalty mechanics do require tracking round-counts and modifier values, but this is bookkeeping, not positional geometry.

### Where it fits less cleanly

- **Volume of results per table.** The tables have 95 distinct results each (19 rows x 5 columns). This is the single largest adoption cost - writing this content is significant.
- **The combat chassis is tightly coupled.** The tables generate letter codes (A through E) from weapon Attack Tables. In WSR's BRP chassis, the equivalent trigger would have to be redesigned. We are not adopting the Rolemaster attack-roll-plus-OB-minus-DB mechanic; we need a different trigger.
- **Penalty vocabulary differs from BRP.** Rolemaster penalties are expressed as modifiers to OB/DB (e.g., "foe is at -30"). BRP uses percentile skill modifiers (Easy/Difficult/etc.) and round-count conditions. A WSR table would need its result vocabulary adapted to BRP's idiom.
- **The bleeding mechanic in crit tables.** Rolemaster critical results include ongoing hit-per-round bleeding embedded in the result text. BRP has its own bleeding mechanic (1 HP/round from slashing special success). These could conflict; one or the other would need to be the canonical bleed trigger. Recommend: crit table results describe bleed fiction but use BRP's existing bleed mechanic rather than adding a second bleed track.

## Adoption Sizing

If WSR adopts this format for its weapon-type critical tables, here is the content estimate:

**Proposed WSR damage types** (from notes/source-5-combat.md canonised list):
- Slashing (sword, axe, dagger)
- Impaling/Piercing (spear, arrow, javelin)
- Crushing (mace, hammer, club)
- Possibly: Burning (sorcerous fire, Greek fire)
- Possibly: Grappling (for creature and unarmed combat)

That is **3-5 damage types** depending on decisions on burning and grappling.

**Bands:** 5 (A through E, matching Rolemaster convention).

**Results per band column:** Rolemaster uses 19 rows. A WSR table could reduce this to **10 rows** (roughly: 01-10, 11-20, 21-30, 31-40, 41-50, 51-60, 61-70, 71-85, 86-99, 100) without losing the lethality gradient. This is workable and preserves the "floor of the band is still meaningful" principle.

**Entry count estimates:**

| Scenario | Tables | Bands | Rows | Total entries |
|----------|--------|-------|------|---------------|
| Rolemaster-scale (full) | 5 | 5 | 19 | 475 |
| WSR minimum (3 types, 10 rows) | 3 | 5 | 10 | 150 |
| WSR comfortable (5 types, 10 rows) | 5 | 5 | 10 | 250 |
| WSR comfortable (5 types, 15 rows) | 5 | 5 | 15 | 375 |

**Recommendation:** 3 core damage types (Slash, Pierce, Krush) with 10 rows per band = 150 entries as the minimum viable set. Add Burning and Grappling tables (100 more entries) as the full set. This is a significant but scoped writing task - more like writing a short bestiary chapter than an infinite table-generation project.

## Recommendation: Inspire Only (Not Full Format Adoption)

**Verdict: Inspire Only.**

**Reasoning:**

The Rolemaster critical table format is genre-perfect for WSR's aesthetic. The results are visceral, the lethality gradient is tight, and the narrative format works in theatre-of-mind play without any modification. This is the right *kind* of content.

However, full format adoption creates two problems:

**1. Trigger redesign.** Rolemaster generates critical severity (A through E) from the net attack roll cross-referenced against Armor Type on individual weapon attack tables. This is the full Rolemaster chassis - a different core mechanic from BRP. WSR cannot adopt the critical tables without either (a) adopting Rolemaster's attack resolution entirely (which defeats the purpose of building on BRP) or (b) designing a new BRP-compatible trigger for band assignment. Per design.md §3.1 ("stay faithful to BRP's percentile, skill-based identity"), option (a) is rejected. Option (b) means designing the trigger from scratch anyway.

The user's proposed trigger - doubles on the attack dice (matching dice critical) - is a clean and elegant BRP-native solution. It would generate a severity band through a separate roll or through the degree of match (e.g., doubles on a special success = B, on a critical = D/E). This is WSR-original design using Rolemaster's *format* as inspiration.

**2. Content volume.** 150-250 entries is a substantial writing commitment. This should be a conscious scoping decision rather than a format adoption that implies "and therefore we write all of it."

**What to adopt from Rolemaster:**
- The A-E severity band concept (5 bands, escalating from fight-disrupting to instantly lethal)
- The prose result format (1-4 sentences, narrative + mechanical penalty + condition)
- The lethality gradient principle: band A disrupts, band C is fight-ending for NPCs, band E is near-always fatal
- The damage-type specificity: different tables for Slash, Pierce, Krush (matching WSR's existing special damage type categories from BRP, which already distinguish bleeding/impaling/crushing)
- The row structure: 10-15 distinct rows per column is the right granularity
- The vocabulary: stun (N rounds), penalty to all actions (expressed in BRP as penalty to skill), ongoing damage per round, limb effects, instant death vs death-in-rounds

**What not to adopt:**
- Individual weapon attack tables (WSR keeps BRP's single attack roll vs. weapon skill)
- Armor Type cross-referencing
- The OB/DB system
- Open-ended rolls above 100 (BRP handles this via its own open-ended mechanic)

## Risks

1. **Content volume.** Writing 150-250 distinct critical results that are all vivid, genre-appropriate, and mechanically precise is the single largest content-generation task in WSR's combat chapter. It should be explicitly scoped and scheduled - do not begin table-writing until the trigger mechanic and band structure are locked.

2. **Lethality calibration.** Rolemaster's Band E is extremely lethal - most results above roll 50 kill within rounds. WSR's "wounds matter, but the protagonist wins" framing (design.md §2.3) and the Luck Roll / Fate Points mechanic need to clearly interact with critical results before the table entries are written. If a Band E result says "dies instantly," the Fate Point spend to negate it needs to be defined. Write the interaction rule before writing the table entries.

3. **Penalty vocabulary translation.** Rolemaster penalties are expressed as OB/DB modifiers. BRP uses skill percentages and categorical difficulty modifiers. Every result needs to be expressed in BRP's idiom, not Rolemaster's. The translation is not difficult but requires consistency: define the WSR vocabulary (e.g., "foe fights at -30%" is expressed as "foe's attacks and parries are Difficult for N rounds") before writing any entries.

4. **Bleed interaction.** BRP already has a bleeding mechanic triggered by slashing special success (1 HP/round). Rolemaster's Slash critical results also embed bleeding in the result text. The two must not stack. Decision needed: either (a) critical result bleed overrides the special-success bleed and uses BRP's halt-with-First-Aid rule, or (b) special-success bleed and critical-result bleed are tracked separately. Option (a) is simpler.

5. **Copyright.** The critical table format (A-E bands, d100 rows, narrative results) is a game mechanic, not copyrightable expression. WSR is safe to adopt the format and write original result entries. The result text in this document is paraphrased, not reproduced. ICE retains copyright on the original result text; WSR must write its own entirely. This is already implicit in the "inspire only" recommendation.
