# Source Notes: Sorcery (BRP UGE pp. 87-102)

**Section:** Chapter 4 / Powers / Sorcery
**Pages:** 87-102
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Sorcery agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

BRP UGE Sorcery is a grimoire-based system in which spells always succeed (no skill roll to activate) and are powered by Power Points (PP), which regenerate up to the caster's POW characteristic. The spell list is divided into eight categories covering augmentation, characteristic enhancement, combat, enhancement, elemental summoning, manipulation, occult utility, and creature summoning. Demon summoning is a full sub-system: demons have true names, personalities, and negotiation mechanics, and binding them costs permanent POW. The section also covers how sorcerers learn spells (scholarly research, grimoire study, being taught, or supernatural gift), how to ready and dismiss spells relative to the INT memory limit, and special-case rules for casting across planes, handling resistance rolls, and spell interactions. This section is the primary magic candidate for WSR and is almost entirely genre-appropriate in structure, though the specific cost mechanism requires a design call before the chapter can be finalised.

## Canonise

- **Automatic success (no skill roll to cast).** Sorcery simply works if cast. Keeps magic alien and theatrical per §2.2 - spells are not attempts, they are acts. Failure modes come from resistance rolls or external interference, not caster incompetence.

- **Power Points as the casting resource.** PP (up to POW max) regenerate naturally; casting depletes them. The pool structure is clean and ties magic directly to the sorcerer's vital force, fitting the costly-choice framing of §1.2 feature 6.

- **POW 16+ minimum to use sorcery.** Creates a real threshold that makes sorcerers genuinely exceptional per §2.5. Low-POW characters remain adjacent to sorcery (enchanted items still work on them) without being practitioners themselves.

- **Grimoire-based learning.** Spells must be written down in a physical grimoire; learning is slow (weeks of study) and requires literacy in the right language. This makes arcane knowledge a precious, dangerous commodity consistent with §2.2 ("each entry is a brief invocation of dread"). The grimoire-as-object also becomes a target for enemies and a source of adventure hooks.

- **INT memory limit for in-memory spells.** Sorcerers can hold INT levels of spells "on the shelf" for immediate casting; others are stored in the grimoire only. Creates a real resource-management layer that is internal to the character, not an external counter, per §3.1 (depth via BRP texture, not added complexity).

- **Readying time = spell level in combat rounds.** Pulling a spell from the grimoire into memory during combat costs rounds equal to its level. Supports the genre's "sorcery is risky to deploy under fire" feel per §2.2.

- **Casting takes one combat round; takes effect on Powers phase; sorcerer can do nothing else that round.** Clean, clear, and creates tactical pressure without a separate casting-phase subsystem.

- **Distraction check (Idea roll) when taking damage or surprise while casting.** If failed, spell fails and PP are lost. Meaningful consequence without excessive complexity; fits the visceral combat of §2.3.

- **Duration = caster's POW in combat rounds.** Elegant - ties duration to the most important sorcerer characteristic, keeps bookkeeping minimal.

- **Sorcerer's idiosyncratic casting signatures (Characteristic Effects spot rule).** Each sorcerer chooses personal sounds, gestures, foci, smells, etc. for their spellcasting. Makes sorcerers visually and narratively distinctive per §2.2 ("Visual drama. Magic is theatrical").

- **Demon summoning as a core sub-system.** Summoning costs PP, negotiation takes hours, binding costs permanent POW, and demons have personalities and true names. This is the heart of Moorcock-lineage sorcery per §1.1 - binding a demon to service is exactly how Elric's pacts work. Canonise the full sub-system.

- **True-name mechanics for bound demons.** A demon's true name is surrendered on binding; knowing it lets the sorcerer recall and dismiss the demon. Names as power is a genre fundamental per §1.1 (Clark Ashton Smith, Moorcock).

- **Four ways to break an ordinary demon binding** (sorcerer dismisses, kill the demon, kill the binder, learn true name and rebind). Clean, game-ready, and opens many plot hooks.

- **Demon binding types: flesh, object, place.** Object-binding (weapon, amulet) is especially aligned with the genre - cursed blades, demon-bound rings, etc. per §2.4 (deep past breathing through objects).

- **Summoning circle/preparation requirement** (magical circle, special items, Knowledge (Occult) research). Sets up summoning as a ritual event rather than a casual action, appropriate to §2.2.

- **Summon Elemental requiring knowledge of the linked elemental spell** (Fires of the Sun before fire elementals, etc.). Makes the spell list interconnected and creates natural specialisation paths for different sorcerer archetypes.

- **Invoking a God sub-section.** Gods cannot be summoned directly (too powerful); sorcerers may beg for attention through ritual, with a 1% base chance (Difficult chance if a champion). Fits §1.1's Moorcock accent - patrons are vast, alien, and their attention is not guaranteed.

- **Other Planes rules.** Spells learned on one plane may cost double PP and have halved effect; spells fail more often. Suitable for encounters with demons or planar entities per §1.2 feature 4 (deep cyclic history, things from beyond the world).

- **Resistance table procedure for contested spells.** Clean resolution: compare current PP vs PP after deducting casting cost. Already standard BRP chassis.

- **Enchanted weapons rule.** A weapon with an active sorcery spell retains the effect when transferred to another character. Opens genre-appropriate play: cursed enchanted blades passed between hands.

- **Spells per Object limit (one spell per object at a time).** Prevents stacking cheese, maintains clean fiction.

- **Sorcery Spell List - spell categories to canonise (with individual call-outs below in Modify/Drop as needed):**
  - Summoning Spells (Summon Demon, Summon Elemental) - per above
  - Occult Spells (Brazier of Power, Chain of Being, Undo Sorcery, Ward) - per genre utility below
  - Combat Spells (Hammer of the Gods, Hell's Razor, Sorcery's Sharp Flame, Sorcerous Armor, Talons of the Beast, Unbreakable Bulwark) - weapon/armor enhancement by sorcery is entirely genre-appropriate; keep per §2.2
  - Manipulative Spells (Curse of Sorcery, Fury, Inescapable Bonds, Liken Shape, Muddle, Pox) - curses and compulsions are genre-core; keep per §1.1 and §2.2
  - Augmentation Spells (Cloak of Night, Sureness) - stealthy rogue-adjacent sorcery; keep per §1.2 feature 6 and §2.5

- **Brazier of Power (4).** Creates a permanent reservoir of PP tied to a physical focus, costing 1 permanent POW and 4 PP per expansion. A sorcerer's lair is defined by the objects of their power. Exactly genre-appropriate per §2.4 (deep past, old sorcerers' sanctuaries).

- **Chain of Being (4).** Allows up to eight individuals to pool PP for a single spell. Directly supports party cohesion - a party contributing PP to a sorcerer's great working is a classic genre scene (per §3.1 party cohesion watch-item).

- **Ward (3).** Creates a magical alarm on a defined perimeter. Fits ancient-world sorcery of protective ritual circles per §2.4.

- **Witch Sight (3).** Detects POW strength and sorcerous auras; essential tool for demon summoning (reveals unbindable demons). Fits §2.2 - knowing what you're dealing with before you try to bind it is a survival mechanic that creates drama.

- **Fury (1).** Induces berserker rage. Directly Howard-lineage per §1.1 - the berserker who cannot stop fighting even when dying is a genre archetype.

- **Curse of Sorcery (4).** Disfigures target with a grotesque physical feature. Excellent genre-appropriate curse; its allegiance point implication (points to forces of cruelty or chaos) is a natural corruption hook.

- **Liken Shape (4).** Shapeshifting into another creature or person. Genre-standard sorcerer ability (Elric's sorcerous disguises, Smith's shape-thieves).

- **Undo Sorcery (1-4).** Counter-magic. Keeps the sorcery space contested; sorcerer duels are a genre staple.

- **Midnight (1) / Moonrise (1).** Darkness and light creation. Essential toolkit for the theatrical sorcerer; low cost, high narrative impact per §2.2.

- **Bird's Vision (1) / Vermin's Vision (1) / Keen Ear (1).** Remote perception through creatures. Appropriately unsettling - sorcerers who spy through birds and rats fit the genre's idea that sorcery reaches places it shouldn't per §2.2.

- **Farsight (1).** Doubles visual range; stacks with re-casting. Simple, useful, genre-appropriate.

- **Cloak of Night (1-4) / Sureness (1-4) / Leap (1-4).** Skill augmentation spells. Appropriate - a sorcerer blessing a rogue's heist is a classic Leiber scenario per §1.1.

- **Inescapable Bonds (3).** Immobilizes living targets. Genre staple; the sorcerer who binds enemies for later use.

- **Muddle (1).** Disorients target, prevents spellcasting and planning. Useful, thematically fits the "sorcery confounds the mind" genre note.

- **Pox (1).** Drains target's PP via resistance roll. PP-drain as an attack is genre-appropriate in a PP-driven system.

## Drop

- **Heal (2) spell.** Restores lost hit points. Explicitly prohibited by §2.2 ("No healing magic") and §1.3 ("No Cure Light Wounds"). Drop entirely.

- **Make Whole (3) spell.** Repairs broken items (size up to SIZ 50). While not a healing spell per se, this is a utility/craft spell with no genre resonance - it belongs to a different tonal register (workshop magic rather than dark power). Per §1.3 ("Magic as utility. Sorcery is not a helpful toolkit"). Drop.

- **Make Fast (1) spell.** Glues two inanimate objects together. Pure utility with no dark character per §2.2 ("No flavorless spells"). Drop.

- **Breath of Life (1) spell.** Provides breathable air underwater. Survival utility with no genre weight per §2.2. Drop.

- **Mutations, Psychic Abilities, Superpowers** (pp. 70-102, neighboring sub-sections). Not part of the Sorcery section itself, but recorded here for completeness: these three power sets are genre-alien and are entirely dropped per §1.3 and §source-structure.md note 2.

- **Computer file, recording, or audio file examples for grimoires.** The source text allows grimoires to be computer files, recordings, etc. Strip all modern/tech framing; in WSR, a grimoire is a physical manuscript, scroll, carved stone, or similarly ancient medium. Per §1.2 feature 3 (ancient world setting) and §2.4.

- **The four campaign power levels for starting spells** (Normal / Heroic / Epic / Superhuman) as a formal mechanical option. WSR has a single power level (exceptional but mortal per §1.2 feature 5). The starting-spells framework is useful as GM guidance but the tier labels should not appear in the Player Book. Drop the tier structure; the GM Book can specify starting quantities directly.

- **Sorcerous Heritage** as a character creation method (trading 2 points of other characteristics for 1 POW). The trade-at-creation option is potentially useful, but the specific rule is tied to BRP's point-buy chargen assumptions. Flag as a Modify candidate below rather than drop outright - see Modify.

- **Elemental Spells (Bounty of the Sea, Fires of the Sun, Gift of the Earth, Wings of the Sky).** These fill an area with a massive amount of their element (a roomful of water, fire, dirt, or wind). The effects are more cataclysmic than genre-appropriate for PC-level sorcery and feel like set-dressing spells for pre-planned encounters. They also exist primarily as prerequisites for the Summon Elemental sub-types. Consider whether WSR needs elemental summoning at all; if yes, these prerequisites can be kept but should be trimmed. Tentatively Drop as PC-castable spells; their prerequisite function can be folded into the Summon Elemental description. See Open Questions.

- **Inhuman Plasticity (1-3) spell.** Grows or shrinks the target's SIZ. Physical transformation is genre-adjacent but this specific spell - making someone larger or smaller like a size-change device - reads more like fantasy utility than dark sorcery. Per §2.2 ("No flavorless spells"). Drop.

- **Lightning Speed (1-3) spell.** Increases MOV. Movement speed augmentation has no genre resonance; fast travel is not a sword & sorcery concern. Drop.

- **Wisdom of the Sage (1-3) spell.** Boosts target's INT temporarily. Intelligence augmentation is flavorless and feels too D&D-adjacent per §1.3 ("Become D&D in disguise"). Drop.

## Modify

- **Sorcerous Heritage** (POW-for-characteristic trade at character creation). The mechanic of deliberately burning other capabilities for power is genuinely genre-appropriate (the sorcerer who sacrificed their health or beauty for arcane strength). Keep the concept; rewrite as WSR-specific background trait rather than a point-buy option. The "Luck roll required, 00 always succeeds" detail is messy and should be replaced with a cleaner chargen decision. Cite §2.5 (sorcerers are exceptional individuals who have paid a cost to get there) and §3.1 (character distinctiveness without classes).

- **Exercising Power** (POW increase via defeating equal-or-greater POW opponents in resistance rolls). Keep the concept but reframe the flavor. The current text sounds too much like a training mechanic. In WSR, defeating a demonic entity or overwhelming another sorcerer in a power contest and thereby absorbing a measure of their essence is genre-appropriate. Rename and reframe per §1.1 Moorcock accent.

- **Gift of the Gods** (GM gifts POW to a sorcerer in exchange for service or dangerous deed). Keep as-is mechanically but rename to "Patron's Gift" or similar - "Gods" implies a theological framework; in WSR patrons may be demons, ancient powers, star-beings, etc. Per §2.2 and §1.2 feature 6 (sorcery as costly - debts to powers).

- **Characteristic Enhancement spells** (Bolster the Soul, Relentless Vitality, Suppleness of the Serpent, Titan's Strength, Unearthly Beauty). These are worth keeping but the "roll D100 for max level, on 00 the effect becomes permanent but costs 9 points of other characteristics" mechanic is baroque and will confuse at-table play. The permanent-transformation-on-a-fumble concept is excellent genre fiction (the sorcerer who pushed too far and was changed forever) but needs to be extracted into a clean spot rule or optional consequence table rather than buried in each spell entry. Modify to reference a single "Permanent Sorcery Consequence" spot rule. Per §2.2 (no flavorless spells - the narrative of permanent change belongs in the rules, but clearly presented).

- **Starting spells (1/2 INT in levels).** The Normal campaign starting allocation (up to 1/2 INT in spell levels) is the right quantity for WSR's exceptional-but-mortal baseline per §1.2 feature 5. Keep the number, drop the tier framing. WSR sorcerers start with INT/2 (round up) spell levels known; all inscribed in their grimoire.

- **Grimoire language rules** (must be able to read the grimoire's language; skill below INT x5 requires a Language roll). Keep this rule; it reinforces that arcane knowledge is hard-won and not automatically transferable. Per §2.2 and §1.2 feature 3. The specific threshold (INT x5) is fine.

- **Spell names.** The source gives "generic" names and explicitly invites renaming. WSR must rename nearly all spells for genre flavor before the Player Book is finalized. "Hell's Razor" and "Hammer of the Gods" are usable; "Cloak of Night" is usable; "Make Fast" and "Inhuman Plasticity" are dropped anyway. The acceptable spell names to keep: Hell's Razor, Hammer of the Gods, Cloak of Night, Midnight, Moonrise, Fury, Witch Sight, Inescapable Bonds. Everything else should be renamed. This is a design/prose task, not a rules task. Per §2.2 ("each entry is a brief invocation of dread").

- **Unearthly Beauty (1-3).** Keep as a sorcery spell (seductive glamour / terrible unnatural attractiveness is genre-standard) but the name is acceptable. Confirm keep once spell list is finalized.

- **Refutation (1-4) spell.** Counter-magic that specifically protects against Undo Sorcery. Mechanically sound. However, the name is flat. Flag for rename (something like "Veil of the Sorcerer" or "Iron Ward"). Keep mechanically per §2.2.

- **Sorcery Spell List - open for expansion.** The source explicitly states "the following list is not exhaustive" and invites new spells. WSR should design at least a handful of genre-specific spells not present in BRP UGE (e.g., necromantic effects, curse-of-the-land, plague-sending, binding oaths) to reinforce the tone. Per §2.2 and §3.1 (center the genre in every chapter).

## Defer

These rules all touch the sorcery cost question (how much does magic truly cost?) and need a design call before the chapter is finalised. Each option is described so the user can compare them.

### Option A: Power Points Only (current BRP UGE default)

Spells cost Power Points equal to their level. PP regenerate naturally up to the caster's POW. A sorcerer who exhausts their PP is simply tired and cannot cast until they recover. Losing all PP does not harm the sorcerer beyond inability to act magically.

- **Upside:** Clean, no bookkeeping beyond PP tracking. Fast at-table resolution.
- **Downside:** Low "cost-as-curse" feel. A rested sorcerer has effectively zero cost. Does not produce the Moorcock sense that sorcery is eating something important away. Per §2.2, this option alone may produce "magic feels like a toolkit."

### Option B: Permanent POW Expenditure on Powerful Spells

Some spells (especially demon binding) already require spending permanent points of POW. The question is whether to expand this to ordinary spell use (e.g., every casting has a small chance of permanent POW loss, or casting above a certain threshold always costs permanent POW).

- **Upside:** Sorcerers who cast big spells become measurably diminished. Aligns with §1.2 feature 6 (every step costs something). The Brazier of Power already uses this cost.
- **Downside:** If permanent POW loss is too frequent, sorcerers become unplayable over a campaign. Needs calibration.
- **Mechanic present in source:** Demon binding costs 1-3 permanent POW. Brazier of Power costs 1 permanent POW per focus expansion. The max-level 00-on-D100 permanent enhancement mechanic already exists. So the infrastructure for permanent cost is there; it just needs to be applied more broadly if this option is chosen.

### Option C: Fatigue / Exhaustion Track

After casting, the sorcerer gains fatigue points (or moves down an exhaustion track). Enough fatigue incapacitates or kills. This is not natively in the BRP UGE Sorcery section (fatigue is a Spot Rule in Chapter 7) but is compatible with the PP system.

- **Upside:** Produces the physical cost of sorcery (the hollow-eyed, gaunt sorcerer) without permanently reducing a characteristic. Recoverable.
- **Downside:** Adds a second tracking mechanism alongside PP. Needs a clear fatigue-recovery rate.
- **Mechanic present in source:** BRP UGE Chapter 7 has fatigue rules; they would need to be applied to spellcasting specifically. Not in this section.

### Option D: Sanity / Corruption Track

Casting sorcery degrades the caster's connection to ordinary humanity. Each casting (or each significant casting) advances a corruption counter. At thresholds, the sorcerer gains personality changes, compulsions, or physical marks. This is the most genre-resonant option (Elric's soul-debt, the corrupted sorcerer who can no longer walk among ordinary people).

- **Upside:** Directly produces §2.2's "alien, costly, and corrupting" magic. Creates long-arc campaign pressure. Supports the Moorcock accent §2.1.
- **Downside:** Requires designing a corruption table or scale. The source's Sanity system (Chapter 10, pp. 258-261) could be repurposed, but it was designed for cosmic horror (CoC lineage), not sorcerous cost. Significant adaptation required.
- **Mechanic present in source:** Chapter 10 Sanity rules. Allegiance rules (also Ch. 10) already accumulate points toward chaotic/cruelty forces from some spells (Curse of Sorcery, certain permanent-transformation results). Could be the scaffold.

### Option E: Debt-to-Power / Patron Obligation

Casting sorcery at scale creates obligations to the powers that enable it (demons, old gods, chaos forces). Rather than tracking a number, the GM accumulates "debt" that is called in narratively - a patron demands service, a demon insists on something, an old power exerts pressure. This is entirely narrative and has no number to track.

- **Upside:** Zero bookkeeping. Directly produces the "strings attached" feel of §1.2 feature 6. Every great spell opens a story hook.
- **Downside:** Entirely GM-dependent; no player-facing pressure. Inconsistent between tables. Hard to make feel inevitable rather than arbitrary.
- **Mechanic present in source:** "Accepting Sorcery as a Gift" (p. 89) already states gifts "come with strings attached, known or unknown." The Invoking a God section implies obligation. Not formalised.

### Option F: Hybrid (PP + one other track)

POW Points handle ordinary casting. A second track (corruption, permanent POW, or patron debt) applies only to major workings: summoning demons, binding elementals, casting spells above a threshold, or deliberately pushing past PP exhaustion.

- **Upside:** Ordinary sorcery feels accessible; extraordinary sorcery feels existentially costly. Matches the genre's distinction between hedge magic and true sorcery.
- **Downside:** Requires defining what "major working" means and policing the boundary.

**All of the above: needs design call.** Per design.md §2.2 and §3.3.

---

### Deferred: Starting Spell Quantities by Campaign Tier

BRP UGE gives four tiers (Normal 1/2 INT, Heroic INT, Epic INT x2, Superhuman INT x4). WSR defaults to one tier but the exact starting quantity (1/2 INT or full INT) affects how competent starting sorcerers feel. Needs a call at chapter design time.

### Deferred: Whether Elemental Summoning Is In Scope

The Summon Elemental spell and its four prerequisite elemental spells (Bounty of the Sea, Fires of the Sun, Gift of the Earth, Wings of the Sky) form a sub-system parallel to demon summoning. Elementals are described as more pleasant and less dangerous than demons. Whether WSR includes elemental summoning alongside demon summoning, or restricts PC sorcerers to demon-binding only, is a design call touching the breadth of the magic system and the tone (elementals feel more D&D-adjacent; demons feel more genre-correct). Needs design call.

## Open Questions

1. **Spell list scope.** The full BRP UGE sorcery spell list is 32 entries after removing the drops above. This may still be too large for a focused system. Should the Player Book contain the full curated list, or a smaller "core" list with the rest in the GM Book as optional or rare spells?

2. **Elemental summoning: in or out?** See Defer above. If in, the four prerequisite elemental spells should be Modify candidates (reframe as ritual knowledge). If out, Summon Elemental is also dropped.

3. **Sorcery as a PC path: how available?** BRP UGE implies any character who achieves POW 16+ can use sorcery. Should WSR restrict sorcery to characters who have specifically pursued it (background/culture/profession choice), or is the raw POW threshold sufficient? This affects character distinctiveness significantly per §3.1.

4. **Spell naming pass.** Most spell names need replacing before publication. This is creative/prose work, not rules work, but it needs to be scoped into the chapter production plan.

5. **Characteristic enhancement spells and the permanent-consequence mechanic.** The "roll D100 at max level; on 00 the effect becomes permanent but costs 9 characteristic points" rule is on every enhancement spell. If kept, it should become a single shared spot rule. If dropped, enhancement spells lose their most interesting long-arc consequence. Design call on whether to keep, restructure, or replace.

6. **Grimoire physicality rules for WSR's ancient-world setting.** The source allows a wide range of grimoire forms. WSR should define its own list of acceptable forms (scroll, codex, clay tablets, carved bone, obsidian slab, etc.) and strip the anachronistic options.

7. **Does Chain of Being belong in the Player Book?** It enables group PP pooling for a single spell - a party mechanic. If the party cohesion design goal is leaned into, this spell is a strong candidate for explicit spotlight in the Player Book with a worked example. See Watch-items: party cohesion.

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

**Significant match.** Several rules in this section create distinctive sorcerer characters without classes:

- The **INT memory limit** means sorcerers make meaningful choices about which spells to keep ready - two sorcerers with the same spell list will feel different based on what they have readied.
- **Characteristic Effects** (idiosyncratic casting signatures - each sorcerer's sounds, gestures, smells, foci) is a direct distinctiveness mechanic. Canonised.
- **Bound demons** function almost like familiar-NPCs with their own personalities, histories, and true names - a sorcerer defined by their demon is a distinctive character.
- The **Sorcerous Heritage** chargen option (sacrificing characteristics for POW) produces sorcerers who are measurably physically diminished - visible distinctiveness.
- **Permanent consequence on max-cast** (the D100 roll per enhancement spell) means some sorcerers will be permanently transformed by their own magic, differentiating campaign veterans from newcomers.

These collectively give sorcerers strong distinctiveness. The gap is between sorcerers (what makes one Sorcerer feel different from another)? The characteristic-sacrifice and bound-demon mechanics help, but a WSR-specific background/culture system (Chapter 2) will need to carry the load for non-magical character types.

### Party cohesion

**Moderate match.** Two specific mechanics create party-level binding:

- **Chain of Being (4):** Up to eight characters pool their PP for a single great working. A sorcerer cannot always summon a powerful demon alone; they need the party's PP. This creates a mechanical dependency that is also narratively satisfying - the whole party contributes to the act of binding.
- **Brazier of Power:** A sorcerer's base of operations (the lair with its power focus) becomes a party asset if the party operates from it - the sorcerer may want protection while the focus is vulnerable.
- **Accepting Sorcery as a Gift:** A patron entity granting sorcerous ability to a non-sorcerer character creates a bond of shared complicity.

The gap: sorcery otherwise tends to isolate rather than bind. The sorcerer deals with forces the party cannot perceive or interact with. WSR may need to design additional party-cohesion mechanics (perhaps: assisting a sorcerer's summoning circle as a distinct action with mechanical benefit). This should be a design note for the magic chapter.

### Mook rules

**No match in this section.** The Sorcery section has no mook/minion mechanics. However, demon summoning and elemental summoning do produce a sorcerer-controlled creature that functions similarly to a minion. The rules for bound demons include a kind of "minion cap" (total demon POW dice count against INT limit, same as spells). The NPC Digest (Chapter 11) has a Sorcerer stat block that may be relevant when the mook question is resolved. The core mook-rules question (how do enemy sorcerers' demons behave as cannon fodder vs. named opponents?) remains unresolved and is a design call for the GM Book.

### Sorcery cost

**Primary match - full coverage.** This section contains all the BRP UGE sorcery cost mechanisms:

- Power Points (default, regenerates)
- Permanent POW (for demon binding, Brazier of Power)
- The D100 permanent-characteristic-drain on max-level enhancement spells
- "Strings attached" narrative debt from patron gifts (not formalised)
- Physical interruption/distraction during casting (Idea roll or lose PP and spell)

All are described in the Defer section above. The cost mechanic is the single most important design decision for the sorcery chapter and will determine the overall "weight" of magic in WSR. Flagged for user decision before chapter drafting begins.

## Book Placement

**Split: Player Book and GM Book.**

- **Player Book** contains: the full sorcery rules introduction (who can use sorcery, POW threshold, grimoires, INT memory limit, readying/dismissing), all rules governing casting (timing, duration, distraction, resistance table procedure), the curated spell list with WSR-flavored descriptions, the three POW-increase methods (Exercising Power, Sorcerous Heritage, Patron's Gift), and the spot rules that a player needs at the table (Characteristic Effects idiosyncrasy, Enchanted Weapons, Spells per Object).

- **GM Book** contains: the Demon Summoning sub-system in full (preparation, negotiation, binding types, breaking bindings, permanent binding rules, the four ways bindings break, how bound demons behave when mistreated), the Invoking a God rules, NPC sorcerer creation guidance, advice on patron design and the nature of deals, the Other Planes casting rules (GM-adjudicated and not routine), and any expanded corruption/cost mechanic that requires GM tracking.

**Reasoning:** PC-facing sorcery goes in the Player Book per §3.3 (Player Book / GM Book division). Demon summoning is borderline - PCs can summon demons, so the spell itself belongs in the Player Book; but the full negotiation and binding system is primarily a GM-run social encounter, and the demon's personality, true name management, and mistreatment consequences are GM-side information. The Player Book describes what the PC can do and what it costs; the GM Book describes what happens on the other side of the ritual circle.
