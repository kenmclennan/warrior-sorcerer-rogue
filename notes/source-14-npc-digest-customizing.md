## Summary

The NPC Digest (pp. 288-297) presents 16 archetypal non-player characters spanning prehistoric, ancient, medieval, modern, and science-fiction settings. Of the 16, roughly 6 are genre-appropriate for WSR with little or no modification, 3 are adaptable with renaming or re-statting, and 7 are incompatible with the ancient-world sword-and-sorcery frame and are dropped. Customizing Creatures (pp. 298-299) provides four campaign-tier templates (Normal, Heroic, Epic, Superhuman) describing how to scale creature and NPC stats upward; the templates address number of opponents, characteristics, skills, equipment, and powers, but contain no rule that accelerates foe resolution or reduces per-foe bookkeeping - the mook gap identified by the Combat agent is confirmed by this section. Creature Hit Location Tables (pp. 299-303) present D20 hit location charts for twelve body-plan types plus a lookup table for HP per location; these are dropped per `decisions.md` (Major Wounds as default, per-location HP excluded).

## NPC Digest (pp. 288-297)

The digest is framed explicitly as a toolkit: entries "can be used instantly or easily customized as needed, reskinned or even used as quick player characters." Skill spreads range from 25% to 95%; the source intends these as competent-but-not-exceptional characters suitable for most encounters. All stats are for a Normal campaign baseline.

### Skill Spread Calibration Data (for WSR Profession Design)

The following are the most relevant skill anchors for WSR profession design, extracted from the canonised and modified archetypes:

**Warrior-type NPCs (Knight, Viking, Tribal Warrior):**
- Primary weapon skill: 55-75%
- Secondary weapon skill: 50-65%
- Dodge: 40-50%
- Brawl/Grapple: 35-55%
- Command (military): 45%
- HP range: 13-15

**Rogue-type NPCs (Pirate, Ninja/Shadow-Killer, Thug):**
- Primary weapon skill: 55-75%
- Stealth: 25-75% (Thug low, Shadow-Killer high)
- Climb: 55-80%
- Fine Manipulation: 45-75%
- Navigate/Swim: 35-45% (Pirate)
- Sleight of Hand: 35-55%
- HP range: 12-15

**Sorcerer NPC:**
- Knowledge (Occult): 70%, Knowledge (Demonic): 50%, Knowledge (Blasphemous Lore): 20%
- Research: 75%
- Physical combat: Dagger 35% (deliberately low)
- POW: 21 (highest of any NPC; drives PP pool and spell count)
- HP: 13 (fragile)
- Spells in memory: 7 (Curse of Sorcery 4, Sorcerous Armor 4, Sorcerer's Sharp Flame 3, Summon Demon 1, Undo Sorcery 2, Heal 2, Witch Sight 3)

**Key calibration finding:** The gap between a Sorcerer's Dagger skill (35%) and a Viking's Broad Sword skill (65%) is the built-in mechanical expression of the warrior/sorcerer split. The Sorcerer's POW 21 vs. Viking's POW 11 is the corresponding sorcerous power gap. These ratios should anchor WSR profession starting skill recommendations.

## Customizing Creatures (pp. 298-299)

The section header is "Customizing Creatures and Nonplayer Characters." It opens with a framing statement: "It may be necessary to customize these creatures and nonplayer characters based on the power level of your game, or to better match the competency of the player characters."

### Tier Templates

**Normal Campaigns (default):**
- The digest NPCs are geared for a normal campaign baseline
- Adjusting any characteristic triggers recalculation of all secondary characteristics (damage bonus, HP, major wound, power points)
- Note on armor: "Adding 3 points of armor to an entity can make them challenging, and with more than +6 points they become very difficult to harm"
- No specific stat modifications; this is the baseline tier

**Heroic Campaigns:**
- Increase number encountered by +25% or more
- Add one extremely competent leader at Epic power level
- Have foes use superior tactics, be more cautious or aggressive, exploit terrain
- Increase key characteristics by another D6 or flat +3 (keeping rollable maximums intact)
- Modify combat-oriented and relevant skills by +25% or multiplied by x1.5
- Add more powers or increase efficacy of existing powers
- Equip with better equipment, armor, weaponry

**Epic Campaigns:**
- Double the number encountered vs. normal characters
- Give them a leader at Superhuman power level
- Increase characteristics by +6 or more, beyond racial maximums
- Double skill rankings
- Use Command and Strategy skills to coordinate encounters
- Add all-new powers or multiple power groups; combine entries with Extra Hit Points superpower
- Give foes the best equipment, armor, and weaponry; include magical artifacts, instant-healing medical supplies

**Superhuman Campaigns:**
- Mix normal, heroic, and epic level NPCs to vary challenge
- Increase characteristics far past rollable maximums using powers
- Triple or quadruple skill ratings far above 100%
- Add powers like Extra Hit Points, Super Characteristics, Super Skill
- Equip with exceptional arms, armor, gear including magical or powered gear; foes use it tactically

**General Notes (source text):**
The section closes: "Don't be afraid to scale back an encounter by having enemies flee, or increase the difficulty by having new enemies arrive, or by introducing some environmental factor... Gauging an enemy's relative strength against a player character's is a difficult art to master, and the flat distribution of dice results and unexpected tactics can make a potentially easy encounter a life-or-death struggle or end a climactic battle with a single first (and lucky) shot."

### Mook Rules Assessment

**VERDICT: Confirmed - no native mook handling in BRP UGE. Must design or borrow.**

The Customizing Creatures section is the last possible source for native mook mechanics in BRP UGE; the Combat agent confirmed none appeared in Chapter 6. A complete reading of pp. 298-299 confirms that this section also provides nothing usable as a mook system.

Here is what was found and what was not found:

**What the section provides:**
- Scaling templates for making foes harder (more HP, higher skills, better armor, more powers)
- Guidance on adjusting encounter composition (add leaders, increase numbers)
- GM guidance on encounter calibration (flee, reinforcements, environmental factors)
- One qualitative suggestion relevant to group encounters: "increase the number of the creatures or nonplayer characters encountered by +25% or more" (Heroic tier)

**What the section does not provide:**
- No rule that lets a GM make a foe go down faster than full BRP combat
- No single-HP or single-threshold "mook" stat block
- No simplified HP track for expendable combatants
- No group-as-single-entity rule (treat 5 thugs as one stat block)
- No one-shot rule (any threshold below which a hit just kills the foe)
- No morale system that causes groups of ordinary foes to break before they're individually dead
- No "minion" or "rabble" category with abbreviated resolution

**One partial lead - from the Combat chapter, not here:**
The Combat agent flagged that the "Initiative Rolls (DEX+D10) Option" (which WSR is dropping) mentions "roll 1D10 to represent a group of nonplayer characters." This is a GM convenience note about initiative grouping, not a resolution mechanic. It represents the closest thing in all of BRP UGE to a group-enemy shortcut, and it addresses initiative ordering only - not wound tracking, attack resolution, or any other part of the combat loop.

**Implication for WSR design:**
BRP UGE provides zero native tools for making a group of thugs resolve faster than a group of individually-tracked combatants. The gap is unambiguous. WSR must design or borrow a mook system. The most promising design seeds from the source:

1. **The Thug stat block as the mook template:** HP 15, Dodge 35%, primary weapon at 50% - a competent fighter, but not dramatically. A rule that "any PC attack that beats a Thug's skill in the same round the attack lands automatically succeeds" (i.e., Thugs cannot parry) would make groups faster to resolve.

2. **The initiative grouping note (from the dropped Initiative Rolls option):** Treat a group of mooks as a single initiative entry acting at a shared DEX rank. One roll per "mob" rather than six separate DEX counts.

3. **From design.md §2.3:** "Soldiers, thugs, and city guards... are sketches of combatants, designed to be dispatched." The design intent is already defined; it just lacks a mechanic.

4. **Candidate borrow sources:** RuneQuest 6/Mythras has a "rabble" rule; the Stormbringer/Elric! BRP lineage may have mook conventions. Either is a legitimate borrow target given the genre lineage.

**Watch-item #3 status: Escalated. Gap fully confirmed. Mook design or borrow is required before the Combat chapter can be drafted.**

## Watch-Items

### Character Distinctiveness

**Strong match from this section.** The NPC Digest demonstrates that BRP UGE's classless system produces legible archetypes from skill spread alone. The Sorcerer is recognisable not from a class label but from POW 21 + Dagger 35% + Knowledge (Occult) 70%. The Viking is recognisable from Broad Sword 65% + Navigate 35% + Bargain 35%. This directly validates design.md §3.1's approach to distinctiveness without classes: skill priority, derived stats, and equipment choices create readable archetypes. The canonised WSR archetypes will form the calibration layer for profession starting skill recommendations.

### Party Cohesion

**Indirect contribution.** The NPC Digest archetypes suggest natural party compositions: a Viking (warrior) + Pirate (rogue/sailor) + Sorcerer is a legible WSR adventuring group - the Fafhrd / Mouser / occasional sorcerer pattern. No mechanic supports this explicitly, but the archetype spread across warrior, rogue, and sorcerer types maps to the game's title. No party-binding mechanic surfaces here; that gap remains open from the Combat notes.

### Mook Rules (Watch-Item #3 - Highest Priority)

**Gap confirmed and escalated.** Full assessment above under Customizing Creatures / Mook Rules Assessment. No native mook handling anywhere in BRP UGE (Combat chapter confirmed; Customizing Creatures section confirmed). WSR must design or borrow. This is the highest-priority open design question for the Combat chapter draft.

### Sorcery Cost

**Calibration data found.** The Sorcerer NPC has POW 21 and seven spells in memory at levels summing to 19 total "levels" (Curse 4 + Armor 4 + Flame 3 + Demon 1 + Undo 2 + Heal 2 + Witch Sight 3). This implies a POW 21 sorcerer can hold approximately 19 levels of sorcery in memory - a calibration ratio of roughly 1 POW per 1 spell level. Whether this is the actual mechanic (from the Sorcery chapter) or incidental will need verification against notes/source-1-sorcery.md.
