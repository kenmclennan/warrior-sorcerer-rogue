# Source Notes: Chapter 2 - Characters (pp. 2-29)

> Phase 1 Task 2 extraction notes. Every decision cites design.md.

---

## Summary

Chapter 2 is a complete character creation system covering characteristics (STR, CON, SIZ, INT, POW, DEX, CHA), derived stats (HP, PP, damage modifier, experience bonus, MOV), skill point allocation, and a profession list of ~40 entries. Character distinctiveness is carried primarily by the profession system plus an optional Personality Type step, optional Distinctive Features, and an optional Culture and Characters framework. The chapter makes no mention of passions, reputation, or death/replacement mechanics - those live in Chapters 10 and 6 respectively. The power level framework (Normal/Heroic/Epic/Superhuman) gives WSR a concrete calibration point: Heroic is the right home for sword & sorcery protagonists. Experience and improvement are handled by reference to Chapter 5 (System) rather than detailed here.

## Canonise

- **Seven core characteristics** (STR, CON, SIZ, INT, POW, DEX, CHA): the standard BRP set. (design.md §3.1 - "Stay faithful to BRP's percentile, skill-based identity.")
- **3D6 roll for STR/CON/POW/DEX/CHA; 2D6+6 for INT and SIZ**: produces the 3-18 range, with INT and SIZ guaranteed a human-range floor. (design.md §3.1 - faithful to BRP.)
- **Up to 3 points redistribution at chargen, cap 21**: small amount of player agency without making chargen a build puzzle. (design.md §3.1 - "Make characters feel distinctive without classes.")
- **Heroic power level as WSR default**: 325 professional skill points, skill cap 90%. PCs start exceptionally competent. (design.md §1.2 point 5 - "exceptional but mortal heroes"; §2.5 - "upper edge of human ability in their archetype"; §1.3 - "zero-to-hero arcs" explicitly rejected.)
- **Derived stats: HP = (CON+SIZ)/2, PP = POW, Damage Modifier (STR+SIZ table), Experience Bonus = INT/2, MOV = 10**: the standard BRP chassis. (design.md §3.1 - faithful to BRP.)
- **Major Wound level = HP/2**: a concrete threshold for serious combat consequences. (design.md §2.3 - "wounds matter, but the protagonist wins"; §1.2 point 7 - "naturalistic healing.")
- **Power Points (PP)** renamed as "Magic Points" in the text where appropriate: POW as the spiritual/magical resource. Central to sorcery chapter. (design.md §2.2 - sorcery is costly; §1.2 point 6.)
- **Profession system** (see Modify section for how we reshape it): skill-based starting package that defines what a character can do without defining what they are. (design.md §3.1 - "Make characters feel distinctive without classes.")
- **Profession skill list of ~10 skills per profession**: the core mechanism for skill differentiation between characters. (design.md §3.1 - distinctiveness without classes.)
- **Personal skill point pool (INT x10)**: free-choice points that let a player push a character beyond their profession. (design.md §3.1 - "character specialisation" as the route to depth.)
- **Wealth levels** (Destitute/Poor/Average/Affluent/Wealthy) per profession: grounds starting equipment and social standing. (design.md §2.4 - decadent civilization; §1.2 point 3 - ancient world setting.)
- **Characteristic Rolls** (STR×5 Effort, CON×5 Stamina, INT×5 Idea, POW×5 Luck, DEX×5 Agility, CHA×5 Charm): the catch-all resolution tools. Canonise all. (design.md §3.1 - faithful to BRP.)
- **Luck Roll (POW×5)**: directly supports pulp survivability - coincidences, narrow escapes, fortunate timing. (design.md §2.3 - "Luck / Hero points"; §1.2 point 5 - luck mechanics let PCs handle ordinary foes and survive near-death situations. Note: this is a Luck *roll*, not a Hero Point spend - see Defer for the hero point question.)
- **Distinctive Features (Option)**: cosmetic only, no mechanical value, scaled by CHA deviation from average. Ten categories covering Hair, Facial Hair, Facial Feature, Expression, Clothes, Bearing, Speech, Arms, Torso, Legs. (design.md §2.5 - "sweat and scars"; §3.1 - "Make characters feel distinctive.")
- **Personality Types (Option, Step 6)**: four broad archetypes (Brutal, Skilled, Cunning, Charming), each giving 20 points to ~13 specific skills. A lightweight first pass at character flavor before profession. (design.md §3.1 - distinctiveness mechanisms.)
- **Culture and Characters (Option)**: framework for defining cultural backgrounds including Leader, Culture, Appearance, Demeanor, Language(s), Occupations, Religions, Arms and Armor, Cultural Skills, and Items. Provides a top-level layer above profession. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world setting; §2.4 - real-world ancient flavor; §3.1 - "center the genre in every chapter.")
- **Creating New Professions** guidelines (title, description, wealth level, 10 skills): the toolkit that lets the GM design WSR-specific profession packages. (design.md §3.1 - "Be a focused, opinionated extraction"; WSR professions must be redesigned from the generic list.)
- **Step Nine: Size and Distinctive Features** - height/weight from SIZ, plus Distinctive Features note. Good genre flavor for physical presence. (design.md §2.5 - "sweat and scars.")

## Drop

- **Education (EDU) characteristic** (Option): Modern/academic flavor; redundant for ancient-world protagonists who learn by surviving, not schooling. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §1.3 - no pseudo-medieval or modern trappings.)
- **Knowledge Roll (EDU×5)**: dropped with EDU. (Same reasoning.)
- **Fatigue Points (STR+CON)** as a detailed tracked subsystem: too much bookkeeping overhead at the table. WSR uses Simple Fatigue (see Modify). (design.md §3.1 - "Keep rules text uncluttered"; §4.2 - players think in fictional terms, not resource tracking.)
- **Sanity Points (POW×5)** as a standalone stat: the CoC/horror-specific SAN mechanics are not the right carrier for sorcery's cost in WSR. Deferred for modification/replacement in the magic chapter. (design.md §2.2 - "alien, costly, and corrupting" - a corruption track is more appropriate than clinical sanity loss; §3.3 - sorcery cost mechanic deferred to magic chapter.)
- **Higher Starting Characteristics Option (2D6+6 for all stats)**: unnecessary if Heroic power level is established via skill points rather than pumped characteristics. (design.md §1.2 point 5 - "exceptional but mortal heroes" - not superhuman.)
- **Point-Based Character Creation Option** (all stats start at 10, buy from pool): dice rolling suits the sword & sorcery convention of random fate; a point-buy system removes that flavor and invites optimization. (design.md §1.2 point 5 - characters are not Marvel heroes built to spec.)
- **Superhuman and Epic power levels**: outside the genre. WSR is Heroic. (design.md §1.2 point 5 - exceptional but mortal; §1.3 - "zero-to-hero arcs" rejected.)
- **Normal power level**: too underpowered for sword & sorcery protagonists who start exceptional. (design.md §2.5 - "upper edge of human ability in their archetype.")
- **Choosing Characteristic Values Option** (roll 3D6 seven times, pick): increases player control at the cost of genre-appropriate randomness. Drop. (design.md - no explicit citation, but fits the "faithful to BRP" and anti-optimization stances.)
- **Cultural Characteristic Modifiers** as positive/negative racial adjustments for humans: the source flags this as a "potentially sensitive topic" and discourages penalizing INT. WSR drops characteristic modifiers for human cultures. Cultural differentiation comes through Cultural Skills and the Culture and Characters framework instead. (design.md §1.3 - "Pulp-era prejudice. The genre's regrettable racial and gender attitudes do not survive the port.")
- **Non-human Characters Option**: no demi-human PC races in WSR. (design.md §1.3 - "No elves, dwarves, halflings.")
- **Skill Category Bonuses (full version)** with primary/secondary/negative characteristic math: adds bookkeeping complexity without meaningful distinctiveness payoff. Drop in favor of Simpler Skill Bonuses (see Modify).
- **~20 professions that are genre-alien**: Computer Tech, Journalist, Mechanic, Pilot, Politician, Scientist, Student, Teacher, Technician, Writer, Engineer, Doctor, Detective, Lawyer (as a named profession), and several others are modern-era or genre-inappropriate. They are dropped and replaced by the WSR profession list. (design.md §3.1 - "Center the genre in every chapter"; §1.2 point 3 - ancient world setting.)
- **Finishing Touches background questions** (as written): the questions given ("Are they happy in life?", "Do they have many friends?") are too domestic for sword & sorcery. WSR will replace with genre-appropriate prompts. (design.md §1.2 point 2 - anti-heroes with personal codes; §2.5 - outsider status, personal codes.)

## Modify

- **Characteristic generation**: canonise the standard 3D6 / 2D6+6 roll. Add the "Choosing Characteristic Values" option but restrict it: players may roll 3D6 twice for each characteristic and choose which result to keep (one reroll per stat). This preserves randomness and genre fate while reducing "useless character" frustration. (design.md §3.1 - playable characters from session one.)
- **Power level**: canonise Heroic as the WSR default and only option. Rewrite the Step Seven text to say "250 professional skill points, skill cap 90%" without mentioning Normal, Epic, or Superhuman. Age bonuses follow Heroic scale (+20 per 10 years). (design.md §2.5 - exceptional but mortal.)
- **Hit Points**: use the standard (CON+SIZ)/2 formula. Note Total Hit Points option (CON+SIZ without dividing) in a GM's toolbox sidebar as the mechanism for making named NPC antagonists or boss-tier threats feel harder to kill. PCs always use standard formula. (design.md §2.3 - "wounds matter, but the protagonist wins"; §3.3 - mook vs. tactical tension.)
- **Simple Fatigue**: canonise the Simple Fatigue variant (CON×3 rounds before Difficult, CON×4 before quarter-skill, CON×10 before near-incapacitation) rather than the full Fatigue Point tracking system. This gives fatigue real weight without per-round bookkeeping. (design.md §3.1 - "Keep rules text uncluttered"; §2.3 - "combat is dangerous" but not drowning in tracking.)
- **Personality Types (Step 6)**: keep the four types but rename and reframe for the genre. "Brutal" becomes something like "Barbarian/Warrior" framing; "Skilled" becomes "Artisan/Rogue" framing; "Cunning" becomes "Schemer/Survivor" framing; "Charming" becomes "Manipulator/Courtier" framing. The mechanical structure (20 points to 13 skills) remains identical. Some of the skills listed (Pilot, Technical) are replaced with genre-appropriate equivalents. (design.md §3.1 - "Center the genre in every chapter.")
- **Professions**: the generic list is heavily pruned and renamed. WSR-appropriate professions to keep (possibly renamed): Assassin, Athlete (Gladiator or Arena Fighter), Beggar, Crafter (Artisan), Criminal (Brigand), Explorer (Wanderer), Farmer (Peasant), Gambler, Herder (Nomad/Pastoralist), Hunter, Merchant (Trader), Noble, Occultist (Scholar of the Dark Arts), Priest (Cultist/Temple Servant), Sailor (Mariner), Scholar, Servant, Shaman, Slave (with the source's own sensitivity note retained), Soldier (Mercenary), Spy (Infiltrator), Thief, Tribesperson (Barbarian), Warrior. Wizard merges into Occultist as the starting profession for sorcery-capable PCs. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §3.1 - "Center the genre in every chapter.")
- **Simpler Skill Category Bonuses**: adopt the "Simpler Skill Bonuses" table (half the primary characteristic, rounded up) in place of the full primary/secondary/negative calculation. Reduces chargen friction while preserving characteristic influence on skills. (design.md §3.1 - "Keep rules text uncluttered.")
- **Distinctive Features**: expand the cosmetic categories slightly with ancient-world-appropriate entries (tattoos, brands, ritual scarification, missing fingers from oaths kept poorly, arcane marks). The mechanical structure (CHA-scaled number of features, cosmetic only) is unchanged. (design.md §2.5 - "sweat and scars"; §1.2 point 2 - "anti-heroes with personal codes.")
- **Culture and Characters framework**: adopt and expand as a mandatory (not optional) component of the WSR GM Book. Provide 4-6 worked example cultural packages as starting points (Civilized City-Dweller, Steppe Nomad, Desert Raider, Coastal Mariner, Temple Initiate, Barbarian Outland). Each package has Cultural Skills that give small bonuses. This is the top layer of character distinctiveness above profession. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §2.4 - real-world ancient flavor; §3.1 - "center the genre in every chapter.")
- **Step Eight: Possessions**: the "personal item showing some relation to family or background" bullet is excellent genre flavor. Canonise and strengthen it: at chargen, every WSR character must define one significant possession with a story attached. This is a lightweight party-cohesion and character-distinctiveness hook. (design.md §3.1 - "Make characters feel distinctive" and "Provide hooks for party cohesion.")
- **Background questions (Finishing Touches)**: replace generic questions with WSR-specific prompts: Who wronged you and have you settled it? What oath have you sworn that binds you? What are you running from? Who do you owe a debt to? What one possession are you never without, and why? (design.md §1.2 point 2 - anti-heroes with personal codes; §2.5 - outsider status, personal codes, debts.)

## Defer

- **Luck Roll (POW×5) vs. Hero Points**: the source's Luck Roll is a standard skill roll, not a finite spendable resource. design.md §2.3 explicitly anticipates "a finite resource that lets PCs reroll, deflect a killing blow, or pull off the cinematic move." Whether WSR uses the Luck Roll as written, augments it with a Hero Point spend, or replaces it entirely with a Hero Point pool is an open §3.3 question. Defer to the combat/system chapter where the luck/hero-point design is resolved. Canonise Luck Roll for now as baseline; flag for possible augmentation.
- **Death mechanics and replacement loop**: Chapter 2 mentions that a character dies when HP reaches 0 at the end of the following round, and that a Fatal Wound (more damage than current HP) kills at end of next round without intervention. No character replacement rules exist anywhere in Chapter 2. This is a design.md §3.3 open question ("Death and replacement. Pulp mortality is established, but the replacement loop needs concrete mechanics."). Defer to phase 2 design; flag for the GM Book.
- **Experience and improvement**: Chapter 2 references experience checks and the experience bonus (INT/2) but routes all detail to Chapter 5 (System). Do not design improvement rules until Chapter 5 has been read. Flag here that the genre expectation (design.md §4.5) is growth in "experience and reputation, not raw power" - this should constrain how generous the improvement rules are.
- **Sorcery access at chargen**: the Wizard and Occultist professions both flag "Special: you may use magic or sorcery" with a reference to Chapter 4. The chargen integration of sorcery (starting spells, starting PP expenditure, initial grimoire contents) cannot be designed until Chapter 4 Sorcery has been reviewed. Defer entirely to that chapter's notes.
- **Total Hit Points option** (CON+SIZ without dividing): flagged as a Modify item above (GM toolbox for antagonists) but the full mook-vs-boss HP architecture is a §3.3 "mook handling" deferred question. The Combat chapter (Ch. 6) may provide more tools.
- **Sanity/Corruption track**: dropped as SAN but the sorcery cost mechanic question (design.md §3.3) means some corruption/cost derived stat will likely exist. Whether it is POW-based, SAN-derived, or a new stat is deferred to the magic chapter.

## Open Questions

1. **WSR-specific profession list**: the current analysis identifies ~24 genre-appropriate professions from the source list. The final WSR list needs to be drafted, with each profession's skill list rebuilt for ancient-world skills (no firearms, no computers, no vehicles). Should this happen in Phase 1 notes or Phase 2 extraction? Recommend Phase 2, once the Skills chapter has been audited.

2. **Personality Types as mandatory vs. optional**: the source treats them as optional. Making them mandatory would strengthen character distinctiveness, but four broad types may be too constraining. Alternative: keep optional but recommend them in the text. Open.

3. **Culture packages as Player-facing or GM-facing**: the Culture and Characters framework is GM-designed in the source. If WSR provides pre-built cultural packages in the Player Book, players can use them as a layer of background above profession. If only the GM has them, they are pure setting flavor. Decision needed before Player Book structure is finalized.

4. **Starting age**: the source uses 17+1D6 as default. For a sword & sorcery game where PCs start exceptional, age 17-23 feels young. Should WSR use a higher floor (e.g. 20+1D6) or just drop formal age mechanics entirely? Open.

5. **Distinctive Features: cosmetic-only or light mechanics?**: the source is explicit that distinctive features are cosmetic. But design.md §2.5 mentions "sweat and scars" as a mark of adventure. Should distinctive features carry any mechanical weight (e.g. a scar from a notable wound providing a +1D10% to Intimidate)? Or stay cosmetic? Open.

## Watch-Items

### Character Distinctiveness (Watch-Item #1)

This is the primary distinctiveness section and the mechanisms are:

1. **Personality Types (Step 6)**: four broad archetype-flavored skill bundles (Brutal / Skilled / Cunning / Charming). Lightweight, optional, but provides a pre-profession identity frame. Approximately as distinctive as a D&D background.

2. **Profession + skill list (~10 skills per profession)**: the primary distinctiveness layer. 40 professions in the source, each with a named skill package. Characters with the same profession can still differ substantially through the "choose from" slots within the profession list.

3. **Personal skill pool (INT×10)**: a second layer of specialization beyond the profession. A Warrior who spends personal points on Navigate and Knowledge (History) is immediately different from one who spends on Bargain and Persuade.

4. **Distinctive Features (CHA-scaled cosmetics)**: no mechanics, but vivid physical markers that serve "sweat and scars" flavor. Thin without mechanical support.

5. **Culture and Characters framework**: the source's top-level layer, providing cultural context (occupations, cultural skills, common items, demeanor, languages). WSR should promote this from optional to recommended/standard - it adds a third differentiation axis above personality type and profession.

6. **Significant possession (Step Eight)**: small but potent. A mandatory "one item with a story" rule costs nothing mechanically and does real work for distinctiveness.

7. **Background questions**: the source provides generic questions. WSR's replacements (see Modify) are the strongest distinctiveness tool at no mechanical cost.

**Gap assessment**: the system is classless and the skill differentiation within professions is meaningful, but the layers are shallow compared to, for example, a Passions/Reputation system. Passions (Chapter 10) will do the heaviest work on this watch-item. At chargen, the WSR player has profession + personal skill pool + culture package + one signature item + personal code answers. That is enough for distinctiveness, especially combined with Passions. Recommend: do not design new distinctiveness mechanics until Passions have been reviewed.

### Party Cohesion (Watch-Item #2)

- No party cohesion mechanics at all in Chapter 2. Characters are created as individuals.
- The "personal item with a story" modification (Step Eight) can be a cohesion seed if the item has a connection to another PC.
- The background questions can include "who in this party do you owe a debt to?" but this is a house rule at chargen, not a mechanic.
- No cooperative abilities, shared histories, or group-binding rules exist in this chapter.
- **Gap flagged**: party cohesion must be designed or borrowed. Watch subsequent chapters (especially Passions, Ch. 10) for any relationship mechanics.

### Mook Rules (Watch-Item #3)

- No mook rules in Chapter 2. The Total Hit Points option (CON+SIZ, no divide) could serve as a "named NPC" flag, but this is not presented as a mook system.
- The source's NPC Digest (Chapter 11) will have more to say here.
- **Gap confirmed**: no native mook system visible in this chapter.

### Sorcery Cost (Watch-Item #4)

- Power Points (max = POW) are established here as the core resource. PP recover at rest.
- POW reaching 0 = catatonic without will or life force (per the POW description).
- The Sanity option (POW×5) is dropped, but the PP framework is directly relevant to sorcery cost design.
- No corruption or cost-beyond-PP mechanics appear in this chapter.
- **Reference point**: PP = POW is the baseline. Any additional cost mechanic (corruption, physical cost, patron debt) must be layered on top in the magic chapter.
