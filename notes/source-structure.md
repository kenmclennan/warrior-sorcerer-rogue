## Notable Structural Features

### 1. No appendix, glossary, or index

The document ends at page 303 with the last creature hit location table. There is no back-matter glossary, master index, or designer's notes section.

### 2. Five distinct power systems in one chapter

Chapter 4 is 73 pages covering five completely separate systems: Magic, Mutations, Psychic Abilities, Sorcery, and Superpowers. Each is a self-contained sub-system. WSR will almost certainly use only one (Sorcery) and possibly elements of a second (Magic, reframed). Mutations, Psychic Abilities, and Superpowers are genre-alien and will be dropped.

### 3. Sorcery (pp. 87-102) is the primary magic candidate for WSR

Key properties directly aligned with the WSR tone:
- Automatic success - no skill roll, sorcery works if you cast it
- POW-driven cost - spells cost Power Points; losing all POW means death or transformation
- Grimoire-based - spells must be learned from a physical book; knowledge is dangerous
- Demon summoning as a core sub-system - demons have personalities, make deals, resist control
- Corruption potential built in

### 4. Magic system (pp. 56-70) is the alternative

Skill-roll based, more accessible, less alien. Could serve as "hedge magic" or hedge-witch abilities distinct from the sorcerer's art. The genre supports this split (Solomon Kane's puritan faith-powers vs. Elric's demonic pacts).

### 5. Equipment chapter is mostly dropped material

Chapter 8 (183-227) is heavily oriented toward modern firearms, vehicles, surveillance gear, and advanced-era technology. For WSR's ancient-world setting, only the melee weapons, shields, and personal armor tables are directly useful. Roughly 70% of the chapter is out of scope.

### 6. Skills chapter needs heavy pruning

Chapter 3 includes skills with no ancient-world referent: Computer Use, Demolition, Drive (various), Pilot, Repair (Electronic), Technical Skill, etc. The Status skill has three era tables; only the middle table (Ancient/Dark Age/Medieval/Imperial) is directly relevant to WSR.

### 7. Chapter 9 contains a critical Optional Rule Checklist

Pages 229-230 (approximate) enumerate all optional rules with brief descriptions of when each is appropriate. This checklist is the single most efficient tool for the WSR canonise/drop/modify decision process.

### 8. Chapter 9 is GM-only by design

The chapter explicitly flags itself as primarily useful to the gamemaster. This directly validates the planned Player Book / GM Book split: Chapter 9 (Gamemastering) belongs entirely in the GM Book.

### 9. Chapter 10 optional rules are strong genre candidates

- **Passions** (pp. 253-255): Love, Hate, Fear, Honor, Devotion, Loyalty - these are the emotional drivers of sword & sorcery protagonists. Strong canonise candidate with possible simplification.
- **Reputation** (pp. 256-258): Fame/notoriety score with social leverage - fits the genre's concern with name and legend.
- **Allegiance** (pp. 250-252): Divine patronage mechanic - could serve as a lighter-weight divine intervention system without requiring a full theological framework.
- **Sanity** (pp. 258-261): Cthulhu-lineage cosmic horror mechanic. Possible candidate for a modified "corruption" track rather than literal sanity.

### 10. Creature chapter has usable WSR material

Fantasy Creatures section includes: Demons (Lesser and Greater, directly tied to the Sorcery sub-system), Ghost, Ghoul, Mummy, Orc, Skeleton, Vampire, Zombie - all viable ancient-world antagonists. The NPC Digest includes a Sorcerer stat block with spells in memory, and a Viking and Pirate. Science Fiction Creatures are entirely dropped.
