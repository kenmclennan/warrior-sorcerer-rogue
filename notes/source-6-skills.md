# Source Notes: Chapter 3 - Skills (pp. 30-53)

> Phase 1 Task 2 extraction notes. Every decision cites design.md.

---

## Summary

Chapter 3 is the full BRP UGE skill list: 47 named skills (many with specialties making the functional count much larger), plus the mechanical scaffolding that governs how skills work - categories, base chances, complementary skill bonuses, augments, and the skill rating table. The chapter is genre-functional in its core resolution machinery but requires heavy pruning of its skill list for ancient-world fit. Roughly one-third of the source skills are dropped outright (modern/sci-fi technology), one-quarter are modified (renamed, pruned of inappropriate specialties, or restricted to the relevant era table), and the rest canonise cleanly. The Complementary Skill and Augment mechanics are a significant find for both party cohesion and character distinctiveness - they reward skill breadth and collaboration without adding resolution complexity. After pruning, WSR retains approximately 33 skills (not counting weapon specialties as separate skills), which is a tight and usable list for an ancient-world game.

---

## Mechanical Scaffolding (canonise as-is)

These mechanics are the chassis, not individual skills. All canonise.

- **Six skill categories** (Combat, Communication, Manipulation, Mental, Perception, Physical): clean taxonomy; category bonuses are optional but the categories themselves serve chargen and table reference. (design.md §3.1 - faithful to BRP.)
- **Percentile base chances with era variants**: the source already acknowledges that base chances shift by era. WSR uses this to quietly enforce the ancient-world setting by selecting the appropriate base chance column where era variants exist. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world setting.)
- **Skill Rating Table** (Novice through Master): the competency ladder. Canonise exactly. Central to the "exceptional but mortal heroes" calibration. (design.md §1.2 point 5.)
- **Skill Specialties mechanic**: the single most important mechanism for character distinctiveness. A character with Melee Weapon (Spear) and one with Melee Weapon (Sword) are functionally different fighters. The half-rating cross-specialty rule (use a related specialty at half rating) is clean. (design.md §3.1 - "Make characters feel distinctive without classes"; Watch-item #1.)
- **Complementary Skills**: add 1/5 of a supporting skill to the primary skill roll. Explicitly noted in the source as applicable to party collaboration - "you and other players will doubtless devise more." This is the party cohesion mechanism hiding in plain sight. (design.md §3.1 - "Provide hooks for party cohesion"; Watch-item #2.)
- **Augment mechanic**: roll a supporting skill first; success shifts difficulty one step in your favour, failure shifts it against you. Higher risk, higher reward than a Complementary bonus. Good for dramatic moments. Canonise. (design.md §4.2 - "players think in fictional terms"; the augment is a fictional-reasoning tool.)
- **Skills above 100%**: multiple attacks, better odds against Difficult tasks. Canonise for high-level warriors and master sorcerers. (design.md §1.2 point 5 - PCs are at the upper edge of human ability.)
- **Secret/Concealed Skill Rolls**: the GM rolls on the player's behalf for perception and social skills. Essential for the genre's deception, spy, and intrigue scenarios. Canonise. (design.md §4.1 - complications include betrayal; §2.1 - Leiber's wit with schemes within schemes.)
- **Acting Without Skill** (Wild Chance / Skill Category Bonus fallback): clean "try anyway" rule. Canonise. (design.md §3.1 - lean resolution, no dead ends.)
- **Changing the Skill List** note: the source explicitly invites renaming, adding, and removing skills. WSR should cite this as its license for the heavy modifications below. (design.md §3.1 - "Be a focused, opinionated extraction.")

## Canonise

Skills that survive without modification into WSR. Grouped by role.

### Combat

- **Brawl** (combat): unarmed combat - kicks, punches, headbutts. Universal. Also functions as a parry that inflicts minimum weapon damage. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Dodge** (combat/physical): DEX x2 base. The core defensive alternative to parrying. Canonise. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Grapple** (combat): wrestling, submission holds, choking, throwing. Grapple Effects subsystem is detailed and tactically interesting - notably the Strangle, Throw, and Disarm options. Canonise. (ancient-world fit: pankration, wrestling, and tavern brawls are genre-standard.)
- **Martial Arts** (combat): fighting style mastery that stacks on top of Brawl, Melee, Missile, or Grapple. Specialties by tradition. WSR specialties: rename to ancient-world traditions (Pankration, Kalaripayattu, Swordsmanship School, etc.) rather than modern martial arts names. Specialty mechanic makes this a strong character distinctiveness tool. (design.md §3.1 - distinctiveness; Watch-item #1.)
- **Melee Weapon (various)** (combat): per weapon class. Specialties are weapon classes - WSR will define its own weapon classes in the equipment chapter (bronze sword, short sword, axe, mace, spear, polearm, etc.). Canonise the skill; weapon class list deferred to equipment chapter. (ancient-world fit: core.)
- **Missile Weapon (various)** (combat): bows, crossbows, slings, javelins, throwing axes, spears, blowguns, boomerangs. All valid for the ancient world. Canonise. Note: grenades redirect to Throw. (ancient-world fit: core.)
- **Parry (various)** (combat): per weapon or shield class. Canonise alongside Melee Weapon. (ancient-world fit: core.)
- **Shield** (combat): separate from Parry; blocking with a shield, including aimed attacks. Canonise. (ancient-world fit: shields are central to ancient warfare.)

### Exploration / Physical

- **Climb** (physical): 40% base. Walls, trees, cliffs. Ladders automatic, sheer faces Difficult. Extended climbs require periodic rolls. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Hide** (physical): 10% base. Concealing oneself or objects. Opposed by Spot. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Jump** (physical): 25% base. Leaping for height or distance; also reduces falling damage. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Navigate** (physical/perception): 10% base. Stars, landmarks, memory, sea charts. Featureless terrain (open desert, open sea) is Difficult without instruments. (ancient-world fit: star navigation and landmark travel are period-appropriate; global positioning tech mentions are noted in System Notes but won't be reproduced in WSR.)
- **Ride (various)** (physical): 05% base. Per animal type. Mounted combat rules (50%+ threshold for fighting without a check) are solid. Specialties: Horse, Camel, War Elephant, etc. (ancient-world fit: core to the genre's cavalry, nomad, and raider archetypes.)
- **Stealth** (physical): 10% base. Moving without being heard or seen. Opposed by Spot or Listen. Slow movement is normal; moving at half MOV or faster is Difficult. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Swim** (physical): 25% base. Dangerous waters only; floating is Automatic. Underwater melee rules (use lower of combat skill or Swim; only impaling weapons useful). (ancient-world fit: obvious; rivers, harbors, sea crossings are all genre-relevant.)
- **Throw** (physical): 25% base. Improvised thrown objects and non-throwing weapons. Also covers lassos. Half damage modifier. (ancient-world fit: rocks, furniture, nets, lassos - all period-appropriate. Note: grenade and dynamite examples in system notes are not reproduced.)
- **Track** (perception): 10% base. Follow trails of person, animal, or vehicle across terrain. Opposed by concealment. (ancient-world fit: hunter and wilderness archetype skill; core to chase and pursuit scenarios.)

### Social / Communication

- **Bargain** (communication): 05% base. Haggling, negotiation, compromise. Opposed rolls for important deals. (ancient-world fit: market cultures of the ancient world - merchant, thief, diplomat all use this.)
- **Command** (communication): 05% base. Inspiring and directing subordinates in combat or complex tasks. Critical success adds 1/5 Command as complementary bonus to subordinates. Party cohesion note: Command is a direct assist-the-party skill. (design.md §3.1 - party cohesion; Watch-item #2.)
- **Disguise** (communication): 01% base. Posture, costume, voice alteration. Opposed by Spot or Insight. (ancient-world fit: spies, infiltrators, assassins - all genre-standard.)
- **Etiquette (various)** (communication): 05% base. Per social group or culture. In WSR, specialties would be: Noble Court, Merchant Guild, Thieves' Guild, Temple, Military, Nomad Clan, etc. A character who knows one culture's etiquette is lost in another's without the relevant specialty. (ancient-world fit: decadent courts and hierarchical cultures make Etiquette essential; design.md §2.4.)
- **Fast Talk** (communication): 05% base. Quick convincing, bluffing, distraction. Temporary effect; wears off once the target thinks clearly. (ancient-world fit: the Leiber-urban-rogue toolkit; design.md §2.1.)
- **Insight** (perception): 05% base. Reading character, emotional state, and motives. Opposed by Etiquette, Fast Talk, or Persuade. (ancient-world fit: universal; especially relevant to social intrigue scenarios.)
- **Language (various)** (communication): INT x5 base for native. Per language; separate skill per language. Language Fluency Table is useful for ancient-world polyglots. (ancient-world fit: multilingual settings - Greek, Persian, Aramaic, Egyptian, various steppe tongues - are period-appropriate and mechanically interesting.)
- **Perform (various)** (communication): 05% base. Acting, dancing, playing an instrument, singing, oration, ritual performance. Setting Note in source: "Perform (Ritual) may augment a magic power." This is significant for WSR's sorcery-adjacent skills. Keep Perform (Ritual) as a named specialty. (ancient-world fit: bards, storytellers, cult performers; design.md §2.2 - "visual drama" in magic.)
- **Persuade** (communication): 15% base. Lasting opinion change through structured argument or appeal. Slower than Fast Talk; permanent if unchallenged. (ancient-world fit: universal social skill.)

### Knowledge / Mental

- **Appraise** (mental): 15% base. Estimating an object's worth - gems, weapons, horses, artworks. (ancient-world fit: traders, thieves, and adventurers all need this.)
- **First Aid** (mental): 30% base. Stop bleeding, bandage, revive unconscious. Cannot treat poison/disease effectively (use Medicine). The specific effect table (1D3 HP healed, revive from 0 HP if acted on immediately) is well-calibrated for naturalistic healing. (ancient-world fit: essential; design.md §1.2 point 7 - naturalistic healing; §1.3 - no healing magic.)
- **Gaming** (mental): INT+POW base. Resolving games of skill and strategy - chess, dice, gambling. To cheat: use Sleight of Hand or Fast Talk. (ancient-world fit: gambling and gaming are genre-standard in tavern and court scenes; design.md §2.1 - Leiber tone.)
- **Listen** (perception): 25% base. Hearing sounds, recognizing voices, detecting approaching danger. Both active and passive. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)
- **Sense** (perception): 10% base. Touch, smell, taste, and non-standard perception. Can detect the uncanny at GM's discretion. (ancient-world fit: obvious; potentially useful for sorcery detection in a genre where the supernatural is physically perceptible.)
- **Spot** (perception): 25% base. Actively or passively noticing details, hidden objects, disguised foes. (ancient-world fit: obvious.)

### Craft / Manipulation

- **Art (various)** (manipulation): 05% base. Creating visual or written art. Specialties pruned for ancient-world: Calligraphy, Drawing, Painting, Poetry, Pottery, Sculpture, Writing. Drop: Conceptual Art, Digital Art, Photography. (ancient-world fit with pruning; character distinctiveness - an artist-thief is meaningfully different from a warrior.)
- **Craft (various)** (manipulation): 05% base. Constructing functional objects. Ancient-world specialties: Blacksmithing, Carpentry, Ceramics, Cooking, Leatherworking, Locksmithing (ancient locks existed), Metallurgy, Stonemasonry, Ropemaking, Weaving. (ancient-world fit: core to artisan professions.)
- **Sleight of Hand** (manipulation): 05% base. Picking pockets, palming objects, stage magic, cheating at cards or dice. Opposed by Spot. (ancient-world fit: core rogue and entertainer skill; design.md §2.1 - Leiber tone.)

## Drop

Skills with no ancient-world referent or that require modern/futuristic technology. Brief reasons.

- **Artillery (various)**: as written, covers "modern or futuristic artillery and vehicular weapons" including cannons and automatic-fire systems. The ancient-world equivalent (ballista, catapult, scorpion) is mechanically simple enough that a specialist Craft or Missile Weapon specialty suffices, or it becomes a renamed Technical Skill. Drop the skill as a standalone; cover siege engines under Technical Skill (Siege Engine) in the Modify section. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; source/source-structure.md §6.)
- **Computer Use** (not in this chapter but referenced): not present as a standalone skill but appears in Technical Skill specialties. Dropped there. (design.md §1.2 point 3.)
- **Demolition**: explicitly covers gunpowder, dynamite, Molotov cocktails, plastic explosives, anti-matter bombs. System Notes reference blueprints, fuses, detonators. No ancient-world referent whatsoever. (design.md §1.2 point 3; source/source-structure.md §6.)
- **Drive (various)**: automobiles, motorcycles, trucks. The specialties include Bicycle, Carriage, and Chariot which have ancient-world referents, but these are better handled as Ride (Chariot) specialties or folded into Ride mechanics. Drop Drive; absorb period-appropriate vehicle handling into Ride. (design.md §1.2 point 3; source/source-structure.md §6.)
- **Energy Weapon (various)**: laser pistols, blasters, disruptors. Explicitly futuristic. Setting Notes flag this as for futuristic settings only. Drop entirely. (design.md §1.2 point 3.)
- **Firearm (various)**: pistols, rifles, machine guns, shotguns. No ancient-world referent. (design.md §1.2 point 3; source/source-structure.md §6.)
- **Fly**: for "beings with wings, antigravity belts, rocket boots, or flight powers." The source notes the base chance is halved for non-winged beings. In WSR, no PC races fly, and magical flight would be a sorcery effect, not a trained skill. Drop. (design.md §1.2 point 3; §1.3 - no demi-humans.)
- **Heavy Machine (various)**: forklifts, tanks, bulldozers, cranes, locomotives, large seagoing vessel engines. Entirely industrial/modern. (design.md §1.2 point 3.)
- **Heavy Weapon (various)**: bazookas, rocket launchers, tripod-mounted laser cannons, miniguns. Explicitly modern and futuristic. (design.md §1.2 point 3.)
- **Pilot (various)**: airplanes, airships, helicopters, starships, submarines. No ancient-world referent. (design.md §1.2 point 3; source/source-structure.md §6.)
- **Projection** (optional): targeting skill for power-using characters. Only relevant in games with Powers (Ch. 4). WSR does not use Mutations, Psychic Abilities, or Superpowers. Drop. (source/source-structure.md §2.)
- **Psychotherapy**: "a relatively modern concept" - the source's own Setting Notes flag this as 00% base chance in settings where psychological treatment doesn't exist. Ancient world: drop. (design.md §1.2 point 3.)
- **Repair (various)**: as written, covers Electrical, Electronic, Hydroelectric, Quantum, and Cybernetics specialties. The mechanical (Repair Mechanical) and structural (Repair Structural) specialties have some ancient-world referent but are well-covered by Craft. Drop Repair as a standalone skill; absorb period-appropriate maintenance into Craft. (design.md §1.2 point 3; source/source-structure.md §6.)

## Modify

Skills that survive but require changes - renamed, pruned of inappropriate specialties, restricted to era-relevant content, or expanded for WSR's specific needs.

- **Knowledge (various)**: canonise the skill, prune specialties. Keep: Academic Lore (Ancient Eras Only - this specialty is explicitly designed for ancient settings and serves as a general "learned person" skill; excellent), Anthropology, Archaeology, Art History, Folklore, History, Law (customary and codified law of the ancient world), Linguistics, Literature, Occult (directly relevant to the sorcery tone), Philosophy, Politics, Region/Subspecialty, Religion/Subspecialty, Streetwise. Drop from specialties: Accounting (absorb basic commerce into Bargain), Blasphemous Lore (collapse into Occult or make it an Occult subspecialty - its 0% base and SAN-link are worth preserving as a gateway to sorcery knowledge; see Defer), Business (corporate law is modern), Espionage (modern spy organizations), Genetics, Planetology, Psychology (modern), Quantum Mechanics, Xenobiology. The source's own note that "in fantasy and historical settings, only a small subset of specialties exist" justifies this pruning explicitly. WSR's Knowledge specialties: Academic Lore, Anthropology, Archaeology, Art History, Folklore, History, Law, Linguistics, Literature, Occult, Philosophy, Politics, Region, Religion, Streetwise. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §3.1 - opinionated extraction.)
- **Literacy (various)** (mental, optional): in the source, Literacy is optional and in modern settings equals Language rating automatically. For WSR's ancient world, Literacy is a genuine distinct skill - reading and writing were elite accomplishments. WSR modifies: Literacy is a separate skill with 00% base for all characters regardless of Language rating (matching the source's ancient-world intent). Characters must spend skill points to become literate. Specialties by script/language (Cuneiform, Hieroglyphic, Greek, Aramaic, etc.). This is a meaningful character distinctiveness tool - a warrior who can read is unusual; a scholar who cannot fight is equally so. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §3.1 - distinctiveness.)
- **Medicine** (mental): canonise the skill and effects table (the 1D3 HP healing per week, poison/disease stabilization, etc.). Modify: remove all futuristic equipment references (artificial spray skin, nanotech healers, medical scanners). Ancient-world Medicine is herbalism, surgery, and triage. The complementary bonus note (1/2 Medicine + 1/5 Science (Pharmacy) for First Aid) is kept but Science (Pharmacy) becomes Knowledge (Herbalism) in WSR. (design.md §1.2 point 7 - naturalistic healing; §1.3 - no healing magic.)
- **Research** (perception): 25% base. In source, explicitly covers "books, documents, articles, databanks, archives, or websites." For WSR: research is library and archive work only - consulting scrolls, interviewing sages, reading temple records. Remove all electronic/database references. Base chance stays 25%. Modify: Research requires Literacy in the relevant language to succeed except in oral-tradition contexts (where it functions as Knowledge). (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world.)
- **Science (various)** (mental): deeply anachronistic in most specialties (Genetics, Quantum Mechanics, Planetology, Computer Science, etc.). Modify severely: retain only pre-modern specialties as named Knowledge subspecialties rather than a separate Science skill. Retain: Astronomy (becomes Knowledge (Astronomy)), Botany (becomes Knowledge (Herbalism/Botany)), Chemistry (becomes Craft (Alchemy)), Natural History, Pharmacology (becomes Knowledge (Herbalism) or Craft (Alchemy)). Drop the Science skill as a category; redistribute its surviving specialties to Knowledge or Craft. The distinction between "scientific method" and "learned knowledge" is anachronistic in the ancient world. (design.md §1.2 point 3 - ancient world; §3.1 - "opinionated extraction.")
- **Status** (communication): 15% base. Canonise the skill but keep only the **Ancient/Dark Age/Medieval/Imperial Status table** (the middle of three tables). The Prehistoric and Victorian/Modern tables are dropped. The surviving table runs from Lower Class (Destitute, cap Destitute) through Monarchy (Wealthy, cap Wealthy). The Status-as-reward mechanic (1 point per adventure, 1D3-1D6 for extraordinary deeds) is directly relevant to the genre's concern with reputation and legend. Note: Status is not improved by experience/training only; it is a social-position tracker, not a practiced skill. This creates an interesting asymmetry with other skills. (design.md §1.2 point 3; §4.5 - "PCs grow in experience and reputation"; source/source-structure.md §6 - only Ancient/Dark Age table relevant; Watch-item #1 for reputation and distinctiveness.)
- **Strategy** (mental): 01% base. Military command at large scale - squads to armies, logistics, fleet coordination. Canonise for the genre (Conan as war-captain, Howard's many battles). Modify System Notes: remove the corporate-negotiation note. WSR Strategy is purely military. (design.md §2.4 - hostile wilderness and military conflicts; §2.3 - combat is brutal and consequential.)
- **Teach** (communication): 10% base. Training another character in a skill, requiring twice the student's rating or 50% (whichever lower). Canonise. This is a party cohesion tool: a high-skill PC can improve a lower-skill ally's capabilities between adventures through Teach rolls. (design.md §3.1 - party cohesion; Watch-item #2.)
- **Technical Skill (various)**: drop the modern specialties (Computers, Cybernetics, Electronics, Robotics, Sensors) but retain and expand ancient-world ones. WSR Technical Skill specialties: Siege Engine (operating a ballista, catapult, scorpion, or similar crew weapon - this absorbs the ancient-world case that Artillery covered), Traps (setting, identifying, disarming mechanical traps), Clockwork (rare; applicable in an ancient super-science context consistent with design.md §1.2 point 10). The renamed scope of this skill in WSR is narrow and profession-specific. (design.md §1.2 point 10 - "science fiction permitted" as lost super-science; source/source-structure.md §6.)

## Defer

- **Blasphemous Lore** (Knowledge subspecialty): the source defines it as starting at 0%, not increasable by experience, and tied to SAN loss. WSR dropped SAN as a mechanic (see source-2-characters.md), but the flavor of Blasphemous Lore - knowledge that corrodes the knower - is directly aligned with WSR's sorcery tone (design.md §2.2). Defer: decide in the magic chapter whether Blasphemous Lore becomes a gateway/prerequisite for sorcery paths, a Corruption track equivalent, or collapses into Occult with a special note. Do not eliminate; it is too thematically resonant to discard before the magic chapter is written.
- **Passions interaction with skills**: the source's Passions system (Chapter 10, already reviewed in source-4-passions-reputation.md) uses Passion ratings in opposed or complementary ways during skill rolls. The exact interaction between Passions and the Complementary Skill / Augment mechanics needs reconciling in one place (most likely the System chapter or a dedicated Passions sub-rule). Defer to Phase 2.
- **Hero Points / Luck Roll interaction**: as flagged in source-2-characters.md, the Luck Roll (POW x5) may be augmented or replaced by a Hero Point pool (design.md §2.3, §3.3). If a Hero Point spend can substitute for a failed skill roll, this has implications for how skills are calibrated. Defer to the system/combat chapter.
- **Complementary Skills list for WSR professions**: once the WSR profession list is finalized (flagged in source-2-characters.md Open Question #1), each profession's typical complementary pairings should be listed as examples in the Skills chapter text. E.g., Warrior: Command complements Strategy; Thief: Sleight of Hand complements Bargain; Sorcerer: Knowledge (Occult) complements Literacy. Defer to Phase 2 chapter drafting.
- **Craft (Alchemy)**: absorbs Chemistry and Pharmacology from Science. Whether Alchemy can produce supernatural results (potions with magical effects) or is purely naturalistic is a magic-chapter question. Defer the scope of Craft (Alchemy) to the magic chapter.

## Open Questions

1. **Siege Engine as Technical Skill vs. standalone**: WSR's Artillery drop leaves siege engine operation without a home. Technical Skill (Siege Engine) works mechanically, but it may be simpler to handle it as a Missile Weapon subspecialty or even a narrative detail (siege engines require a crew and are not PC-operated in most scenarios). Recommend deferring until the combat and equipment chapters clarify what role siege warfare plays.

2. **Literacy as gating mechanic**: making Literacy 0% base and requiring skill point investment is genre-authentic but may be frustrating in play if important clues or information are locked behind it. Consider whether the GM Book should advise GMs to provide oral-tradition or illustrated alternatives so non-literate characters are not stonewalled. Low priority but worth flagging before the Player Book Skills chapter is drafted.

3. **Ride (Chariot) vs. Drive (Chariot)**: the source's Drive specialty includes Chariot. WSR drops Drive but chariots are period-appropriate. This is resolved by absorbing Chariot into Ride specialties, but it should be stated explicitly in the WSR Ride entry rather than left implicit.

4. **Knowledge (Academic Lore) scope in WSR**: the source defines Academic Lore (Ancient Eras Only) as equivalent to a general knowledge roll "for nearly any area of knowledge" when the whole of written knowledge can fit in one library. This is a powerful and genre-appropriate specialty. In WSR it would allow a Scholar-type character to attempt almost any Knowledge roll at their Academic Lore rating. Whether this is too broad (making other specialties redundant for scholars) or appropriately reflecting the era's small body of written knowledge is worth deciding before the chapter is drafted.

5. **Status as a skill vs. a derived characteristic**: Status in the source cannot be improved by experience or training - only by deeds and social events. This makes it behave more like a reputation score than a skill. The Passions/Reputation chapter (source-4-passions-reputation.md) likely overlaps here. Consider whether Status should remain a skill (trained like any other) or be reclassified as a secondary characteristic that tracks social position separately from the skill list. If Reputation (from Chapter 10) is canonised, it may duplicate Status's social-leverage function.

## Watch-Items

### Character Distinctiveness (Watch-item #1)

Skills with specialty/specialisation mechanics are the primary distinctiveness engine in this chapter. The following create meaningfully different characters:

- **Melee Weapon (various)**: weapon-class specialties directly define a fighter's style (Spear vs. Sword vs. Axe are tactically different, not just cosmetically).
- **Missile Weapon (various)**: same logic - an archer and a slinger are different characters.
- **Parry (various)**: paired with Melee, reinforces weapon-style identity.
- **Martial Arts**: tradition specialties create the "school of fighting" identity layer above weapon skill.
- **Language (various)**: native language plus learned languages define cultural background. A character who speaks Persian, Greek, and Aramaic carries implicit backstory.
- **Knowledge (various)**: the breadth of subspecialties means two Scholar-profession characters can be completely different (Knowledge (Occult) vs. Knowledge (History) vs. Knowledge (Region)).
- **Craft (various)** and **Art (various)**: profession-grounding skills that make an artisan PC feel different from a warrior even without combat skills.
- **Etiquette (various)**: knowing one court's rules and being lost in another is a built-in distinctiveness and plot hook.
- **Status**: functions as a reputation score that can grow through deeds - a character's social position is meaningfully different depending on their history. Not a typical skill.
- **Literacy (modified)**: whether a character can read is a genuine distinction in the ancient world. A literate warrior or illiterate scholar is immediately characterful.
- **Ride (various)**: animal-type specialties (Horse vs. Camel vs. War Elephant) carry geographic and cultural implications.

Total skills with specialty/specialisation mechanics: approximately 14 out of 33 survivors. This is a strong distinctiveness foundation.

### Party Cohesion (Watch-item #2)

The Complementary Skill and Augment mechanics are the most significant party-cohesion finding in this chapter:

- **Complementary Skills** (pp. 34): adding 1/5 of a supporting skill to another PC's roll is explicitly party-applicable. The source says "you and other players will doubtless devise more." This is the BRP system's native assist mechanic and it is solid. No new design needed - just explicit guidance in the WSR text that cross-player complementary bonuses are standard. Examples: one PC uses Command to add a bonus to another PC's Persuade roll; a scout's Navigate augments the party's Ride check through difficult terrain.
- **Command** (communication): the most direct party-buffing skill. In combat, Command adds 1/5 Command as a complementary bonus to every subordinate's relevant roll each round.
- **Teach** (communication): between-session skill improvement for party members. A high-skill warrior who teaches a newer PC between adventures is engaging party cohesion fiction.
- **Augment mechanic**: one PC can augment another's roll at personal risk. This creates interesting party decisions about whether to risk the augment.

Gap assessment: the native BRP complementary/augment mechanics are more than adequate for party cohesion at the skill-resolution level. No additional cooperative mechanics need to be designed for the Skills chapter. The larger gap (shared history, group bonds) remains in the domain of Passions and chargen background questions.

### Mook Rules (Watch-item #3)

Nothing in this chapter bears on mook rules. No mechanical shortcuts for simplified NPC skill resolution appear. Gap confirmed; still pending.

### Sorcery Cost (Watch-item #4)

No sorcery-specific mechanics in this chapter. Oblique relevance: Knowledge (Occult), Literacy (in grimoire scripts), and Perform (Ritual) are the skill-level prerequisites that feed into sorcery access. Blasphemous Lore's 0% / non-improvable structure may inform how arcane knowledge is gated (deferred above).
