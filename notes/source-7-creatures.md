# Source Notes: Fantasy Creatures + Summoned Creatures (BRP UGE pp. 267-283)

**Section:** Chapter 11 / Creatures / Fantasy Creatures + Summoned Creatures
**Pages:** 267-283
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Creatures agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

Pages 267-277 cover eighteen Fantasy Creatures: Centaur, Dragon, Dwarf, Elf, Ghost, Ghoul, Giant, Griffin, Halfling, Minotaur, Mummy, Orc, Skeleton, Troll, Unicorn, Vampire, Werewolf, and Zombie. Pages 278-283 cover five Summoned Creatures: Angel, Demon (Greater), Demon (Lesser), and the four Elementals (Air, Earth, Fire, Water), plus a Chaotic Features table that applies to demons and chaotic creatures. The Tolkien demi-humans (Dwarf, Elf, Halfling) and the Angel are the clearest drops for genre reasons per design.md §1.3. The undead suite (Ghost, Ghoul, Mummy, Skeleton, Vampire, Zombie, Werewolf) is essentially genre-perfect and can be canonised with light modification. Demons (both tiers) are directly load-bearing for the sorcery sub-system and require detailed retention. The elementals as written are tonally soft and need a decision - the sorcery notes (source-1-sorcery.md) already flagged this; this read confirms the concern.

## Creature List and Decisions

### CANONISE

**Ghost** - Immaterial, POW-draining, malignant spirit. Mechanically elegant (only INT, POW, CHA; attacks by POW vs. POW resistance draining 1D3 PP). The "ghost reduces target to 0 PP, then may possess or compel suicide" escalation is genuinely dark and genre-appropriate. The source's ghost variants (redemption ghost, poltergeist, ghost vehicle) are mostly flavour; they can be trimmed to keep the WSR entry lean. Strong genre fit: ancient ruins, tombs of the deep past, cursed places per design.md §2.4. Fits as-is with minor prose reframe (strip the "ghost train" vehicle example which is modern).

**Ghoul** - Deformed human-adjacent predator feeding on the dead. Venom paralysis mechanic, PP-through-consumption, sunlight vulnerability, and howl-as-AOE are all mechanically distinct and flavourful. Infection vector (bitten but non-paralysed characters purge venom at 1 POT/day) creates useful scenario complications. The ghoul is simultaneously a mook-level threat and a persistent hazard per design.md §2.3. Canonise stat block largely as-is; prose reframe slightly toward the ancient-world register (graveyards and bone pits of fallen cities rather than "hideous deformed human").

**Giant** - Massive, flesh-eating humanoids; the source's 8-metre "generic" giant entry notes giants grow with age and lists varied subtypes (hill, sea, mountain, fire, frost). Stat block is straightforward (STR/CON/SIZ 6D6+36 at full-grown; 12-point skin from tough hide plus damage modifier dice). The sweep-attack specialization is mechanically useful. Giants threatening isolated settlements, guarding mountain passes, or serving ancient warlords fit design.md §2.4 (hostile wilderness, deep past). Note: the source's monster-size-for-hit-locations guidance (roll 1D10 for location against a giant) is dropped per the decisions.md ruling that hit location HP is not used. Canonise with that caveat.

**Minotaur** - Bull-headed humanoid dwelling in ruins or wild places; some bestial, some speaking. Berserk-on-wound mechanic (activates for CON rounds on a wound or special success strike, equivalent to Fury sorcery spell) is distinctive and genre-appropriate. Statblock is usable directly. Strong ancient-world-mythology resonance per design.md §2.4. Canonise.

**Mummy** - Preserved undead guardian of ancient tombs, animated by curse or necromancy. The fire-vulnerability rule (twice as easy to ignite, harder to extinguish) is mechanically elegant. The "mummy sorcerer" variant (retaining spells from former life, access to grimoire and spell scrolls in intact tomb) is a direct WSR scenario hook. Stat block solid (STR 6D6, POW 1D6+12, HP 15, 3-point skin). Canonise with light prose polish for ancient-world tone - no "some cults keep mummies in temples" softening; WSR mummies are from buried, pre-cataclysm civilisations per design.md §2.4.

**Orc** - Grayish-skinned predatory humanoids described as cannibalistic and brutal. The source's entry positions them as generic antagonists: lesser and greater stat variants, wolf-riding cavalry, sorcerer-priest subtype. Their Orc statblock (STR 4D6 / 14, POW 2D6+3 / 10) makes them competent but not dominant fighters. Critically: the sorcery-using orc (POW 16+ variant) is a useful NPC archetype (priest, chieftain, dangerous leader). The source says "Orcs often serve the forces of darkness" which is serviceable. Genre concern: "orc" as a concept carries heavy Tolkien freight that §1.3 rejects. However, WSR's world needs human-adjacent predatory humanoids to populate ruins, raider bands, and chaos cults. Decision: Canonise mechanically but rename and reframe editorially. In WSR these are "Goblinkin" or simply referenced descriptively; the stat block is kept. See Modify.

**Skeleton** - Animated bone constructs, created by sorcery or ancient curse. Mechanical distinctiveness: HP = SIZ (not CON), immune to bleeding/impaling secondary effects, immune to crushing damage from thrusting weapons unless special/critical, brittle (blow deals damage x4% chance of instant shattering). No fatigue points, never tires. This is exactly the mook-able, genre-correct undead swarm creature design.md §2.3 calls for. A party fighting skeletons in a tomb under torchlight is a core WSR encounter. Canonise directly.

**Troll** - Huge flesh-eating nocturnal humanoids with regeneration (1D3 HP/round) stopped by sunlight or fire. Per-round regeneration is distinctive and creates tactical pressure without adding chart complexity. Stat block strong (STR 3D6+12, CON 2D6+6, SIZ 4D6+12; 20 HP, 3-point skin). The "trollwife" sorceress variant (smaller, smarter, equipped with Curse of Sorcery, Pox, Undo Sorcery, Witch Sight) is an excellent named-adversary template. Ancient-world folklore troll (not Tolkien cave-troll) fits design.md §2.4 hostile wilderness. Canonise.

**Vampire** - The source entry is detailed and mechanically rich: Blood Drain draining STR or CON, Chill Touch draining PP, Hypnotic Gaze (POW vs. POW), regeneration (1D3 HP/round), Night Sight, plus optional powers (Legions of the Night, Mist Form, Shape Shifting, Spellcasting). Weaknesses are equally detailed (Blood Thirst costing 1D6 PP/day, Daylight damage, Decapitation, Fire, Holy Symbols, Holy Water, Sacred Earth, Stakes). The vampire's use of its current PP pool instead of POW to overcome targets is a clever mechanical twist. The range from "beautiful urbane vampire" to "barely sentient bat-creature" supports a wide NPC roster. Canonise the full entry. Note: the Holy Water/Holy Symbol weakness depends on the Allegiance system and Devotion Passions; WSR needs a ruling on how these translate when/if we use a modified Allegiance system (see Open Questions).

**Werewolf** - Three stat blocks (human form identical to PC stats; hybrid form STR 6D6/21, CON 2D6+6/13, SIZ 3D6/10-11; wolf form SIZ 3D6+1D3/12-13). Immunity to normal weapons (magic, fire, silver do full damage); regeneration (1 HP/round stopped by silver wounds). Infection mechanic (bite from non-silver weapon transmits lycanthropy at next full moon) is a persistent scenario hook. The hybrid form's two-claw-one-bite attack combination with the wolf form's faster bite are mechanically differentiated. Canonise.

**Zombie** - Slow (Move 4), strong, near-mindless animated corpse. The half-damage rule (most weapons do half, fire does full) and the optional "head-only kill" variant create useful tactical texture without adding chart complexity. Infectious bite variant is present. Zombies never dodge or parry - mechanically exactly right for a threatening-but-killable horde creature. Strong mook candidate (see Watch-items). Canonise.

### DROP

**Elf** - Per design.md §1.3: no Tolkien-style demi-humans as PCs. The source entry (INT 3D6+6 / 16, POW 2D6+6 / 13, high DEX; Longbow specialist; magic spells Dispel/Heal/Illusion/Perception/Resistance/Vision; naturally long-lived) is a recognisably Tolkien high-elf derived from D&D tradition. The entry even notes "half-elves" as a playable option. No genre basis in Howard, Leiber, or Moorcock for this creature type in this form. The ancient and strange peoples of the WSR world (Melniboneans, pre-cataclysm survivors, etc.) will be designed directly for the setting, not extracted from this entry. Drop entirely.

**Dwarf** - Per design.md §1.3. The source entry (short temper, fine craftsmanship, ancient grudges, excessive drinking, long beards; STR 4D6/14, low SIZ 1D4+4/6-7; Hammer and Battleaxe; Dark Vision super sense) is canonical Tolkien-lineage dwarf with no sword & sorcery genre anchor. Drop entirely.

**Halfling** - Per design.md §1.3. The source entry (pipe-smoking, farming, innkeeping, underground warrens, quiet peaceful villages; STR 2D6/7, -1D6 damage modifier) is direct Tolkien pastiche. Even the BRP source's own text notes their "fondness for eating, pipe-smoking, and drinking." Drop entirely.

**Angel** - Winged divine emissary with flaming sword; skills at Command 100%/Dodge 100%/Flight 100%; Divine Radiance (10-pt armor), Healing Touch (1 HP per PP spent, permanent POW cost for major wounds), Holy Fire (10D6), Voice of Heaven (AOE stun); stats POW 4D6+6/26, CHA 4D6+12/26. The source notes that viewing an angel may restore SAN. This is the D&D/Christian celestial hierarchy in stat-block form. Sword & sorcery per design.md §1.1 does not feature benevolent divine agents intervening to help mortals. Powers who notice mortals in WSR are either indifferent or dangerous (Moorcock patron-beings, chaos gods). An entity with Healing Touch and Holy Fire that protects pious characters is alien to the genre's tone per design.md §2.2 ("alien, costly, and corrupting" - applied to all supernatural entities, not just magic). The angel also requires the Allegiance system to function properly (alignment-based immunity to holy fire; SAN restoration for the pious). If WSR uses a modified Allegiance system, the angel's mechanics do not map onto it. Drop. Note: the *idea* of ancient divine powers is not dropped - those belong in the GM Book as patron-entities, designed WSR-specifically. This stat block is not a usable template for that.

**Unicorn** - "Sign of chivalric and maidenly virtue," heals wounds with its horn (1 PP per HP restored), detects poison, reattaches severed limbs if 6+ PP spent, permanent POW cost for infirmities. "Especially trusting of maidenly human or elf women." This creature is drawn from medieval European romance and heraldry, not sword & sorcery. The magical-horse-with-horn is not an antagonist or a creature of the hostile wilderness per design.md §2.4. The healing power explicitly contradicts design.md §1.2 feature 7 (naturalistic healing, no healing magic) and §2.2. Drop.

### MODIFY

**Centaur** - The source entry presents centaurs as "pastoral beings, keepers of lore, fine craftsmen, great musicians" who "serve as mentors or guardians." This is the benevolent centaur of Greek myth as filtered through D&D. However, centaurs have genuine ancient-world roots (Greek mythology, Mesopotamian hybrid creatures) and the stat block is mechanically fine (STR 3D6+6/16-17, SIZ 4D6+12/26, composite bow + lance + bastard sword + kick + trample). The issue is the description, not the creature concept. Modify: strip the "pastoral, wise mentor" framing entirely. WSR centaurs are predatory raiders of the open steppe, servants of dark gods of the hunt, or ancient things that have gone feral over millennia. They are dangerous, unpredictable, and do not assist lost travellers. The mechanical stat block is retained largely as-is. The "Powers: Centaurs prefer magic to sorcery" note is kept (they may have magic spells at GM discretion) but the specific spell list (Enhance, Heal, Illusion, Perception, Vision) should strip Heal per design.md §2.2.

**Dragon** - The stat block is usable (STR 20D6/70, CON 10D6/35, SIZ 20D6/70, HP 53, Armor 12-pt scales, Breathe Flame 75%/4D6+special). The "some dragons are intelligent, know magic or sorcery" variant is excellent - a dragon with a grimoire is a genuine WSR villain (ancient intelligence, hoard including arcane knowledge, fire breath as area attack). The problem is the source's generic fantasy framing ("hoarders, collecting gold or other treasures"). Modify: WSR dragons are survivors of an earlier age, not mere animals. They are rare, ancient, and their lairs contain fragments of the deep past per design.md §2.4. The bestial dragon stats are kept; the intelligent variant gets a note that it may know sorcery (not magic). The "dragons may have breath weapons other than flame" note is kept as GM guidance. The "Energy Projection" superpower reference is stripped (superpowers are dropped from WSR entirely).

**Griffin** - Heraldic lion-eagle chimera; "traditionally depicted in heraldry." This heraldic framing is the problem, not the creature. The griffin is otherwise a large predatory flying beast (STR 10D6/35, SIZ 10D6/35, +3D6 damage modifier, Bite 70%/Claw 70%) that fits the hostile wilderness perfectly per design.md §2.4. Modify: drop all heraldic/emblematic framing. WSR griffins are apex predators nesting in high crags, encountered as hazards in wild terrain or kept as mounts by ancient kings. The stat block is retained; the flavour text is rewritten entirely.

**Orc (as Goblinkin)** - See Canonise above. Mechanically sound, thematically overloaded. Modify: rename "Orc" to a generic editorial direction of "Goblinkin" (or let the GM Book supply a setting-specific name). The physical description (grayish skin, fangs, pointed ears, darkness-preference) is kept as it is not inherently Tolkienian. Strip the word "goblins" from the source ("sometimes referred to as goblins"). The wolf-riding cavalry variant is kept. The sorcerer-priest subtype is kept but the specific spell list should be updated to the WSR spell list once finalised. The "despise humanity, elves, dwarves" enemy list strips elves and dwarves (dropped). The "forces of darkness" framing is usable if WSR uses a modified Allegiance-type system; defer the exact wording to the GM Book chapter.

**Elementals - Reframe as Bound Spirits** - The source describes elementals as "primal elemental spirits" summoned via the sorcery spell Summon Elemental. The sorcery notes (source-1-sorcery.md, Defer section) flagged these as feeling "more pleasant than demons," and this read confirms it strongly. The four entries as written:
  - Air Elemental: "floating, transparent female humans of surpassing beauty," mischievous, short attention span, "often called sylphs." POW 4D6/14.
  - Earth Elemental: slow-witted, grumpy, surly when summoned, "often called gnomes." STR 5D6+12/29-30. Breaks metal weapons.
  - Fire Elemental: "four-legged reptiles made of fiery plasma," irritable and quick-tempered, "often called salamanders." Flame Breath 50%.
  - Water Elemental: "beautiful aquatic, vaguely translucent human females," alluring, scantily clad, "often called undines." Can drown opponents.

  The air and water elementals' "beautiful female" descriptions are inappropriate per design.md §3.2 (pulp-era prejudice does not survive the port). The "sylph/gnome/salamander/undine" labelling is European folklore-adjacent rather than ancient-world appropriate. The "generally irritable but still do the summoner's bidding" disposition reads as D&D-style helpful magical servant rather than dangerous bound entity.

  Decision: **Modify rather than drop or merge into a single Spirit entity.** The four elemental types are kept because they are mechanically distinct and the Summon Elemental sorcery spell is worth retaining (see source-1-sorcery.md). However, they are significantly reframed:
  - All physical descriptions are rewritten. No "beautiful female" forms; elementals manifest as raw expressions of their element (a column of compressed wind, a moving mass of hot stone, a pillar of crawling flame, a surge of black water).
  - The "pleasant/cooperative" disposition is replaced. Elementals are alien intelligences. They are not malicious like demons, but they are not helpful either; they are vast, focused, and difficult to direct. Binding one is dangerous not because it hates you but because it does not sufficiently value your continued existence.
  - The "often called sylphs/gnomes/salamanders/undines" folklore names are dropped from the main entry. A GM note can reference these as names given by those who have traded with them, not names the entities acknowledge.
  - The female-coded descriptions of air and water elementals are stripped entirely.
  - Mechanical stat blocks and powers are retained as-is (they are mechanically sound). Only flavour text and disposition framing change.
  - Elementals remain distinct from demons: they have no personal agendas, do not negotiate in the way demons do, and cannot be permanently bound in the same way (they simply dissipate and return to their element). This keeps the demon-summoning sub-system as the darker, more costly option.

### DEFER

**Dragon (whether to include a "stat block" in the Player-accessible material)** - If a PC becomes a dragon-slayer and needs to face one, the GM needs stats. But whether WSR provides a worked example of a dragon encounter or keeps the dragon in "here be monsters" territory is a GM Book design question. Defer to GM Book chapter.

**Vampire (holy symbol/holy water mechanics)** - The vampire's weaknesses reference Allegiance (50 allegiance to a "good" deity to create holy water; Devotion Passion at 80%+ to affect with holy symbols). WSR's position on Allegiance is not yet final (source-9-allegiance notes pending). Until the Allegiance decision is made, these specific thresholds should be held. The mechanical existence of the weakness is retained; the threshold values are provisional. Flag for the magic/setting chapter.

**Chaotic Features table** - The D100 table on p. 283 gives 38+ entries of random mutations/bonuses for demons and chaotic creatures (POW bonus, DEX bonus, STR bonus, acid spitting, fire breathing, magic absorption, regeneration, hypnotic pattern, gem-body, etc.). This is a useful GM tool for making each demon or chaotic creature feel distinct. Defer decision on whether to include the full table in the GM Book or compress it to a shorter "representative" table. The concept is sound and genre-appropriate (Chaos as physical mutation is Moorcock-lineage, per design.md §1.1). Lean toward include.

## Demon Stats - Detailed Record

Both tiers are documented here for cross-reference with the sorcery sub-system.

### Lesser Demon (p. 280)

"A grotesque creature, like an emaciated gargoyle with claws, wings, and horns." Wishes to prolong its time in this world as long as possible (it is bullied and tormented in the infernal realms). Not cowardly but prefers combat as a last resort.

| Stat | Roll | Typical |
|------|------|---------|
| STR | 2D6+3 | 10 |
| CON | 3D6 | 10-11 |
| SIZ | 1D6+6 | 9-10 |
| INT | 2D6+3 | 10 |
| POW | **3D6+6** | **13** |
| DEX | 3D6+3 | 13-14 |
| CHA | 1D6+3 | 6-7 |

- Move: 8 (12 flying)
- Hit Points: 10
- Damage Modifier: None
- Armor: 1-pt scaled hide
- Attacks: Claw 50% / 1D3+dm (bleeding); Bite 35% / 1D3+1/2dm (bleeding); Grapple 45% special
- Can make two claw attacks per round; if both hit, grapples and bites next round
- Skills: Bargain 55%, Climb 65%, Dodge 50%, Fast Talk 40%, Fly 75%, Hide 45%, Knowledge (Region: Hell) 35%, Listen 40%, Sense 35%, Spot 50%, Stealth 35%
- Powers: Not particularly powerful; may have sorcery if POW sufficient. Common spells: Curse of Sorcery (4), Flames of the Sun (4), Midnight (1), Pox (1). Sorcerer summoning it may choose its known spells if research done. May have chaotic features.
- POW range: approximately 9-19, typical 13

### Greater Demon (p. 279)

"An infernal being, summoned or on this plane for terrible purpose." Not a mere guardian or servant - encountered because something monumental is at stake. May be bestial or witty, urbane, charming, and persuasive. Viewing one risks SAN loss even if you summoned it.

| Stat | Roll | Typical |
|------|------|---------|
| STR | 3D6+24 | 34-35 |
| CON | 3D6+12 | 22-23 |
| SIZ | 3D6+12 | 22-23 |
| INT | 3D6 | 10-11 |
| POW | **3D6+6** | **16-17** |
| DEX | 3D6+6 | 16-17 |
| CHA | 3 or 21 | 3 or 21 |

- Move: 10 (12 flying)
- Hit Points: 22-23
- Damage Modifier: +2D6
- Armor: 8-pt tough hide (may wear additional armor)
- Attacks: Great Sword (one-handed) 150% / 2D8+dm (bleeding) +2D6 flame (special); Whip 90% / 1D3-1+1/2dm (entangle) +1D6 fire (special); Flame Breath 100% / 6D6 fire (special); Claws 150% / 1D6+dm (bleeding); Grapple 150% special
- Combat style: Opens with Flame Breath, then immobilizes with whip, then great sword 5 DEX ranks later. Two or more attacks per round. Immune to fire.
- Skills: Climb 90%, Dodge 75%, Fly 100%, Jump 75%, Sense 75%, Spot 75%, Throw 75%
- Powers (select or all): Aura of Terror (POW vs. POW in meters radius; failure = terror-stricken, all attacks Difficult; costs 1 PP, lasts POW combat rounds); Blight (corrupts area POW x100 meters over 30 days minus POW; 0 PP cost, automatic, takes months to reverse); Flame Breath (CON-meter range, 3-meter square area, 6D6 fire, lingers 3 rounds; costs 6 PP); Flight (bat wings, effortless); Regeneration (1D6 HP/round, no PP cost); Sheath of Smoke and Fire (1D6 fire to anything within 2 meters; 1-pt extra armor vs. wooden missile weapons; costs 1 PP/round to maintain). Plus possible chaotic features.
- CHA: Fixed at 3 (bestial, horrifying) or 21 (urbane, supernaturally attractive) - not rolled, a creature-design choice.
- POW range: approximately 9-24, typical 16-17

### Key Binding Mechanics (cross-ref source-1-sorcery.md)

- Summoning requires preparation (magical circle, occult research, special items)
- Binding costs the sorcerer 1-3 permanent POW
- Binding types: flesh-binding (demon inhabits the sorcerer's body); object-binding (demon inhabits weapon, amulet, etc.); place-binding (demon is fixed to a location)
- True name surrendered on binding; sorcerer can recall and dismiss using it
- Four ways a binding breaks: sorcerer dismisses, demon killed, binder killed, another learns true name and rebinds
- A sorcerer may choose the lesser demon's known spells if sufficient research is done beforehand - this is the primary mechanical reason to research a demon before summoning

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

No mechanisms in this section that directly create PC distinctiveness. However, creature entries influence PC distinctiveness indirectly:
- A sorcerer with a bound lesser demon is a distinctive character defined partly by that demon's personality, known spells, and the terms of its binding.
- Vampire, werewolf, and ghoul entries include infection/transformation mechanics. A PC who has been bitten by a werewolf (not yet transformed) or is managing an advancing ghoul venom countdown is in a distinctive narrative position.
- No concerns flagged here that need resolution in the creatures chapter specifically.

### Party cohesion

- The lesser demon's Bargain 55% and Fast Talk 40% suggest it can be a negotiating actor rather than a pure combatant - a sorcerer who has bound a demon with useful social skills has an indirect party-cohesion asset.
- No specific party cohesion mechanics in this section.
- Zombie and skeleton hordes lend themselves naturally to mook-swarm encounters where all party members contribute (see below).

### Mook rules

This is the most important watch-item for the creatures chapter.

Several creatures here are inherently mook-scale and should be handled as groups:
- **Skeleton**: HP = SIZ (10-11 typical); brittle (instant-shatter chance); no fatigue, no dodge variation. A skeleton is designed to be dispatched. Strong mook candidate.
- **Zombie**: Move 4, never dodges or parries, half damage from most weapons. The horde framing is built into the source. Strong mook candidate.
- **Orc (lesser)**: STR 14, HP 10, no exceptional powers. As written they fight with 35% skill weapons. These are mook-level in practice.
- **Ghoul**: More threatening than orc/skeleton/zombie; the venom paralysis and howl make individual ghouls more interesting tactically. Not a mook - a named threat. Small groups could be mook-like if handled with group initiative rules.
- **Ghost**: Purely PP-draining; can only be harmed by sorcery, magic weapons, and certain powers. This is more a puzzle-creature than a mook. Named encounter.

The mook-rules question (see design.md §2.3 and §3.3) remains open. This section confirms that skeleton and zombie stat blocks are already designed to be dispatched quickly, which is what WSR needs. The question is whether the GM Book provides a formal "mook handling" rule (group attacks, single HP threshold) or relies on the existing stat blocks being thin enough to use as-is. Flag for the GM chapter design pass.

### Sorcery cost (demon binding)

Confirmed: the demon binding system carries permanent POW cost (1-3 permanent POW per binding). This is the clearest existing example of the "permanent cost for major sorcerous acts" mechanic in Option B / Option F from source-1-sorcery.md. The binding cost varies by demon tier (lesser demon binds imply lower cost; greater demon bindings are described as requiring "considerable effort" and the permanent POW cost is mentioned in the sorcery chapter). Exact per-tier cost values should be confirmed against pp. 87-102 when drafting the sorcery chapter.

## Open Questions

1. **Orc renaming.** What is the WSR editorial direction for human-adjacent predatory humanoid antagonists? "Goblinkin" is a working label but the final name choice belongs in the GM Book worldbuilding section. Does the creature chapter use a generic label, or leave the name blank with a note?

2. **Vampire holy symbol/holy water thresholds.** These depend on Allegiance, which WSR has not yet decided. Can the vampire entry be finalised before the Allegiance chapter is resolved? Options: (a) specify that holy symbols work per the Allegiance rules once those are finalised; (b) replace with a GM-judgment rule ("symbols held by a character of genuine faith" as a fictional rather than mechanical threshold); (c) simply note the weakness and let the GM Book chapter on Allegiance fill in the threshold. Lean toward (c).

3. **Greater demon CHA.** The fixed CHA of 3 (bestial) or 21 (urbane) is a design decision, not a roll. This implies the sorcerer who summons a greater demon encounters a specific creature with a specific personality type, not a randomly generated one. This is correct for WSR - each greater demon is a named entity with a fixed nature. But this means the GM Book needs a roster of at least a few named greater demons with their types fixed. Flag for GM Book creature chapter.

4. **Angel replacement.** Dropping the angel leaves a gap in the "divine powers make contact with mortals" design space. WSR's equivalent should be designed for the GM Book: perhaps patron-entities (cosmic powers, ancient gods of the deep past) that communicate through oracles, signs, and terrible apparitions rather than winged emissaries with healing touch. This is a creation task, not an extraction task. Flag for GM Book design.

5. **Chaotic Features table scope.** The existing table has 38 entries; about a third are pure stat bonuses (POW+1D6, DEX+2D6, STR+1D6) and the rest are qualitative special powers. For WSR, the stat bonuses are mechanically fine but feel less interesting than the qualitative entries. Should WSR include a condensed version weighting toward the qualitative features? Design call for the GM Book.

6. **Elemental stat blocks with superpower references.** Each elemental entry lists "Superpowers" in its Powers section (e.g., Air Elemental has Energy Control/Wind and Energy Projection/Wind superpowers). Superpowers are dropped from WSR. The stat blocks need these lines stripped without losing the underlying ability description. The abilities themselves (an air elemental commanding wind, an earth elemental moving through stone) should be retained as native elemental abilities described in plain-language terms rather than referencing dropped game systems.

7. **Dragon as singular encounter.** Should the GM Book include guidance on running a dragon encounter as a campaign-level event (not a random wilderness encounter) rather than just a stat block? The ancient-survivors framing implies dragons are rare enough to be campaign-defining threats. Consider a brief "Dragon as Antagonist" sidebar in the GM Book rather than just a stat block.

## Book Placement

**All creatures go in the GM Book.** Per design.md §3.1 ("the GM Book holds what a GM needs: running the game, bestiary, NPC sorcerers, scenario seeds, treasure, optional modules").

Within the GM Book bestiary, suggested organisation:
- **Undead** (Ghost, Ghoul, Mummy, Skeleton, Vampire, Zombie, Werewolf) - together, as a thematic cluster. These are the most common antagonist type for tomb-and-ruin scenarios per design.md §2.4.
- **Wilderness Threats** (Dragon, Giant, Griffin, Troll, Minotaur, Centaur) - together. These populate the hostile wilderness per design.md §2.4.
- **Human-Adjacent Predators** (Goblinkin/Orc) - brief entry; the NPC Digest (pp. 288-297) and Customizing Creatures (pp. 298-299) provide the main tool for building humanoid antagonist groups.
- **Summoned Entities** (Demons Lesser and Greater, Elementals) - together, cross-referenced with the Sorcery chapter. Demons require the most annotation (binding mechanics, negotiation, true names).

Player Book placement: none. Creature stat blocks are GM-side. A sorcerer's Player Book entry for Summon Demon describes what the PC does and what it costs; the GM Book contains the demon on the other side of the ritual.
