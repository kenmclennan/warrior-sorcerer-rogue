# Source Notes: Allegiance (BRP UGE pp. 250-252)

**Section:** Chapter 10 / Settings / Allegiance (Option)
**Pages:** 250-252
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Allegiance agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Recommendation

**Canonise as a sorcery patron mechanic - stripped, not as written.**

Do not canonise Allegiance as a general-purpose belief/devotion system for all PCs. The full system - multiple competing allegiance scores accumulating across a career, divine intervention on a critical roll, apotheosis at 100+ - is a theological framework that does not fit sword & sorcery's genre values (design.md §1.2, §1.3: anti-heroes, personal codes, no save-the-village morality, no divine patron as character arc).

However, the core mechanical chassis - a numerical patronage score that accrues through specific actions, unlocks benefits at a threshold, and can invite divine intervention - is exactly what "patron debt (narrative)" from `source-1-sorcery.md` needs if it is to have player-facing structure rather than being pure GM discretion (Option E of the sorcery cost debate). A single stripped-down Allegiance track, renamed and reskinned, can serve as the WSR patron-debt mechanic for sorcerers who have taken a patron. It gives the sorcery cost debate a mechanical backbone without requiring bespoke design.

The Apotheosis end-state (100+ score, becoming a living avatar of the power) is the single hardest genre question. It is appropriate for Moorcock-lineage play (Elric as Chaos Champion is effectively an apotheosis trajectory) and wrong for Howard-lineage play (Conan explicitly rejects divine service). WSR's answer should be: Apotheosis is an optional arc available only to sorcerers with a patron, never to warriors or rogues, and the GM Book frames it as a trajectory toward tragedy rather than transcendence per design.md §1.1.

## Summary

Allegiance is BRP UGE's optional system for tracking a character's devotion to one or more divine forces, moral principles, or cosmic powers. The key mechanics:

**Score structure:** Each force has a separate allegiance score, always 0 or positive, never reduced below 0. No cap listed except the apotheosis trigger at 100+. Multiple allegiances can coexist; only the highest (if 20+ points above all others) confers allied status.

**Starting scores:** Roll 1D6-2 (minimum 0) per available allegiance. Occultist, priest, and shaman professions add POW/2 (round up) distributed as desired. Power types tied to spiritual forces add +1 per applicable power type.

**Increasing scores:** After an adventure in which the character performed actions favored by a force, the GM calls for an allegiance test (same procedure as a skill improvement check). On success, score increases 1D6. If multiple allegiances were favored, multiple tests are called. Scores almost never decrease - aberrant behavior against an allegiance (when allied at 20+ differential) may trigger a D100 roll; if the roll exceeds the score, an opposed allegiance increases by 1D4-1D8 depending on severity.

**Allied status:** A character becomes allied to a force when their score in it is 20+ higher than all other allegiance scores AND the character consciously acknowledges the allegiance. Benefits are not available to a character who ignores the connection even if the score qualifies.

**Benefits of Allegiance (at allied status, on a successful allegiance roll):**
- Instant reserve power points: up to 1/10 of current allegiance score (round up), drawn as last PP before unconsciousness; up to three times per session.
- Temporary hit points: up to 1/5 of current allegiance score (round up) before normal HP, expended first, once per session.
- Skill point pool: entire current allegiance score added as temporary skill points, distributed across skills before rolling, cannot be used for characteristic/experience/allegiance rolls; up to three times per session.
- Divine intervention: at 20+ differential, on a roll equal to a critical success, the power intervenes directly or through proxy; potentially unlimited force.

**Maintaining allegiance:** At 100+, if allied, behavior contrary to the force is at "the character's peril." The character may lose benefits, be considered an enemy by fellow believers, face divine/karmic punishment, or be required to perform penance (fasting, quest, sacrifice, purification ritual).

**Creating allegiances:** The GM defines forces and a checklist of actions worth points (major = 3, significant = 2, minor = 1). Opposed actions reward the opposing force instead of penalizing the original.

**Apotheosis (at 100+, allied):** The force may ask the character in a vision or divine message to serve it as its living avatar/champion. Not automatic - the character must demonstrate faith and accept the offer. If accepted: the character transforms into a living avatar and steadfast champion. Benefits include monumental personal goal, limited immortality (body does not age or suffer disease; can still be killed by weapons; may include hideous scarring or supernatural marks), doubled permanent HP (CON+SIZ or double current), tripled skill ratings in three chosen skills, a signature artifact, or consumption by the force and rebirth as a divine being (effectively removing the character from play as a mortal). At apotheosis, the force communicates constantly and may override PC agency.

**Scope note:** The system is explicitly genre-agnostic. The source provides examples: Christian Heaven/Hell, pantheonic gods, science fiction Law/Anarchy, superhero Right/Wrong. The mechanics are the same regardless of the allegiance's nature.

## Canonise / Drop / Modify / Defer

### Drop

- **Allegiance as a general-purpose system for all PCs.** Warriors and rogues accumulating devotion scores to divine forces and earning divine intervention is not sword & sorcery - it is high fantasy theology. Conan does not accrue Crom points. Per design.md §1.2 (anti-heroes with personal codes, personal not universal) and §1.3 (high fantasy heroism rejected, save-the-village morality rejected).

- **Multiple competing allegiance scores.** The core concept of a character having several allegiances at once, any of which might be rewarded after a given adventure, assumes a morality-play frame (Heaven vs. Hell, Law vs. Chaos as alignment systems). This is the register of Moorcock's cosmic scale, not the human-scale of Leiber or Howard. One patron, one score, for sorcerers who have one. Drop the multi-score apparatus.

- **Allegiance starting scores for all PC archetypes.** In the source, priests, occultists, and shamans get POW/2 distributed across allegiances at character creation. This implies every magic-using PC has divine connections by default. In WSR, patron relationships are exceptional and chosen, not default. Drop the automatic starting score distribution.

- **Skill point pool benefit.** Gaining a pool of skill points from an allegiance score to throw at any chosen skills before a roll is a mechanical benefit with no genre resonance - it reads as D&D inspiration in a theological frame. The PP reserve and temporary HP benefits are more dramatically coherent (miraculous survival, the power sustaining its champion in extremis). The skill pool is clean to drop.

- **Allied status requirement tied to "conscious acknowledgement."** The source's rule that a character must consciously acknowledge an allegiance to benefit from it is sensible for a theology-agnostic generic system but adds unnecessary cognitive overhead in a stripped implementation. In WSR's version, the patron relationship is explicit from the moment it is established.

### Modify (toward a WSR Patron Score mechanic)

- **Rename and reskin entirely.** "Allegiance" is the wrong word for sword & sorcery patron relationships. The Moorcock idiom uses "patron" or "compact" or "debt." A WSR implementation should be called the Patron Score or Patron Compact score, making it explicit that it tracks obligation and favor with a specific entity, not general devotion to a cosmic force.

- **Restrict to sorcerers with an established patron.** Not all sorcerers need a patron. Not all sorcerers with a patron need a mechanical score. The Patron Score is an optional layer for sorcerers who have specifically entered into a compact with a named entity (demon, old god, chaos power, forgotten elemental lord). It tracks both the favor accrued and the debt owed - the same number serves both purposes. Per design.md §2.2 and §1.2 feature 6.

- **Earning patron score.** The source's "perform favored action, make allegiance test after adventure" is a sound chassis. In WSR, the GM defines three to five actions the patron values (appropriate to that patron's nature) and three to five it punishes. After each adventure in which such actions occurred, the sorcerer makes a patron test. Success: score increases 1D6. The GM should set these during patron creation in the GM Book.

- **The benefits (PP reserve and temporary HP).** These two are directly genre-appropriate - the patron sustaining its champion when they are near death is exactly right. Keep both, scaled to the Patron Score. The PP reserve benefit (1/10 of score as emergency PP, up to three times per session) is especially well-designed for WSR because it interacts directly with the sorcerer's PP pool, which also fuels Fate Points (per the locked decision: Fate Points share the PP pool). A sorcerer with a high Patron Score has an emergency reservoir - but only while still in the patron's favor. This is a meaningful, legible tradeoff.

- **Divine intervention - rename to Patron Intercession.** The mechanic (20+ score differential, critical-equivalent roll, patron intervenes directly) is the right shape for "the patron shows up when things are desperate." In WSR it should be called Patron Intercession, should require the sorcerer to invoke the patron by true name as a full action, and should carry a consequence: a successful Patron Intercession always increases the Patron Score by 2D6 (accelerating toward obligation). Per design.md §2.2 - "every step costs something."

- **Score maintenance and aberrant behavior.** The source's penance mechanic (quest, sacrifice, purification) is directly appropriate for sword & sorcery. A sorcerer who offends their patron is in deep trouble. Keep the structure: if the score exceeds 20+ and the sorcerer acts against the patron's interests, the GM may require a D100 roll against the score; failure means a direct consequence (withdrawal of benefits, patron contact demanding explanation, patron sending agents). This is GM Book territory.

### Defer

- **Apotheosis.** The 100+ end-state is an entire campaign arc question. For some WSR campaigns (Moorcock-lineage, running the Eternal Champion thread), apotheosis is the right tragic conclusion to a sorcerer's career: the force finally consumes them and they are no longer a mortal adventurer. For Howard-lineage campaigns, this end-state never occurs because the sorcerer's patron eventually betrays them or is destroyed. The decision is: include Apotheosis as a GM Book option for Moorcock-tone campaigns only, framed as a trajectory toward loss of self rather than transcendence? Or drop it entirely and leave the patron relationship open-ended? This needs a design call at the magic chapter stage.

  **Preliminary position:** Include in the GM Book as an optional campaign-end scenario for patron-bonded sorcerers, explicitly labeled as "appropriate for Moorcock-lineage campaigns." Frame the apotheosis benefits as costs: limited immortality that includes hideous scarring, constant divine communication that overrides PC agency, the character becoming a NPC. This turns apotheosis from a power fantasy into what the genre says it actually is - the moment the sorcerer is consumed by the thing they made a deal with. Per design.md §1.1 (Moorcock: doomed, decadent, adventures end in tragedy or terrible knowledge).

## Open Questions

1. **Single patron or multiple?** BRP's system explicitly allows multiple allegiances. WSR's sorcerer might have made deals with more than one patron over a career. Does the Patron Score track the single dominant compact, or does each patron relationship have its own score? Multiple scores create interesting tension (two patrons with conflicting demands) but also bookkeeping complexity. Likely: one score per patron, maximum of two active patron relationships before the mechanic breaks down; GM Book advice on rival patrons.

2. **Does any non-sorcerer PC ever use this?** The source grants allegiance points to occultists, priests, shamans. Could a rogue with a thieves' guild patron or a warrior sworn to a battle-god have a Patron Score? The genre supports patron-warrior relationships (Solomon Kane and Providence; the Eternal Champion and the Balance). If yes, the mechanic needs a non-sorcery flavor. This could be thin: a warrior's patron gives the PP reserve benefit as a "reserve of battle-fury" that refills their Fate Points rather than their sorcery PP. A small design note, but not a full system.

3. **What triggers a patron's attention?** The source's allegiance test triggers are generic (any favored action). WSR's patron relationships should feel specific to the entity. The GM Book needs a patron creation framework that includes 3-5 trigger actions and 3-5 offenses. This is design work for the GM Book chapter, not the Player Book.

4. **Interaction with sorcery cost mechanic (still deferred from source-1-sorcery.md).** If the hybrid sorcery cost option (Option F: PP for ordinary casting, second track for major workings) is chosen, the Patron Score is a natural candidate for the second track. Major demon bindings or invoking the patron's power directly would increase the Patron Score, accelerating the sorcerer toward apotheosis. This would make the cost mechanic design decision resolve two deferred questions simultaneously. Flag for the magic chapter discussion.

5. **Where does the Patron Intercession mechanic sit relative to the Invoking a God rule in the Sorcery section?** The Sorcery section (pp. 87-102, `source-1-sorcery.md`) has an "Invoking a God" rule with a 1% base chance (Difficult if the sorcerer is a champion). The Patron Intercession mechanic in a stripped Allegiance system would replace this for patron-bonded sorcerers, giving them a better-than-1% chance. Non-patron sorcerers retain the base 1% rule. These two should be harmonized before the magic chapter is finalized.

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

**Moderate match.** A Patron Score creates a visible, numerically tracked relationship that differentiates patron-bonded sorcerers from unbonded ones. Two sorcerers with the same spell list feel different if one has an active patron compact (emergency PP reserve, risk of patron intercession, obligation mounting) and the other has no patron. The patron's specific trigger actions also shape what the sorcerer chooses to do in play - a sorcerer whose patron values betrayal of the innocent will make different choices than one whose patron values destruction of divine servants. This is character distinctiveness through mechanical pressure rather than stat differentiation, which fits design.md §3.1 (distinctive without classes).

### Party cohesion

**Weak match - but with potential.** The source's allegiance system is almost entirely individual: each character has their own scores, their own allied force, their own benefits. It does not create party bonds. However, a patron relationship creates hooks that can pull a party together: the patron makes demands that require the whole party's help; the patron's Intercession saves the party as well as the sorcerer; a rival patron's agents threaten everyone. These are GM-side tools rather than mechanical party-binding, but they serve the recurring-patron dynamic described in design.md §4.5 (a patron who keeps coming back with new jobs). Net: not a party-cohesion mechanic per se, but a recurring-NPC-patron tool with party-level story implications.

### Mook rules

**No match.** Allegiance has no minion or mook mechanics.

### Sorcery cost

**Direct match - this is the key finding.** Allegiance, stripped and modified into a Patron Score, is a viable implementation of Option E (debt-to-power) from `source-1-sorcery.md`'s sorcery cost analysis. Better: it can serve as the "second track" in Option F (hybrid) without requiring bespoke design. The chassis already exists in BRP UGE; it just needs reskinning. The benefits (emergency PP reserve, temporary HP) are directly complementary to the PP-as-primary-cost structure already locked in for sorcery. This resolves the "patron debt is entirely GM-dependent" weakness of Option E by giving the player a visible score they can see accumulating. Per design.md §2.2 and §3.3 (sorcery cost mechanic deferred to magic chapter - here is the candidate).

## Book Placement

**GM Book primary; Player Book summary.**

- **GM Book** contains: the full Patron Score system - creating a patron (trigger actions, offenses, what the patron wants, its nature and true name), the Patron Score test procedure, the Benefits of Patron table (PP reserve, temporary HP, Patron Intercession), the maintenance/penance mechanic, rival patron rules, and the Apotheosis optional arc.

- **Player Book** contains: a summary of what it means to have a patron compact, the fact that a Patron Score exists and accrues, the two player-facing benefits (PP reserve and temporary HP), and a clear statement that high scores attract patron attention (with consequences adjudicated by the GM). The sorcerer should know their score. They should not need the GM Book to understand what it means at the table.

**Reasoning:** The patron relationship is initiated and maintained through GM-defined parameters (what the specific entity values, what it demands, when it intercedes). The player-facing mechanics are simple enough to put in the Player Book; the GM-facing machinery for creating and running a patron over a campaign belongs in the GM Book alongside the demon summoning sub-system. Per design.md §3.3 (Player Book / GM Book division) and §3.1 (GM-facing rules stay lean so sessions run without bookkeeping overhead).
