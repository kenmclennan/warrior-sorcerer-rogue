# Source Notes: Chapter 7 - Spot Rules (BRP UGE pp. 161-182)

**Section:** Chapter 7 / Spot Rules
**Pages:** 161-182
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Spot Rules agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

Chapter 7 is a large alphabetical grab-bag of 60+ situational rules covering combat modifiers, environmental hazards, and special maneuver types. The chapter is explicitly GM-facing: the opening line says "these 'spot rules' are intended for the gamemaster's use." For WSR the chapter divides cleanly into four tiers:

**Strong canonise (genre essentials):** Acid, Ambushes, Armor Damaged, Backstabs and Helpless Opponents, Big and Little Targets, Both Sides Surprised, Broken Weapons, Chases (foot/mounted only), Choking/Drowning/Asphyxiation, Close Combat, Darkness, Desperate Action (modified - see discussion below), Disease, Disarming, Disengaging, Drawing/Exchanging a Weapon, Enclosed Environments, Entangling (spot-rule entry), Falling, Fighting While Down, Fire and Heat, Fortified Positions, Improvised Parrying Weapons, Improvised Weapons, Knockout Attacks, Mass Combat (abstracted version), Mounted Combat, Natural Weapons, Poisons, Prone, Pulling Blows, Quickdraws, Shields and Missile Fire, Slippery or Unstable Surfaces, Stunning or Subduing, Superior/Inferior Positions, Superior Numbers, Sweep Attacks, Thrown Weapons, Two Weapons, Underwater Combat, Weapon Length, Weather Conditions (Wind and Rain), Aimed Attacks, Cover, Firing into Combat.

**Modify (keep concept, strip distance/modern elements):** Chases (range track), Explosions, Extended Range, Knockback, Point-blank Range, Light Sources, Weapon Length distances, Closing, Shooting Up or Down, Missile Fire While Moving.

**Drop (genre-wrong or system-alien):** Aerial Combat, Autofire, Radiation, Vacuum, Zero-gravity Combat, Vehicular Chases (full sub-system), Aura Attacks, Volley Fire (as a formal sub-system).

**Defer:** Mass Combat (the full opposed-POT resistance option), Charging rules within Mounted Combat.

The chapter is rich with material that directly serves WSR's hostile-wilderness and brutal-combat aesthetic (design.md §2.3, §2.4). The environmental hazards (fire, drowning, disease, poison, falling, exposure) are all genre-essential: Conan dies of exposure just as surely as he dies of a sword thrust.

Distance quantification is the main editorial problem. Roughly 12-15 entries contain explicit metre measurements that need conversion to narrative categories per design.md §3.2. Most convert cleanly; the Chases range track is the hardest case.

**Desperate Action vs. Dying Blows:** These are complementary, not duplicates. See the dedicated discussion below.

## Canonise

The following are taken as written (or near-verbatim with minor aesthetic trimming for ancient-world tone):

**Acid.** Three tiers (weak 1D3-1, strong 1D4, very strong 1D6+1 per round). Armor degraded over 1D3 rounds then bypassed. Genre-useful for ancient alchemical hazards, monster secretions, cursed substances. Canonise. Note: "holy water as acid vs. undead" clause is kept as a GM note - it fits the genre's folk religion flavor.

**Ambushes.** Four-tier system based on visibility and detection. Free Easy attacks for unseen ambushers; aware targets can still parry/dodge. Clean, theatre-of-mind compatible, no distance dependency. Essential for the genre's dungeon and wilderness encounter structure. Canonise in full.

**Aimed Attacks (Taking More Time and Targeting a Specific Area).** Both sub-rules. Taking More Time gives +1/10 skill per 5 DEX ranks of delay, capped at base range. Targeting a specific area is Difficult, offset by time spent. Both are player-facing tactical choices that add depth without complexity per design.md §3.1. Canonise.

**Armor Damaged.** Acid, fire, critical hits, and fumbles can reduce AV by half. Armor destroyed twice becomes useless. Repair costs half the armor's normal value. Essential for the genre (the rogue's leather gets set on fire; the warrior's breastplate takes a critical hit). Canonise. Drop the example referencing random AV (already dropped in combat chapter).

**Backstabs and Helpless Opponents.** Attacking a target's unprotected back is Easy; target's defenses are Difficult (if they detect it with a Difficult Listen/Sense roll). Helpless targets (unconscious, asleep, restrained) cannot parry or dodge - attack is Easy. POW x1 Luck roll for lucky incident staying attacker's hand. All genre-essential for assassination and captivity scenarios. Canonise.

**Big and Little Targets.** Target more than twice attacker's SIZ: +20% to skill. Target less than half attacker's SIZ (round up): -20% to skill. Inanimate large objects Easy. Tiny objects Difficult. Clean size-based modifier with no distance dependency. Canonise.

**Both Sides Surprised.** Opposed Spot or Listen roll to detect first; Idea roll for who acts first in a tie; DEX rank breaks further ties. Entirely theatre-of-mind compatible. Canonise.

**Broken Weapons.** Weapon/shield breaks if it parries damage reducing its HP to 0. Broken weapon attacks/parries Difficult (or treated as similar smaller weapon if applicable). Canonise; weapon breakage is a staple of pulp swordplay.

**Chases (core structure).** The six-aspect pre-chase framework (Starting Positions, Course, Skills Required, Combat, Duration, Conclusion) is excellent GM scaffolding and entirely narrative in character. Canonise this framing structure. Chases on foot using CON vs. CON resistance rolls, opposed Agility, Climb, Jump, or Swim rolls are entirely non-distance-dependent in principle. Canonise the foot/mounted chase resolution mechanic (see Modify for the range track).

**Choking, Drowning, and Asphyxiation.** CON x10 (Easy Stamina) first round, then CON x5, reducing by x1 each subsequent round until failure kills. Deliberate choking: CON x1 each round or 1D6 damage to total HP. Surprise asphyxiation: Luck roll to have taken a breath first. Water inflicts 1D8 HP per round; smoke 1D4; dense smoke 1D6. Genre-critical for underwater encounters, smoke-filled ruins, strangulation assassination attempts. Canonise in full. Note: interaction with sorcery - a sorcerer being strangled faces the same escalating CON rolls as anyone else; if PP are being used to cast spells this further limits their options for surviving.

**Close Combat.** Short weapon vs. long weapon distance dynamics: long weapon attacks first regardless of DEX until short-weapon user closes; once closed, long-weapon parries Difficult; long-weapon user can keep short user at bay with a skill roll sacrifice. Essential for ancient-world weapon variety and theatre-of-mind spatial logic. Canonise. (Metre reference "roughly one meter of free space" is kept as SIZ/ENC measurement for weapon sizing, not for distance tracking - this is weapon geometry, not grid positioning.)

**Closing.** The mechanics for closing with a long-weapon user (attacker closes at normal DEX once the initial strike is made; both limited to one action per DEX rank while in close). Canonise as a sub-section of Close Combat.

**Cold, Exposure, Hunger, and Thirst.** Prolonged exposure modifies fatigue loss (x2-4 multiplier). Without food/water: 3 days before difficulty; Stamina roll required after CON/2 days. Actions become Difficult after CON days without supplies; wounds heal half-speed; SIZ may waste from starvation. These are the hostile-wilderness rules that make the world feel dangerous between fights (design.md §2.4). Canonise. Note: "Powers such as Adaptation may negate effects" - strip this clause; WSR has no Adaptation power.

**Cover.** Partial cover makes attacks Difficult; if roll falls between modified and unmodified skill, cover is struck instead of target. Damage may pass through cover if it exceeds cover's AV. Entirely narrative-compatible, no distance dependency. Canonise.

**Darkness.** Fighting in darkness: see Situational Modifiers. Opponent detection in complete darkness requires Difficult Sense or Listen roll; success halves darkness modifier. Light source in hand offsets penalties (cross-reference Light Sources). Canonise.

**Disease.** Stamina roll to resist contracting disease; daily CON escalating rolls (CON x2, then CON x3, etc.) to recover; fumble reduces multiplier by 1. Illness Severity Table: 0 failures = none; 1 = mild (1 characteristic point/week); 2 = acute (1/day); 3 = severe (1/hour); 4+ = terminal (1/minute). Six generic disease types (Atrophy, Chills, Delirium, Malaise, Shakes, Pox) attacking different characteristics. Strenuous conditions reduce recovery roll by x1 per outstanding condition (wilderness = CON x3 instead of CON x5; combat conditions = CON x2 or CON x1). This is an excellent, brutal disease system. The lethality is correct for the genre. Canonise in full. The six disease types are a useful GM tool; encourage GMs to rename them for the setting. Note: "See Chapter 4: Powers and Chapter 8: Equipment for details" - strip cross-references to Powers (Adaptation); keep Medicine skill reference.

**Disarming.** Two methods: (1) Grapple to wrest weapon free; (2) Announce intent at round start, make Difficult attack vs. weapon, damage vs. weapon HP, and resistance roll vs. target's STR. Successful dodge negates; parry of any sort also negates. Genre-appropriate alternative to killing (capturing, humiliating). Canonise.

**Disengaging.** Three methods: (1) Succeed at knockback then Dodge; (2) Fight withdrawal - all dodges/parries, if all succeed then disengage; (3) Turn and run - leaves character open to an immediate Difficult attack that cannot be parried or dodged. All three are distinct tactical choices with meaningful costs. Canonise. (This is the spot-rule entry; the combat chapter also covers Disengage as an action.)

**Drawing or Exchanging a Weapon.** Drawing takes 5 DEX ranks; dropping one and drawing another takes 5 DEX ranks; putting one away and drawing costs 10 DEX ranks. Characters with 101%+ skill draw/sheathe at 0 DEX rank cost. Canonise; these details give the exchange of weapons mid-fight tactical texture without complexity.

**Enclosed Environments.** Characters need ~1 metre radius of free space to fight comfortably; SIZ/ENC 1.5+ weapons Difficult in tight spaces; SIZ/ENC 2.5+ Impossible; SIZ/ENC 0 weapons (daggers, fists) unpenalized; ranged SIZ/ENC 1 Difficult. This is the rule that makes dungeons, caves, and tight corridors tactically distinct from open ground. Canonise. The "one metre" here is weapon geometry (ENC rating), not grid movement - no conflict with §3.2.

**Entangling (spot-rule entry).** Cross-references the combat chapter special effect. Adds: any attacker with a suitable weapon can declare entangle intent; attempting entangle is Difficult; success = target entangled as per special success, no damage. Canonise as a pointer to the combat chapter entangle rules.

**Falling.** 1D6 per 3 metres fallen (see Modify for metre handling). SIZ 5 or less reduces damage by 1D6; SIZ 20+ adds 1D6 (and another 1D6 per fraction of 20 above that). Armor provides half protection up to 3 metres. Stakes and pointed objects add separate damage (1D10+1). Jump roll to choose hit location on landing. Canonise the damage structure, the SIZ modifiers, and the stake complication - these are all genre-vivid. Modify the distance expression (see below).

**Fighting While Down.** Prone character: attacks, parries, Dodges all Difficult; attacks against prone are Easy. Getting up without action is possible but costs the attack slot, makes parries/Dodges Difficult, and leaves character open to Easy attacks. Difficult Dodge roll gets character upright on their DEX rank and allows attack action. Dodge + Agility (unmodified) avoids the Easy modifier but takes the attack slot. Cross-reference Superior/Inferior Positions. Canonise.

**Fire and Heat.** Five tiers: tiny flame (1/round); torch held against target (1D6/round, Luck roll to avoid catching fire); large bonfire (1D6+2/round, POW x1 to avoid catching fire); fiery building (2D6/round plus asphyxiation); intense fire/molten metal (3D6/round, character automatically set ablaze for 1D6/round ongoing). Armor protects for 1D3 rounds before losing effectiveness. Burning character takes 1D4 characteristic point loss on failing a Luck roll when burn damage exceeds HP/4. Fire degrades non-metal armor at 1 point per round. This entire section is genre-essential. Fire is a staple peril in dungeons, ambushes, burning ships. Canonise in full. Strip "firearms ammunition" clause (no firearms in WSR).

**Firing into Combat.** Shooting into a melee is -20%. Shooting while engaged is Difficult. At close combat range, it becomes Easy (point-blank). If attack misses but falls between modified and unmodified skill, GM randomly determines which combatant is struck. Massed fire modifier: +5% per extra body shot at standing, closely grouped targets. Canonise; essential for any combat involving archers and mixed melee.

**Fortified Positions.** Attacks from behind fortified cover (walls, battlements, doorways) are Difficult. Cannot be overcome by missile fire below siege-weapon scale; area attacks and explosions can still threaten. Canonise; relevant to any siege, ambush from walls, door-fighting, or combat across a barricade.

**Improvised Parrying Weapons.** Missile weapons can be used to parry at half base chance (minimum 20%). Improvised parrying weapon takes 1 HP damage per successful parry; always suffers at least 1 HP damage when used. Canonise; the warrior with his broken shield using a spear shaft to parry is a staple image.

**Improvised Weapons.** Characters can improvise weapons from environment. Weapon tables in Equipment provide statistics. Canonise as a principle; the specific statistics cross-reference Equipment chapter.

**Knockout Attacks.** Declare at round start; Difficult attack to head (or equivalent). Damage determines knockout potential: minor wound = minimum weapon damage dealt; major wound equivalent = 1 damage, target knocked out 1D10+10 rounds (secretly rolled). Special/critical effects (extra damage, bypass armor) apply; slashing/knockback special effects don't. Armor defends normally. Canonise; essential for the genre's "capture alive" and "knock the guard out quietly" scenarios.

**Light Sources.** Candle: visible 100m in darkness; requires Spot roll to see. Torch: large flame, hard to blow out, burns ~1 hour. Oil lamp: several hours of light. Dropped flaming source: Luck roll or ignites. Canonise. Strip distance value of 100m (see Modify).

**Mass Combat (narrative abstraction only).** The source offers three options: opposed Strategy rolls, resistance rolls with POT values, or a fully symbolic abstract combat. The narrative abstraction is the relevant one for WSR: GM has decided the outcome; PCs can participate at appropriate scale; every involved PC makes a Luck roll each turn (5 minutes), failure = 1D6 damage, fumble = 2D6 damage, armor reduces. Canonise this abstracted version as the Mass Combat spot rule. Defer the full POT-value resistance option (see Defer).

**Missile Fire While Moving.** Mounted/vehicle fire: combat skill limited to vehicle/mount skill. Running fire: limited to Agility. Mid-leap throwing: Jump skill as cap. Canonise; relevant for mounted archers (a genuine ancient-world archetype) and thrown weapons on the move.

**Mounted Combat.** Full set of modifiers: combat skills capped by Ride skill; some weapons (two-handed polearms, large shields) unusable; Dodges Difficult while mounted; mounted character uses mount's MOV; mounted character has superior position vs. ground targets; knockback or major wound risks dismounting; falling from mount = Luck or 1D6 + 1D3/round dragging damage; fumbled/dropped weapon requires dismount to retrieve; charge attacks use mount's damage modifier. Set weapon vs. charging mount (lance/spear/pike). Canonise the full system. This is genre-essential (Conan fights from horseback; cavalry charges are a staple of ancient-world combat). The metre reference in Superior Position ("standing higher by one meter or more") is the only distance reference - see Modify.

**Natural Weapons.** Fists, feet, claws, fangs, tails, stings, horns. Human natural weapons: same mechanics as regular weapons. Creature natural weapons: 1D6 claws, 1D3 bite/horn plus damage modifier (per Creatures chapter). Wild animals generally don't parry. Canonise; relevant for unarmed combat scenes, monsters, and animal companions.

**Poisons.** POT vs. CON resistance roll. Overcomes CON: full POT as HP damage. Partial: half POT (round up). Delay: 3 combat rounds (fast) or 3 full turns (slow). Two doses of same POT don't stack as a double-POT dose. Antidotes: POT vs. POT; must be administered within 6 turns to fully subtract from poison's POT. Genre-essential. Canonise in full. The system is simple, brutal, and requires no additional subsystems.

**Prone.** Full condition rules: hand-to-hand/medium missile attacks against prone are Easy; long-range missile attacks Difficult; prone character's attacks/parries/Dodges are Difficult; two-handed weapon attacks by prone character blocked (though they can use missile weapons at full skill if appropriate); no positive damage modifier while prone. Getting up: takes attack slot, leaves parries/Dodges Difficult, incoming attacks Easy; Difficult Dodge roll recovers on DEX rank with attack allowed; Dodge + Agility recovers without Easy-attack vulnerability but consumes attack slot. Canonise.

**Pulling Blows.** Declare at round start: character adds less or none of their damage modifier, may use smaller dice, to minimum 1D2 + weapon's die bonus. Cannot split dice reductions across different dice. Genre-essential for subduing, demonstrating, warning, or fighting in non-lethal contexts. Canonise.

**Quickdraws.** Both participants hold action until counted down. First to act: other makes Easy Spot roll to react; if they do, winner determined by DEX vs. DEX resistance roll; winner attacks first and target cannot Dodge or Parry. Subsequent attacks in quick-draw are Difficult to Dodge/Parry. Canonise; essential for knife-fight and challenge duel scenes.

**Shields and Missile Fire.** Half/small shield 15% chance to block missile; full shield 30%; large shield 60%. Kneeling behind full shield: 60% chance; large shield: 90%. Special/critical successes pass through shield. Canonise as a reference supplement to the combat chapter's shield rules.

**Slippery or Unstable Surfaces.** Agility roll to avoid slipping; Difficult if especially slick. Failure: prone or unable to act except to stay upright. Fumble: 1D3 damage. Canonise; genre-appropriate for fighting on wet docks, muddy battlefields, ice-covered ruins, unstable scaffolding.

**Stunning or Subduing.** Four methods: crushing special (combat chapter); Knockout Attack (above); stun weapon (make normal targeted attack, roll damage, subtract armor, resistance roll vs. current HP - success = stunned for rolled damage in rounds; critical = double max rolled; special = max rolled; failure = no stun, minimum weapon damage dealt; 0-1 HP remaining = knocked out or possibly dying from concussion). Grapple/entangle to immobilize. Stunned character: cannot attack; Dodge/Parry require successful Idea roll each attempt; all attacks against stunned target are Easy. Armor protects normally; ancient/medieval armor unaffected by electricity (strip electronics reference). Canonise. Strip "energy weapons and advanced armor" clause.

**Superior/Inferior Positions.** Standing half-again higher than target = superior position: +10% to attacks and parries; +1 to hit location D20 roll. No penalty for inferior position (the advantage given to superior is penalty enough). Superior position bonus negated if inferior has longer weapon and superior does not. Canonise. The "one meter or more" height reference is physical reality, not grid tracking - it is a narrative description of the battlefield situation (standing on the stairs, the raised platform, the boulder).

**Superior Numbers.** No more than 5 characters (human-sized, hand-to-hand) can surround and all attack a single target simultaneously; large weapons reduce this; long spears might allow 8 attackers. GM evaluates terrain and weapon types to set the limit. Remaining attackers crowded out. Canonise; this is the rule that prevents the "infinite pile-on" problem and is directly relevant to mook swarms vs. a heroic PC.

**Sweep Attacks.** Giant or massive-weapon attacker can sweep a horizontal arc, potentially striking multiple foes. Attack is Easy but damage halved; must use appropriate weapon. Target: Jump to avoid (cannot Dodge); Parry requires STR resistance roll vs. half damage. Non-edged sweep causes knockback. Can cause crushing, knockback, or slashing specials only (not impale or entangle). Canonise; relevant for large monsters and two-handed weapons used in desperation.

**Thrown Weapons.** Throwing a melee weapon not designed for throwing: damage modifier halved. Throwing a designed throwing weapon (hatchet, javelin) at full modifier. Throwing into close combat = forced into hand-to-hand range Difficult attacks. Canonise; genre-appropriate for the scene where the warrior's sword is gone and they hurl a dagger.

**Two Weapons.** Two hand-to-hand or small missile weapons simultaneously. Does not grant extra attacks automatically; two-weapon fighter with 100%+ in each weapon can split attacks (both attacks Difficult, second weapon attacks ignored in that splitting). Second attack at DEX rank -5. Secondary hand attacks Difficult if not traditionally off-hand (except at 100%+ skill). Primary advantage: no defenseless moment if one weapon is broken or lost. Ambidexterity by DEX 16+ or Special Luck roll. Canonise.

**Underwater Combat.** Swim roll each turn to maneuver into position for attack (not required if standing on surface). Weapons doing anything other than impaling damage: half damage (round up). Being underwater = additional 3 AV against non-impaling weapons. All attacks/parries/Dodges limited to Swim skill (unless close combat, grappling, or using SIZ 0-1 piercing weapon). Shield parrying: Impossible. Double fatigue point cost per round. Canonise; genre-relevant for ship fights, river ambushes, and dungeon flood encounters.

**Weapon Length.** Three effective distances - close (arm's reach), medium (~1-2 metres), long (2+ metres). Larger SIZ/ENC = attacks first at each distance. Long weapon attacks before medium or short regardless of DEX; long can hold off short-weapon user (see Close Combat). Some weapons (quarterstaffs) usable at all distances. Canonise; this is the non-grid spatial logic of ancient-world weapon choice.

**Weather Conditions (Wind and Rain).** Wind: Light-Moderate (STR 0-1) = no modifier; Strong Wind (STR 2-6) = -10%; Severe Wind (STR 7-9) = -25%; Windstorm (STR 10-12) = -50%; Hurricane (STR 13-15) = -75%; Tornado (STR 16+) = -90%. Rain: -10%; Snow: -25%; Sleet: -50%; Hail: -75%. Wind also requires STR vs. STR resistance roll each round to stay upright. Failure = falls (see Falling). Cloud cover: modifies Navigation, Knowledge (Astronomy), and darkness-sensitive situations. Canonise; ancient-world adventuring happens in all weathers and storm-fighting is a genre staple.

**Aimed Attacks.** Already noted above in canonise.

**Shooting Up or Down.** Firing at angle >45 degrees: Difficult; effective and maximum ranges halved for directly overhead. Shooting up: -10% to skill. Shooting significantly down: +10% to skill. Canonise; terrain and multi-level dungeon relevant.

## Drop

The following are dropped outright. Genre-incompatible, technology-dependent, or entirely science-fiction in context.

**Aerial Combat.** Fly skill modifiers for combat, stunt penalties, weather effects on flying. WSR has no Fly skill and no flying PC option (design.md §1.3 - no superhero abilities). Flying creatures (dragons, demons) are handled as GM-side entities in the Creatures chapter; they don't need player-facing aerial combat rules. The weather modifiers for flight are covered by Weather Conditions for characters on the ground. **Drop entirely.**

**Autofire.** Multiple-shot bursts from automatic weapons. WSR has no firearms. Drop.

**Radiation.** Radiation Effects Table with cancer, sterility, terminal effects. No radioactive materials exist in WSR's ancient-world setting. **Drop entirely.**

**Vacuum.** Explosive decompression, hemorrhaging from vacuum exposure, eyeburst damage from CON x2 rounds. WSR has no space travel, no vacuum environments. **Drop entirely.**

**Zero-gravity Combat.** Agility rolls each round, knockback applies to attacker and defender equally. No zero-gravity environments in WSR. **Drop entirely.**

**Aura Attacks.** Character averages SIZ, POW, and CHA for an "aura total"; target must succeed at POW vs. aura total resistance roll or be mesmerized. This is a superhero/epic mechanic explicitly described as "invaluable resource in epic or superhero games." WSR's protagonists are exceptional but mortal (design.md §1.2 feature 5) and do not overawe opponents through sheer presence as a combat action. The Command skill, Passions, and Reputation mechanics handle social dominance without a separate combat-phase aura mechanic. **Drop entirely.**

**Volley Fire (as a formal sub-system).** The volley fire rules for rapidly firing thrown/launched weapons (attack at normal DEX, subsequent attacks at -5 DEX rank intervals, all Difficult, cannot parry or dodge while volleying) are an abstracted wargame mechanic for representing rapid arrow fire. The Combat chapter's Attacks per Round statistic already handles a character's maximum attack rate. Volley fire as a special sub-system is an add-on for scenarios where arrow suppression or rapid-fire is the tactical goal - this has no genre precedent in sword & sorcery pulp (Conan's archery isn't modeled this way). **Drop.** Characters use the standard attack rate from weapon statistics.

**Vehicular Chases (full sub-system).** Rated Speed, Range, Accelerate/Decelerate, Handling, the full Maneuvers section, the Skill Roll Modifier Chart (vehicle conditions), the Chase Trouble Table, Individual Actions aboard vehicles, and all of the vehicle-specific terms. WSR has no vehicles in the modern sense. Mounted chases are covered under Mounted Combat and Chases on Foot (the range track concept is modified for foot and mounted use - see Modify). **Drop the vehicular sub-system entirely.**

**Extended Range (as written).** The rule defines medium range as double base range, long range as four times base range, with DEX/3 in metres for point-blank. The metre references are grid-dependent. **Drop as written.** A modified version of the range-band concept is kept in Modify below.

## Modify

**Chases (range track concept).** The source's range track is a physical prop requiring miniatures or printed handouts ("copy or print this out and place it on the table"). The five positions (Side-by-Side, Two Lengths, Close, In Sight, Out of Sight) are, however, a clean abstract structure for chase resolution. Modify: strip the physical track and all "squares" language. Replace with five narrative positions on the same model, adjudicated verbally. Chase resolution rolls (CON vs. CON, opposed Agility, Climb, Jump, or Swim) determine position shifts each round. The attack-possibility rules at each range (hand-to-hand possible / missile attacks Difficult / missile attacks at 1/5 skill / only a roll of 01 hits / no chance to hit) translate cleanly to the narrative positions. Per design.md §3.2.

**Explosions (concept kept, metre values dropped).** Explosion damage decreases from epicenter outward - reduce by 1D6 per "significant distance" from the blast (GM arbitrates based on fiction: across the room vs. in the doorway vs. outside the building). Demolition skill adjusts charge strength (+/-50%). The Demolition skill cross-reference is kept for use by characters with that skill (ancient-world: black powder equivalents, alchemical charges, siege engines, mine-shaft collapses). Strip all "2 meters per D6" and "10-meter radius" specific language. Note: this is less relevant in WSR (no firearms, no modern explosives) but siege engines, alchemical fire-bombs, and sorcerous blasts may use similar rules. Retain as a GM tool with narrative distance assessment.

**Falling (metre values stripped).** The damage rate of 1D6 per 3 metres is the core mechanic; convert to narrative tiers: Short Fall (from horse height, a story drop) = 1D6; Significant Fall (two stories, a cliff edge) = 2D6-3D6; Long Fall (tall structure, high cliff) = 4D6-6D6; Extreme Fall = 7D6+. GM calibrates based on the fiction. The SIZ modifier remains (SIZ 5 or less: -1D6; SIZ 20+: +1D6 per 20 above that). Armor half-protection for the first tier. Stake damage (1D10+1) added to the fall damage, separate roll. Per design.md §3.2.

**Extended Range (range bands without metres).** Replace the metre-specific definitions with: Near (within easy reach, missile attack is Easy), Normal (standard range, skill as rated), Distant (challenging range, skill is Difficult), Extreme (edge of capability, 1/5 skill, any success treated as a normal success). Point-blank (arm's reach): missile attacks Easy; close-combat rules apply; bows become Difficult or Impossible depending on weapon. These narrative bands capture the rule's intent without requiring range measurement. Per design.md §3.2.

**Point-blank Range (metre values stripped).** The rule as written uses DEX/3 in metres as the threshold for point-blank. Replace: a target is at point-blank range when they are within arm's reach - touching or nearly touching. The DEX/3 threshold can be abstracted to "the GM judges whether the target is close enough to touch" - typically this is the same as Close Combat range. Bows and thrown weapons become Difficult or Impossible at point-blank (contextual). Per design.md §3.2.

**Light Sources (distance stripped).** "Visible from 100 metres in darkness" - drop the specific distance. Replace: a torch or lantern is visible from a great distance in total darkness; its light illuminates the immediate area around the bearer. A candle or small lamp illuminates only the very close surroundings. GM judges how far light reaches based on environment (open plain vs. enclosed corridor). The functional rules (Luck roll if dropped, illumination allows use of Search/Spot skills, oil lamp burns for several hours) are unchanged. Per design.md §3.2.

**Knockback (metre values stripped).** Already modified in the combat chapter: "1m per 5 damage rolled" becomes "knocked significantly backward / slammed back / tumbled to ground" based on the total damage. The Agility roll to remain standing and the prone result are unchanged. Per design.md §3.2.

**Mounted Combat (superior position metre reference).** "Standing higher than an opponent by one meter or more" - drop the metre specification. Replace: a mounted character has a superior position over any character on foot (automatically, unless fighting uphill terrain reverses this). The one-metre reference is implicit in the height difference between a mounted and unmounted combatant. The +10% bonus and +1 hit location modifier are unchanged. Per design.md §3.2.

**Shooting Up or Down.** "More than 45 degrees" is a reasonable narrative judgment call, not a measurement. Keep the rule as written; the GM determines whether fire is at a steep enough angle to trigger the Difficult modifier and halved range. No distance values to strip. Minor modification: explicitly note that 45 degrees is a GM judgment ("is the target significantly above or below you?"), not a protractor measurement.

**Desperate Action.** See discussion below - modified, not dropped.

## Desperate Action - Discussion

The source rule (p. 169): if a character has used all their actions in a combat round and absolutely must make one final action, they may attempt a **desperate action** on the last DEX rank of the round. Prerequisites: (1) must make a successful Difficult Agility roll to enable the action; (2) if enabled, the action is always Difficult regardless of what it is; (3) the following round, the character cannot attack or move (they are "unbalanced"); (4) Dodges and Parries are unaffected; (5) if fatigue points are used, the action costs double the normal fatigue.

The source's combat chapter (source-5-combat.md) notes that Dying Blows "replaces the Desperate Action spot rule." On careful reading, this is not quite right - they are related but distinct.

**Dying Blows** (from Combat chapter, p. - canonised there): a *mortally wounded* character (fatal wound threshold) may attempt one final action, requiring a Stamina roll. The character cannot attack or move after.

**Desperate Action** (this spot rule): a character who has *exhausted their action economy in a round* but needs to act again. This is not about dying - it is about a character who has already attacked, moved, cast a spell, and has no more DEX ranks but desperately needs to do one more thing.

**Assessment:** These are complementary, not duplicates. Dying Blows covers "I am dying but I get one last act." Desperate Action covers "I have spent all my actions but I need to do one more thing right now." Both can apply to the same character simultaneously (a dying character who has already used their last desperate action). They are not the same rule, and the combat chapter's claim that Dying Blows "replaces" Desperate Action appears to be a shorthand for "we are not keeping Desperate Action as a separate spot rule because Dying Blows is more genre-appropriate for the key use case."

**WSR Decision:** Keep Desperate Action as a modifier to Dying Blows. The standard Dying Blows rule covers the primary genre case (the mortally wounded hero's last strike). Desperate Action's mechanic (Difficult Agility to enable; action is Difficult; movement restricted next round) is folded into the Dying Blows rule as additional detail: if the character has already spent their actions (and is making a Dying Blow on their last DEX rank), the action is Difficult, and no movement is possible after. This avoids a separate spot rule while retaining the mechanical nuance. Strip Desperate Action as a standalone entry. Cross-reference in Dying Blows.

## Defer

**Mass Combat (POT-resistance option).** The source offers a resistance-roll model where each side has a POT value (assigned by GM) and they make opposing resistance rolls to represent the ebb and flow of a large battle. This is a lightweight wargame abstraction that could serve WSR's "the PCs are part of a larger battle" scenario type. However, it requires calibrating POT values for armies, which is a design task rather than a simple extraction. Defer to GM Book design phase.

**Mounted Combat - Charge rules.** The charge attack mechanics (full movement then attack using mount's damage modifier instead of rider's; requires Ride roll to maneuver; set weapon defense against charges) are canonised in principle but the specific interaction with the narrative distance system (how long does a charge take? what is "full movement" in theatre-of-mind?) needs design work. Defer the specific charge resolution to the mount/movement design phase.

**Sorcery and environmental hazards.** Multiple hazards in this chapter (drowning/choking, fire, disease) could interact with sorcery in specific ways:
- A sorcerer drowning still faces the escalating CON rolls; if they are spending PP on spells (to escape, to affect their captor), those PP are unavailable for Fate Point use. This is the PP-pool asymmetry decision (decisions.md 2026-05-09) playing out in a specific context.
- Casting a spell while on fire (3D6/round) creates pressure on the PP/Fate Point decision: spend PP to escape (spell) or hold them against potential killing blow (Fate Point).
- Disease attacking POW (the Malaise type) reduces a sorcerer's PP pool, which is POW-derived. A sorcerer weakened by Malaise disease has fewer PP to cast spells and to absorb killing blows.
These interactions are genre-appropriate and require no additional rules; they emerge from the existing mechanics. Flag for the magic chapter notes to ensure the interaction is explicitly described.

**Superior Numbers as a mook tool.** The Superior Numbers rule (maximum 5 human-sized attackers surrounding a single target) is the closest thing in the source to a mook-management tool. It prevents the infinite pile-on and creates a natural limit on how many mooks can attack a PC simultaneously. However, it does not speed up resolution - each of the five attackers still resolves through the full Attack and Defense Matrix. This rule is useful context for any mook-handling design: the physical limit is already established; the bookkeeping reduction still needs design. Flag for the mook design task (noted in source-5-combat.md as the primary gap).

## Open Questions

1. **Chases and the range track without a prop.** The source's foot chase relies on the range track as a shared visual reference. Without a physical prop, the GM must track five positions mentally or verbally. Is a simple verbal notation sufficient ("you are now Two Lengths behind them")? Or should WSR provide a minimal alternative (a printed reference card)? This is a presentation design question, not a rules question.

2. **Explosions and ancient-world applicability.** The Explosions rule exists primarily for grenades, dynamite, and modern munitions. Ancient-world analogues (Greek fire, alchemical bombs, sorcerous fireballs, catapult stones, mine collapse) could all use a similar radius-damage mechanic. However, catapult stones and sorcerous blasts have different damage profiles. Should WSR provide specific ancient-world variants (e.g., a "Greek fire" entry, a "sorcerous blast" entry) or simply preserve the general Explosions rule for GM adaptation? Defer to GM Book scenario design.

3. **Disease and characteristic recovery.** The Disease rules reduce characteristics (STR for Atrophy, CON for Chills, etc.) but the source does not specify whether recovered characteristics return on the same schedule as the illness progressed. Design.md §1.2 feature 7 ("naturalistic healing, slow") suggests recovery should be slow - but how slow? Does the Conditions of Medical Care table from the Combat chapter apply to disease recovery? This needs a brief design note in the Disease section.

4. **Aura Attacks and the Command/Passions overlap.** Having dropped Aura Attacks, the social/intimidation space is covered by Command skill, Passions (design.md §3.1, canonised from Settings chapter), and Reputation. Is there a gap - specifically, the "overwhelming presence" moment that Aura Attacks simulated? The existing BRP mechanics for Command (a standard skill roll) may be sufficient. Worth checking when the Passions/Reputation chapter notes are assembled (source-4-passions-reputation.md).

5. **Quickdraws and the sorcerer.** A sorcerer in a quickdraw situation faces a specific tension: their first available action slot is in the Powers phase (before Action), where they might want to cast a spell. The Quickdraw rule has participants hold action in the Action phase. Does a sorcerer lose the option to cast in the Powers phase if participating in a quickdraw? This is a combat-sorcery interaction question; flag for the magic chapter.

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

**Moderate match.** This chapter adds distinctiveness through hazard interactions: a Warrior with high CON resists disease and drowning longer; a Rogue with high DEX acts first in quickdraws and recovers from prone more effectively; a Sorcerer with high POW has more PP to spend on Fate Points when fire or poison threaten. The hazard rules create implicit character-type advantages without requiring a formal distinctiveness system. The Mounted Combat rules reward Ride skill investment, creating a cavalry/horseback-fighter sub-archetype. The Two Weapons rules reward DEX 16+ and high skill percentages. No designed distinctiveness mechanic is present - it all emerges from existing stats.

### Party cohesion

**Weak match.** No cooperative mechanics in this chapter. One implicit cohesion element: Superior Numbers benefits the party when they attack an individual enemy, since the limit of 5 simultaneous attackers means a party of 4 PCs can all attack the same named villain simultaneously without crowding each other. This is positive for party action but provides no mechanical reward for coordinated action. The Mass Combat abstraction (Luck roll each turn) applies equally to all PCs present, with no coordination bonus.

### Mook rules

**No match.** Superior Numbers confirms the physical limit (5 attackers maximum vs. a single target) but does not reduce resolution complexity. Disease and poison apply symmetrically to mooks and PCs. No streamlining mechanic exists. The gap noted in source-5-combat.md remains unresolved by this chapter.

### Sorcery cost

**Partial match - implicit interactions.** The PP-pool asymmetry (decisions.md 2026-05-09) plays out visibly in this chapter's hazards. A sorcerer drowning who spends PP on an escape spell is sacrificing both Fate Point reserves and possibly survival. Fire, disease (Malaise type reduces POW), and drowning all create pressure on the sorcerer's PP pool in ways that the Warrior and Rogue do not face in the same way (their Fate Point reserve is not being drained by casting). This asymmetry is genre-coherent (the sorcerer's power is a liability as much as an asset) and requires no additional rules - it is a consequence of the shared PP pool decision.
