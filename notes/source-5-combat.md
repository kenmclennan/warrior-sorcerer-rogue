# Source Notes: Chapter 6 - Combat (BRP UGE pp. 142-160)

**Section:** Chapter 6 / Combat
**Pages:** 142-160
**Reviewer:** Phase 1 Task 2 - Combat agent
**Date:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

Chapter 6 is a complete melee and missile combat system covering a four-phase combat round (Statements, Powers, Action, Resolution), a DEX-ranked initiative sequence, the full Attack and Defense Matrix, five special damage types (bleeding, crushing, entangling, impaling, knockback), optional hit location rules, four fumble tables, armor rules, a minor/major/fatal wound framework, and naturalistic healing. The chapter skews heavily tactical: it is rich with BRP texture that produces visceral, consequential fights with no added mechanical complexity, which is exactly what design.md §3.1 asks for. However, it is not theatre-of-mind by default - movement is measured in metres, the optional hit location system adds significant bookkeeping, and the source actively promotes miniatures and VTTs in a dedicated sidebar. No native mook rules appear anywhere. Power Points appear once, in the worked example, as the cost of a Magic spell cast by an NPC; the chapter does not otherwise address PP consumption in combat, leaving the sorcery cost overlap as an open question. Death-and-dying rules are present and clear; replacement rules are absent. The combat example (Yvarre and Kallistor, pp. 158-160) is genre-resonant and can serve WSR almost verbatim.

---

## Canonise

- **Four-phase combat round (Statements, Powers, Action, Resolution).** Clean structure for theatre-of-mind play - the Statements phase forces players to declare intent in DEX order, creating the information game of sword & sorcery combat without requiring a grid. Per §3.1 ("Run online without a VTT").

- **DEX-based initiative / DEX rank ordering.** Higher DEX acts first. Simple, characteristically-grounded, and produces meaningful character differentiation between fast and slow fighters. Per §3.1 ("Carry tactical depth in player-facing options, not in resolution complexity") and §3.1 ("Stay faithful to BRP's percentile, skill-based identity").

- **DEX rank tie-breaker: relevant skill rating, then simultaneous.** Elegant and already in the base text; no extra mechanics needed.

- **Action delay (delaying to a later DEX rank).** Allows reactive, tactical play ("I wait to see what he does") without an action economy mini-game. Per §3.1.

- **Engaged / unengaged distinction.** "Engaged" = within swinging distance; "unengaged" = not in melee. This binary is sufficient for theatre-of-mind positioning without measurements. Per §3.1 and §3.2 ("Require a grid or VTT for combat" - we reject this, and this rule supports the rejection).

- **Combat action menu: Move, Attack, Noncombat Action, Engage, Disengage, and at-any-time: Parry, Dodge, Fight Defensively, Speak.** The action menu itself is theatre-of-mind compatible. "At any time" defensive actions (parry/dodge) mean players can respond reactively without needing positional data. Per §3.1.

- **Attack roll: D100 vs. weapon skill.** The core resolution chassis. Per §3.1 ("Stay faithful to BRP's percentile, skill-based identity").

- **Attack and Defense Matrix** (the full cross-referenced table of attack/parry/dodge success levels). This is the engine of BRP combat texture. The matrix produces dramatically different results from the same basic rolls, creating moment-to-moment tactical variation without added procedural complexity. Strong canonise per §3.1 ("tactical depth via BRP's existing texture, not added complexity"). The full matrix must be reproduced.

- **Critical Success (1/20 of skill rating).** Maximum possible damage, ignores armor unless countered by critical parry. Pure tactical texture from the percentile roll - no separate sub-system. Per §3.1.

- **Special Success (1/5 of skill rating).** Normal damage plus a weapon-type special effect (weapon-dependent, see Special Damage below). Strong canonise: this is the locus of tactical weapon choice without adding rules. Per §3.1.

- **Success (within skill rating but above special threshold).** Normal damage, defender's armor applies. Baseline result; clean.

- **Failure and Fumble.** Failure: miss, no effect. Fumble (highest 1/20 of failure range): dramatic mishap, roll on fumble table. Fumble tables are genre-appropriate drama engines - the dropped sword, the stumble, the wild swing that hits an ally. Per §2.3 ("muscular, decisive, and brutal").

- **Parry (reaction to successful attack roll).** Roll parrying weapon or shield skill. Successful parry deflects all damage normally; successive parries take -30% cumulative. The -30% cumulative penalty means that a combatant who tries to parry every blow in a multi-attacker fight will quickly exhaust their parry chance - creating decisive tactical pressure consistent with the genre's "the hero fights smart, not just hard." Per §2.3 and §3.1.

- **Dodge (reaction to successful attack roll).** D100 vs. dodge skill, -30% cumulative per attempt. Cannot be combined with parry in the same round unless fighting defensively. Same tactical logic as parry.

- **Parries and Dodges cannot be combined in a round** (unless fighting defensively). Clean constraint; prevents defensive stacking. Per §3.1 ("tactical depth without added complexity").

- **Fight Defensively option.** Forfeit attacks to get one extra free Dodge (no -30% penalty on that dodge). Clear player choice with a meaningful cost (no offense). Fits sword & sorcery's tactical options menu. Per §3.1.

- **Speak as a free action.** Speech doesn't cost DEX ranks. This is subtle but important: it means players can narrate, banter, and threaten during fights without mechanical cost, which supports the pulp tone. Per §2.3.

- **Weapon-type priority within DEX rank** (missile > long weapons > medium weapons > short weapons/unarmed). Theatre-of-mind compatible positional shorthand: a spearman acting before a dagger-user at the same DEX captures reach without a grid. Per §3.1.

- **Special damage types: Bleeding, Crushing, Impaling, Knockback.** All four are canonised. Each is triggered by a special success with the appropriate weapon type. They provide tactical weapon-choice without adding any roll complexity to normal successes. Per §3.1 ("tactical depth via existing texture, not added complexity") and §2.3.

  - **Bleeding:** Slashing special. Ongoing 1 HP/round at DEX rank 1 until stanched or First Aid succeeds. Creates tension and time pressure during and after combat. Genre-appropriate: Conan fights on while bleeding; the wound that isn't treated will end him eventually.
  - **Crushing:** Blunt special. Doubles damage modifier; target must make Stamina roll or be stunned 1D3 rounds. Stunned target is Easy to hit and cannot attack. The "broken bone, seeing stars" result of a club blow - directly pulp in flavor.
  - **Impaling:** Pointed/thrusting special. Doubles rolled damage dice (modifier not doubled). Weapon lodges in target; must be extracted (Difficult skill roll, or full-round action, costs HP or leaves attacker open). Vivid and creates real combat complication. Relevant to ancient weapons: spear, javelin, arrow.
  - **Knockback:** Unarmed throws and shield attacks. Resisted by SIZ resistance roll; 1m knockback per 5 damage rolled. Target may fall prone. Does not require a grid: "you are knocked back, fall prone" is a theatre-of-mind condition. Can canonise with the minor modification that knockback distance is narratively described rather than measured.

- **Entangling** (whip, net, chain, rope). Special success; immobilizes target. Follow-on effects require Grapple skill. Strong genre candidate (the rogue's lasso, the sorcerer's chain). Canonise; see note under Modify for minor trim.

- **Fumble tables (four: Melee Attack, Melee Parry, Missile Attack, Natural Weapon).** The range of results (helpless, fall prone, drop weapon, hit ally, vision obscured, wide open for auto-hit) is exactly right for pulp combat's "the fight can turn in an instant" feel. Canonise all four tables. Per §2.3.

- **Shield rules.** Shields use Shield skill for both attack and defense. Parrying missiles with shields (half/small 15%, full 30%, large 60% base chance for hand-propelled projectiles). Canonise; shields are an ancient-world staple and the skill-based parry mechanic makes shield users feel meaningfully skilled. Per §1.2 feature 3 (ancient world setting).

- **Armor (AV system).** Armor Value subtracted from damage inflicted. No roll to apply. Simple and fast. Armor has disadvantages: Skill Penalties on Agility/Stealth/Swim/Climb/Perception skills; Physical Discomfort (encumbrance/fatigue); Social Issues (armor signals violence); and Access/fit by SIZ. Canonise the full system including disadvantages. Per §2.3 ("wounds matter") and §1.2 feature 7 (naturalistic).

- **Armor and SIZ.** Armor has a 'Fits SIZ' rating; ill-fitting armor is awkward and may be unwearable. Ancient-world flavor: armor is bespoke, rare, and size-locked. Per §1.2 feature 3.

- **Minor Wounds / Major Wounds / Fatal Wounds framework.** Three wound thresholds:
  - Minor: up to HP/2 from a single wound. No impairment to DEX; character can continue acting. Multiple minor wounds totaling HP/2+ trigger Luck roll or fall unconscious.
  - Major: single wound equal to or more than HP/2. Roll on Major Wounds Table or choose an appropriate result (if hit locations in use). Immediate severe consequences - but note that the source says characters "can often still fight" and collapse into unconsciousness rather than dying outright. This is the "protagonist survives terrible wounds" mechanic of design.md §2.3.
  - Fatal: damage exceeds current HP. Immediately prone and unable to act; death follows unless medical intervention in the same or next round.
  This framework is perfect for WSR. Canonise in full.

- **Major Wounds Table (1D100 results).** 20 distinct results including limb effects, characteristic losses (STR, CON, DEX, CHA, INT), and fighting-ability impairment. Critically, most results still leave the character "able to fight" - they just fight worse. This is the "hero with scars" engine. Canonise. Per §2.3 ("Wounds matter, but the protagonist wins") and §2.5 ("sweat and scars").

- **Luck Roll to avoid permanent injury from a Major Wound.** If the Luck roll (POW x5) succeeds, the major wound heals cleanly with no permanent characteristic loss. This is the key mechanism for "the protagonist survives" - it is gated by POW (the luck/spiritual stat) and is not automatic. Per §2.3 and §1.2 feature 5 ("luck mechanics let PCs handle ordinary foes and survive things that should kill them").

- **Healing naturally: 1D3 HP per game week.** Slow, naturalistic, no magic required. Exactly design.md §1.2 feature 7 ("Recovery is herbal, restful, and slow"). Canonise.

- **First Aid: immediately restores 1D3 HP per wound, halts bleeding.** Skill-based. Not magic, but effective for triage. Canonise.

- **Conditions of Medical Care table.** Healing rate modified by quality of care (poor/restful/excellent). A useful GM tool without adding at-table bookkeeping during combat. Per §3.1 (GM-facing rules stay lean).

- **Dying Blows (Option).** A mortally wounded character may attempt one final action (Stamina roll required). Canonise this option - it is explicitly genre-appropriate. Conan does not die without taking a last swing. The source notes it replaces the "Desperate Action" spot rule. Per §2.3 and §1.1 (Howard centre of gravity). Book placement: Player Book as a named mechanic ("the dying blow"), since players should know their PC can do this.

- **Multiple attacks at skill over 100%.** Characters with skill over 100% may split into multiple attacks, each at least 50%, at successive -5 DEX rank intervals. This rewards genuine mastery and creates the "whirlwind fighter" feel of pulp action. Per §2.5 ("exceptional but mortal") and §2.3.

## Drop

The following are dropped on sight per design.md §3.2 ("Require a grid or VTT for combat") or on genre-fit grounds.

- **Movement distances in metres (6-15m, 16-29m, 30m at full move).** Movement expressed as metered distances presupposes positional tracking, which is grid/VTT territory per §3.2. Drop the specific distances. Replace with: a character who moves significantly in a combat round acts at reduced DEX rank (half or quarter, GM's call based on fiction). Narratively described movement ("I sprint across the room") replaces metered tracking.

- **Engage and Disengage rules as written (measured in metres).** "Move up to 5 meters and make an attack" and "move within 5 meters" are grid rules. The engage/disengage concept is worth keeping (see Modify), but the metre distances are dropped per §3.2.

- **Missile weapon range modifiers expressed in multiples of "standard listed range."** Bows and javelins have listed ranges in metres which generate Point Blank / Normal / Medium / Long / Extreme range bands. The range-band structure is fine; the specific metre values reference a grid. Modify: keep the range-band system (flavored as "near / normal / far / extreme") but strip the metre values and leave range assessment to fiction and GM judgment. See Modify.

- **Fast Moving Target (-50%) and Targeting Gear (+40%) modifiers for missile weapons.** The targeting-gear modifier assumes the modern scope/bipod equipment list. Drop. Fast Moving Target is kept but see Modify.

- **"Miniatures, Maps, and Virtual Tabletops" sidebar (p. 158).** The source's two-paragraph endorsement of miniatures, hex maps, and online VTT tools is directly contrary to design.md §3.2 and §3.1 ("Run online without a VTT"). Drop entirely.

- **Hit Points per Hit Location as the default wound-tracking method.** The source presents hit locations as an option (clearly labeled "Option") but also provides rules for hit points per location, separate wound tracking per location, the Damage Equal to/Exceeds Double/Triple thresholds, and Severed and Maimed Limbs rules. The full per-location HP system is incompatible with Major Wounds (the source itself says this) and adds substantial bookkeeping. See Defer - this is a design call on whether to use hit locations at all. Tentatively drop the per-location HP system; keep the hit location roll as a flavoring tool (see Modify).

- **Random Armor Values (Option).** Rolling a dice range for AV every attack adds a die roll and a lookup without meaningful tactical payoff for theatre-of-mind play. Drop. Fixed AV from armor type is cleaner. Per §3.1 ("Keep rules text uncluttered").

- **Volley Fire rules** (increased attacks-per-round for bows, referenced but not detailed here). Ancient-world bows are appropriate for WSR but the volley fire abstraction has no genre precedent in the pulp tradition (it reads as a wargame mechanic). Drop; archers use the standard missile attack structure with their weapon's Attk rate.

- **Dodging Missile Weapons (Option).** The option allows characters in heroic settings to dodge arrows at full skill. While sword & sorcery protagonists are exceptional, freely dodging arrows feels too superheroic for WSR's "exceptional but mortal" framing per §1.2 feature 5. Drop. The standard rule (missiles cannot be dodged except for the first visible one, at Difficult) preserves genre-appropriate lethality for projectile fire.

- **Initiative Rolls (DEX+D10) Option.** This option adds a random D10 to DEX each round to create variable initiative. It makes combat rounds longer and adds no genre texture - the source itself notes it "will make combat rounds somewhat longer." Drop. Static DEX rank is sufficient. Per §3.1 ("Keep rules text uncluttered").

- **Reverse Order Statement of Intent (Option).** Allows high-DEX characters to act with full knowledge of lower-DEX declarations, enabling feints. The source describes it as producing "a richer and more tactical environment." True, but this crosses from BRP texture into positional-game territory that would slow theatre-of-mind play. Drop. The standard Statements phase (highest DEX declares last) already creates some information asymmetry. Per §3.1 (GM-facing rules stay lean).

- **Powers in the Action Phase (Option).** This option merges INT-rank power use into the DEX-rank action sequence, running two countdown timers simultaneously. It adds bookkeeping complexity without genre payoff for most combats. Drop. The standard Powers phase (before Action) is cleaner. Per §3.1.

- **Weapon malfunction rules** (gun jam, lock-up, expressed as a high-number result). These rules exist for firearms and firearms-adjacent technology. WSR has no firearms. Malfunction mechanics for bows (bowstring snap, etc.) could be a GM note but are not a rules section. Drop entirely; fumble tables already cover weapon-dropping and equivalent mishaps for melee. Per §1.2 feature 3 (ancient world setting).

## Modify

- **Engage and Disengage (concept kept, metres stripped).** The engaged/unengaged binary is theatre-of-mind compatible. Rewrite: "Engaged" means a character is within arm's or weapon's reach of an opponent and exchanging blows. "Unengaged" means they are outside that exchange. Engaging costs movement but not an attack. Disengaging requires successful parries or dodges for every attack made against the character that round; if any fail, the character remains engaged. No metre measurements. Per §3.2.

- **Move action (concept kept, distances dropped).** Replace movement expressed in metres with three narrative categories: Short Move (acts at normal DEX rank), Long Move (acts at half DEX rank), Full Sprint (acts at quarter DEX rank, no attack possible, only defensive actions). These are compatible with theatre-of-mind description. Per §3.2.

- **Missile weapon ranges (range band concept kept, metres stripped).** Keep the four-band structure but rename and deformalise: Near (easy shot, skill is Easy), Normal (skill as rated), Far (skill is Difficult), Extreme (1/5 skill, success treated as normal success). This captures the range-difficulty relationship without requiring distance measurement. Per §3.2.

- **Hit location roll as optional flavor, not per-location HP.** The D20 hit location table (Right Leg 1-4, Left Leg 5-8, Abdomen 8-11, Chest 12, Right Arm 13-15, Left Arm 16-18, Head 19-20) is a vivid description tool. On a successful hit, the GM may roll D20 and describe the wound location narratively, without tracking per-location HP. This is the "simulationist underneath, pulp on the surface" principle of design.md §2.3 exactly: the detail is there, but it flavors the fiction rather than tracking a separate number. Per §2.3 and §3.2. See also Defer.

- **Knockback distances.** The source measures knockback in metres (1m per 5 damage rolled). Strip the metre measurement; describe knockback in narrative terms ("hurled backward," "slammed into the wall," "tumbled to the ground"). The resistance roll (SIZ vs. damage) and the prone result are kept. Per §3.2.

- **Removing the Statement of Intent (Option) - partially adopt.** The source's "Removing the Statement of Intent" option (jump straight to the action phase; GM counts down DEX ranks, players declare and roll immediately) is worth offering as an explicit fast-play mode for the GM Book. Not a default, but a clearly described alternative for groups who want faster resolution. Per §3.1 (GM-facing rules stay lean, but offering a fast-play option is appropriate).

- **Attacks and Parries Over 100% (Option).** The option is genre-appropriate (the master warrior who can strike multiple times) and mechanically clean (split the skill, each part at least 50%, second attack at -5 DEX ranks). Keep it, but trim the worked-example framing. Rename section header to "Mastery: Multiple Attacks" or similar. Per §2.3 and §2.5.

- **Entangling: trim the detailed sub-rules.** The entangling section runs to several paragraphs covering Grapple follow-ups, STR vs. STR resistance rolls, whip-vs.-parrying-weapon interactions, dodge/wrestle escapes, and critical-vs.-ordinary parry distinctions. The core (special success entangles target, prevents movement, follow-on effects) is canonised. The nested sub-rules (especially the Grapple sub-skill call-outs) are trimmed to a paragraph; the GM handles edge cases with the resistance table. Per §3.1 ("Keep rules text uncluttered").

- **Healing: strip the "powers and gear" references.** The source healing section references healing powers and advanced technology as accelerants. WSR has no healing magic (design.md §1.2 feature 7, §1.3) and no modern technology. Rewrite to reference only First Aid skill, Medicine skill, and natural recovery. Per §1.2 feature 7.

- **Wound flavoring for WSR.** The Major Wounds Table results (severed tendons, fused ankle bones, missing ear, sliced nose, hewn thigh, severed fingers, wrist damage) are excellent genre content. Most can be kept verbatim. Results referencing modern anatomical framing ("spinal nerve damage," "kidneys and liver") can be rewritten with ancient-world equivalent description ("the blade finds the organs of life," "the blow shakes the bones of the back"). Aesthetic modification only; mechanics unchanged.

## Defer

- **Hit locations: full system or flavor-only?** The design call on this is significant. Using the D20 hit location table as a narrative flavor tool (Modify above) is the current tentative position. But the full hit-location HP system - where wounds to legs disable the limb, chest wounds cause bleeding deaths in two turns, head wounds knock unconscious - adds the "simulationist underneath" that design.md §2.3 describes as the intent. Against: it is explicitly incompatible with the Major Wounds system (the source says so directly), and running both would require a design decision about which takes precedence. The source's own resolution: use one or the other, not both. WSR's Defer position: use Major Wounds (already canonised) plus flavor-only hit location roll; reserve the full per-location HP system as an optional advanced module in the GM Book for groups who want maximal tactical depth. Per §3.3 ("lightweight-vs-tactical tension").

- **Mook handling.** No native mook rules found anywhere in Chapter 6. The chapter treats all combatants as equally complete stat blocks. Nothing in the four-phase round, the wound system, the damage types, or the fumble tables provides for reducing mooks to a faster resolution - everything applies symmetrically to PCs and NPCs. **This is the primary mook-rules gap finding.** BRP UGE does not natively solve this. See Watch-items for implications.

- **Death and replacement.** Fatal wounds lead to death without medical intervention; the source mentions "a secure way for nonplayer characters to abandon characters for dead or capture them rather than killing them outright" as a GM note in the Major Wounds section. There are no mechanics for this beyond the GM's discretion. Character replacement remains an undesigned open question. Per §3.3.

- **Power Points in combat / sorcery cost overlap.** The chapter's worked example (p. 159) shows NPC Kallistor spending 3 PP per level of the Healing spell, and notes "Kallistor wants to keep some power points in reserve in case of emergency." This is the only PP consumption visible in the combat chapter. The critical finding: PP are drawn from the same pool whether used for sorcery spells or for any other power use. In combat, casting a spell costs PP that cannot be recovered until rest. If WSR's final sorcery cost design adds a second cost layer (corruption, permanent POW, etc.) on top of PP, the in-combat cost question is: does casting in combat cost both PP and the additional cost, or only PP? This is a §3.3 sorcery cost question that also has a combat chapter answer. Flag for the magic chapter design decision. Per design.md §3.3.

- **Hero points / Fate points mechanic.** Design.md §2.3 explicitly anticipates "a finite resource that lets PCs reroll, deflect a killing blow, or pull off the cinematic move." The Luck Roll (POW x5) exists as a characteristic roll, and it is used to avoid permanent injury from Major Wounds. But this is a regular roll, not a spendable finite resource. BRP UGE as read provides no Hero Point or Fate Point mechanic in Chapter 6. The full "Luck / Hero points" design is deferred. This is the single largest gap between what the genre needs and what BRP UGE natively provides in this chapter. Per §2.3 and §3.3.

- **Fighting Defensively as a mook-management tool.** The source mentions that fighting defensively "under no circumstances can be combined with any attack or offensive action." For named/dangerous opponents, this means a player who fights defensively is genuinely buying protection at cost. For mooks, this is irrelevant because the mooks don't get a Fighting Defensively status currently. If WSR designs a mook rule, Fighting Defensively may need a "vs. mooks" clarification (e.g., a character fighting defensively against mooks automatically succeeds all parries/dodges against them). Defer until mook design is resolved.

## Open Questions

1. **Mook handling: design or borrow?** The chapter contains zero native mook mechanics. WSR must either design a minion rule from scratch (e.g., mooks have half HP, or mooks go down on any successful hit, or mooks are resolved as a group roll) or borrow from a sibling BRP product. The Runequest "mob" rules or the older Stormbringer minion conventions would be candidates. This is the watch-item #3 flag; escalate to the design phase.

2. **Hit location: Major Wounds only, or full per-location HP as optional module?** The two systems are incompatible and the source says so explicitly. WSR must pick one as the default. Recommendation (tentative): Major Wounds default, per-location HP as a GM Book optional module for groups who want it. Needs a decision before chapter drafting.

3. **Hero points / Fate points: design scope?** The gap between BRP's Luck Roll and what design.md §2.3 describes ("a finite resource that lets PCs reroll, deflect a killing blow, or pull off the cinematic move") is a design gap, not just an extraction gap. This is WSR's single largest new-design task in the combat chapter. The Luck Roll can serve as a baseline ("you may push your luck once per session, spending an automatic Luck result instead of rolling"), but a full Fate Point economy needs design. Needs a decision before chapter drafting.

4. **Entangling weapons in an ancient-world context.** Whips, nets, bolas, lassos, chains, ropes: all ancient-world appropriate and all supported by the entangling rules. But the rules reference "man-catchers" and other gladiatorial/exotic equipment. Does WSR's ancient-world skill list include all these weapon types? This is a coordination question with the Skills and Equipment chapter agents.

5. **Disengage failure: tactical implication.** The source says a failed parry/dodge during disengage means "your character is still engaged." This creates a situation where a PC trapped in melee by multiple foes cannot break free with bad rolls. For genre purposes, is this the intended behavior? Conan can fight his way out of a mob; this rule makes it mechanically possible to be trapped. Probably correct for the genre, but worth flagging as a design note.

6. **The worked example (pp. 158-160).** The Yvarre/Kallistor example is genuinely good genre fiction and demonstrates the system cleanly. It does not use hit locations, it uses basic First Aid, it shows PP depletion, and it ends in flight and survival rather than clean victory. Recommend keeping a lightly rewritten version of this example (with WSR-appropriate names and setting details) in the WSR combat chapter's examples appendix.

## Watch-items

### Character distinctiveness

**Moderate match from combat mechanics.** The combat system produces distinctiveness through weapon and skill choice: a character with Sword at 75% and Shield at 60% plays very differently from one with Great Axe at 80% and no parrying weapon. The special success types (bleeding from slash, crushing from blunt, impaling from thrust) create weapon-type identities. A character known for their impaling attacks with a spear is narratively distinctive. The "Attacks over 100%" rule creates a clear mastery tier. However, none of this is a designed distinctiveness system - it emerges from the existing combat mechanics. No combat-specific traits, stances, or named techniques exist in the source. Character distinctiveness in combat comes entirely from the skill percentages, which vary by profession and personal skill allocation. Adequate but not rich.

### Party cohesion

**Weak match.** The combat chapter has no cooperative mechanics, flanking bonuses (rightly absent per §3.2), combined-action rules, or party-level special abilities. Two PCs attacking the same target get no coordination benefit. The source is entirely individual-action-resolution throughout. The only implicit party-cohesion element is the Speak free action, which means a player can shout tactical information without cost. There is no mechanical reward for doing so. Designing a simple cooperative combat mechanic (e.g., assisting an ally's attack gives a bonus to the ally's next action; or two PCs engaging the same opponent create an "outnumbered" disadvantage for the opponent) would serve design.md §3.1 ("Provide hooks for party cohesion") and would need to be a WSR original design. Nothing in Chapter 6 provides this natively.

### Mook rules

**No match. Gap confirmed.** Chapter 6 contains no mook, minion, or mob mechanics of any kind. All combatants - PC, NPC, soldier, thug - resolve through the same four-phase structure with full wound tracking, fumble tables, and the Attack and Defense Matrix. A fight against six city guards resolves exactly the same as a fight against a single named assassin: six DEX-rank action slots, six parry attempts, six fumble opportunities. This will produce slow, grindy combat against groups of ordinary foes - the opposite of the "dispatched quickly" requirement of design.md §2.3 ("Soldiers, thugs, and city guards are not BRP-standard combatants - they are sketches of combatants, designed to be dispatched"). **BRP UGE does not solve the mook problem.** WSR must design or borrow a solution. This is the highest-priority open design question emerging from this chapter.

The only relevant native tool found: the source mentions that GM-side combatants can be given a single D10 initiative roll to represent an entire group ("roll 1D10 to represent a group of nonplayer characters" in the Initiative Rolls option). This is a GM convenience note in an option we are dropping, but the concept - treating a group of mooks as a single initiative entry - is a useful seed for a mook design. Flag for the design phase.

### Sorcery cost

**Partial match.** Power Points appear in the worked example as the cost of spell use in combat (Healing 1 costs 3 PP per level; Kallistor uses Healing 1 and 2, spending 9 PP total from a pool of 14, ending at 5). The chapter establishes that PP spent on spells during combat come from the same pool as all other PP use. No additional cost mechanic is present in the chapter - PP is the only currency. The overlap question: if WSR adds a second cost layer to sorcery (corruption, permanent POW, patron debt), casting a spell in combat would incur that additional cost at the worst possible moment. This is genre-appropriate (sorcery in a fight is a desperate act with compounding consequences) but needs explicit rules language. The chapter itself does not provide that language; it must come from the sorcery chapter design. Per design.md §3.3.

## Book Placement

**Split: Player Book (primary), GM Book (variant rules and GM tools).**

**Player Book:**
- Full combat round structure (four phases)
- DEX rank initiative, tie-breaking
- The full action menu (Move, Attack, Noncombat Action, Engage, Disengage, Parry, Dodge, Fight Defensively, Speak)
- Engaged / unengaged rules (modified, no metres)
- Attack and Defense Matrix (reproduced in full)
- All four success levels (Critical, Special, Success, Failure/Fumble) with definitions
- Parry rules (including cumulative -30%)
- Dodge rules (including cumulative -30%)
- Fight Defensively rules
- All five special damage types (Bleeding, Crushing, Entangling, Impaling, Knockback) - these are player-facing tactical choices
- Dying Blows (labeled as a named WSR mechanic)
- Armor rules (AV, disadvantages, SIZ fit)
- Shields (parry and missile blocking)
- Minor / Major / Fatal wound framework
- Major Wounds Table
- Healing Naturally
- First Aid rules
- Multiple attacks over 100% (labeled "Mastery: Multiple Attacks")

**GM Book:**
- All four Fumble tables (the GM rolls on these, not the player)
