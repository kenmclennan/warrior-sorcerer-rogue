# Source Notes: Chapter 9 - Gamemastering (pp. 228-241)

## Summary

Chapter 9 is explicitly labelled as content "primarily useful only to the gamemaster" and covers the full arc of GM practice: recruiting players, group size, running one-shots vs campaigns, adventure design structure, NPC creation and roleplay, session management techniques, and a personality trait system for NPCs. The chapter's most mechanically significant item is the **Optional Rule Checklist** (pp. 229-230), which catalogues every optional rule in BRP UGE with a one-line description - this is the fastest single tool for making canonise/drop/modify decisions across the entire ruleset. The GMing advice itself is mostly system-agnostic craft content that needs heavy WSR-specific rewriting rather than direct extraction. No mook/minion rule system appears anywhere in the chapter; mook handling is not addressed in BRP UGE's GM chapter at all (see Mook Rules watch-item below). The chapter closes with a Personality Traits system for NPCs (pp. 240-241) that has direct relevance to character distinctiveness.

## Canonise

GMing content that WSR should carry into the GM Book (modified to WSR tone and setting):

- **Niche protection / character review at session start** (p. 230-231) - examining PCs' skills and high-skill ratings to spotlight them in play. Directly serves character distinctiveness (design.md §3.1 "Make characters feel distinctive without classes"). Rename or reframe for WSR vocabulary.
- **Character Goals interview** (p. 231) - asking players what their characters want before play begins; the answers seed hooks for the GM. Fits personal stakes as core driver (design.md §1.2 #1, §4.2 "At the larger level, players make character decisions").
- **Connecting the Characters to Each Other** (pp. 234-235) - the technique of having each PC name one other character they have a connection to (personal history, past rivalry, friendship). This is the party cohesion mechanism BRP UGE provides natively (design.md §3.1 "Provide hooks for party cohesion"). Canonise and flag in the GM Book as a session-zero practice. Note: the text explicitly mentions Passions (Loves/Loyalties) as the mechanical representation of these connections - reinforces Passions canonise.
- **Integrate the Characters into the Story** (pp. 234-235) - spreading world contacts across the group, not concentrating all plot integration on one PC. Good GMing practice that maps to the "string of stories" campaign model (design.md §4.5).
- **Nonplayer Characters** section (pp. 236-237) - the distinction between named NPCs (fully realised) and unnamed NPCs ("Guard #3"). The observation that giving an NPC a name makes them important is the mook-differentiation principle in prose form (see Mook Rules watch-item). Canonise the principle; note it does not provide mechanics.
- **Personality Traits** (pp. 240-241) - the 20-pair percentile trait table for NPCs. Fast characterisation tool for the GM. Canonise for GM Book as the NPC personality quick-reference (design.md §3.1 "enough scaffolding to run a session"). Consider whether to include for PCs or restrict to NPCs (overlaps with Passions).
- **Personality Traits and Player Characters** (p. 241) - the option to apply personality traits to PCs as passion-like mechanics (experience checks on traits, opposing trait decreasing). Flag as an optional adjunct to Passions if both are canonised.
- **Set the Initial Scene** structure (pp. 235-236) - Location / Introductions / Motive / Carrot / Stick / Drama framework. Practical session-opening advice; fits the "hook" structure of WSR's short-story session format (design.md §4.1).
- **Recurring Nonplayer Characters** principle (p. 234) - villains who survive, antagonists who return, unexpected escapes. Fits the anthology campaign model where old enemies recur (design.md §4.5 "An antagonist who keeps escaping").
- **Gamemastering Techniques** (pp. 238-241) - Introduction, Player Characters Are the Stars, Show Don't Tell, Flashbacks, 'Jobber' Sequences. These are good-practice tools the WSR GM Book should include. Jobber sequences (emphasising a new threat's danger by showing it dispatching weaker opponents) have direct genre relevance for introducing sorcerers, demons, and boss-level threats.

## Modify

GMing content worth keeping in transformed form:

- **Adventure design structure** (pp. 232-233) - the Introduction / Complication / Rising Action / Loss / Rising Action / Climax / Denouement phases. The seven-phase model is more elaborate than WSR's short-story format needs (design.md §4.1 describes hook / complications / reckoning as the three-part shape). **Modify:** compress to WSR's three-phase shape (Hook / Complications / Reckoning) and rewrite to reflect the genre-specific ending conditions (escape with the bag, not moral vindication).
- **One-Shot vs Campaign** (pp. 228, 232-233) - BRP frames these as equal options; WSR frames one-shots as the default with campaigns as anthology series. **Modify:** rewrite to reflect WSR's episodic short-story default (design.md §4.1, §4.5), with campaign-as-anthology framing and the explicit point that the campaign's "grand arc" is personal revenge/debt/reputation, not world-saving.
- **Describe the Setting** (pp. 229) - the three questions (What is the setting like / Who are the player characters / What do they do). Useful structure but written for generic BRP. **Modify:** rewrite to WSR-specific framing: the three questions become WSR-specific (which cultural analogue, what is each PC's immediate personal stake, what is the first job/hook).
- **House Rules** (pp. 236) - generic advice about making rulings. **Modify:** condense to a single paragraph and reframe as "the GM is the final arbiter" principle; drop the meta-references to online house-rule communities.
- **Absent Player Characters** (pp. 237) - generic handling for missing players. **Modify:** condense significantly; in WSR's small-group context (2-4 PCs, design.md §4.3) an absent PC is more noticeable. Note that narrative absence (PC called away, captured, etc.) fits the genre naturally.
- **Cutscenes** (p. 239) - the technique of showing antagonist action the PCs aren't present for. **Modify:** useful technique for the GM Book but BRP's framing is generic. Rewrite with WSR-specific examples (the sorcerer performing the ritual; the assassin receiving the contract; the warlord's generals planning the ambush).
- **Nonplayer Characters** mechanics section (pp. 237) - the mechanical guidance for statting NPCs (what attributes to prepare for combat vs. social encounters). **Modify:** align with WSR's character framework once that is established; the distinction between important/unimportant NPCs is preserved but the attribute list will differ.

## Open Questions

1. **What is "Step Seven" in the Optional Rule Checklist?** The checklist entry is opaque without seeing Chapter 2. Flagged as needs-deeper-look; to be resolved at Chapter 2 review.
2. **Personality Traits vs Passions boundary.** Both systems can apply to PCs and NPCs. Does WSR use one for PCs and one for NPCs, or both, or only Passions (which are already flagged likely-canonise)? Cannot decide until Passions dispatch results are integrated.
3. **Cultural Modifiers scope.** The checklist flags this as useful for "dramatically different cultures." WSR's ancient-world setting has multiple cultural analogues (Mediterranean, Levantine, Steppe, etc.). How granular should the cultural modifier list be? Does this replace or supplement the profession skill package? Needs Chapter 2 review.
4. **Fate Points - are they power-point-based?** The checklist says "Players can spend power points to adjust dice results." If Fate/Luck Points draw from the same pool as sorcery, this has significant design implications (sorcerers spending Luck degrade their casting ability). Needs Chapter 10 review.
5. **NPC statting conventions.** The chapter's guidance on NPC mechanics (INT, POW, CHA for social; DEX, CON, STR for combat) will need to be re-anchored once WSR's characteristic list is confirmed from Chapter 2.

## Watch-Items

### character distinctiveness
- **Distinctive Features** (checklist) - likely-canonise; directly serves this goal.
- **Cultural Modifiers** (checklist) - likely-canonise; cultural background differentiates characters at creation.
- **Personality Traits** (pp. 240-241) - likely-canonise for NPCs; may extend to PCs as a complement to Passions.
- **Niche protection / character review** practice (pp. 230-231) - a GM technique, not a mechanic, but reinforces distinctiveness in play.
- **Freeform Professions** (checklist) - needs-deeper-look; may be needed if standard professions don't cover the anti-hero archetype range.
- Gap noted: BRP UGE's GM chapter does not introduce new distinctiveness mechanics; the tools are in Chapters 2, 3, and 10. The GM chapter primarily provides the practice of spotlighting existing character distinctiveness.

### party cohesion
- **Connecting the Characters to Each Other** (pp. 234-235) - the most direct party-cohesion tool in the chapter. A session-zero practice, not a mechanic, but explicitly mentioned alongside Passions (Loves/Loyalties) as the mechanical layer. This confirms that BRP UGE's cohesion mechanism is Passions, not a dedicated cooperative-ability system.
- **Complementary Skills** (checklist) - likely-canonise; mechanical cohesion through skill cooperation.
- Gap noted: no cooperative ability or combo-trigger mechanic found anywhere in Chapter 9. The party cohesion mechanism is purely narrative (character connections) + Passions. If WSR wants mechanical party-binding beyond Complementary Skills, it will need to be designed. Flag for design.md §3.1.

### mook rules
**No mook/minion rules found in Chapter 9.** The chapter's NPC section (pp. 236-237) does contain the *prose principle* that unnamed NPCs need less preparation than named ones ("Guard #3" vs. a fully developed character), but there is no mechanic for this. Specifically: no streamlined stat blocks, no "mooks drop in one hit," no mass-combat abstraction, no minion hit-point pooling. The only mechanic-adjacent gesture is the observation that unnamed NPCs can simply be described and narrated around rather than fully statted.

**Conclusion:** BRP UGE does not provide a mook rule natively in its GM chapter. The Chapter 11 "Customizing Creatures" section (pp. 298-299 - Normal/Heroic/Epic/Superhuman tiers) may be the closest candidate; check Chapter 11 notes. The Total Hit Points optional rule (PC-and-important-NPC-only variant) could be repurposed as a de facto mook rule (all unnamed foes use standard HP; PCs and named antagonists use Total HP). This is a likely-modify candidate and a design question for the combat chapter.

### sorcery cost
- **Sanity** (checklist) - needs-deeper-look as corruption-track candidate; flagged for magic chapter.
- **Fate Points / power points** interaction - if Fate Points draw from the power-point pool, this becomes relevant to sorcery cost (sorcerers cannot freely spend Luck without affecting their casting). Flag for magic and Chapter 10 review.
- No new sorcery cost information in Chapter 9; the watch-item remains open.
