# PA3. Inspirational Influences

> _"Civilized men are more discourteous than savages, because they know they can be impolite without having their skulls split."_
>
> - Robert E. Howard, _The Tower of the Elephant_

This appendix lists works that informed _Warrior \* Sorcerer \* Rogue_'s tone, mood, and texture. It is the "Appendix N" of WSR - what to read, watch, hear, and look at if you want to get the vibe right.

The list is not exhaustive. The genre is rich; pursue what intrigues you. Add what you love.

---

## 1. Films

The visual lexicon of pulp adventure and the pacing of cinematic sword and sorcery:

- **The Seventh Voyage of Sinbad** (1958) - Ray Harryhausen at his prime. The cyclops, the dragon, the singular pleasure of stop-motion menace.
- **Jason and the Argonauts** (1963) - The skeleton fight is canon. The film establishes the ancient-world flavour the system aims for.
- **The Golden Voyage of Sinbad** (1973) - More Harryhausen. The Kali statue fight is a master class in visceral combat staging.
- **Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger** (1977) - Continuing the Sinbad cycle; the sabre-toothed tiger.
- **Clash of the Titans** (1981) - The original. Mythological adventure with heroes, monsters, and capricious gods.
- **Conan the Barbarian** (1982) - The Milius and Stone adaptation. The cinematic distillation of Howard's tone. The Riddle of Steel.
- **The Beastmaster** (1982) - Cheaper, weirder, and beloved. Captures the lone-hero-against-evil-sorcerer beat.
- **The Sword and the Sorcerer** (1982) - The three-bladed sword is iconic. Pulp and proud of it.
- **Krull** (1983) - Sword-and-sorcery blurring into sword-and-planet. The Glaive sets a tonal note.
- **Red Sonja** (1985) - Brigitte Nielsen's Sonja and Schwarzenegger's Kalidor. Vengeance on a pulp epic scale.
- **The Barbarians** (1987) - The Paul brothers as twin warriors. A cult favourite.
- **John Carter** (2012) - Edgar Rice Burroughs' Mars adapted to film. Establishes the planetary-romance flavour permitted in WSR per the science-fiction allowance.

## 2. Books

The literary core of the genre:

- **Robert E. Howard** - the centre. Particularly:
  - _The Tower of the Elephant_
  - _The God in the Bowl_
  - _The People of the Black Circle_
  - _Red Nails_
  - _The Phoenix on the Sword_
  - _The Scarlet Citadel_
- **Michael Moorcock** - the Elric saga. Doom, decadence, sorcery-as-curse. Particularly:
  - _Elric of Melniboné_ (novel - the definitive introduction)
  - _The Dreaming City_
  - _While the Gods Laugh_
  - _The Stealer of Souls_
  - _Kings in Darkness_
  - _The Singing Citadel_
  - _Stormbringer_ (novel - the sequence's culmination)
- **Fritz Leiber** - the Swords books. Fafhrd and the Grey Mouser. Picaresque, urban, sardonic. Particularly:
  - _Ill Met in Lankhmar_ (origin story; Hugo and Nebula winner)
  - _Bazaar of the Bizarre_
  - _Lean Times in Lankhmar_
  - _The Howling Tower_
  - _The Sunken Land_
  - _Stardock_
  - _The Swords of Lankhmar_ (novel)
- **Gardner F. Fox** - _Kothar of the Magic Sword_ and the rest of the Kothar series. Pulp paperback sword and sorcery.
- **David Gemmell** - particularly the Druss and Waylander novels. The brooding doomed hero, the warrior with a code, the moral complexity.
- **Michael Shea** - _Nifft the Lean_. Brilliant prose, a thief-hero, the underworld (literal and metaphorical).
- **Edgar Rice Burroughs** - the Martian (Barsoom) series. _A Princess of Mars_, _The Gods of Mars_, _The Warlord of Mars_, and onward. The sword-and-planet origin point.
- **Oliver Dickinson** - the Griselda stories. Ancient-world thief tales tonally close to what WSR wants.

## 3. Comics

- **Head Lopper** - Andrew MacLean. Norgal the Head-Lopper, his witch-companion, and a procession of ancient horrors. Gorgeous art and a well-tuned sense of pulp dread.

## 4. Music

For the table, or for inspiration while writing:

- **The Chronicle of the Black Sword** - Hawkwind. A Moorcock-collaboration concept album drawing on Elric. Plays directly to the Moorcock-doom register.
- **Conan the Barbarian OST** - Basil Poledouris. The genre's defining soundtrack. _Anvil of Crom_ and _Riders of Doom_ are scene-setters.
- **Krull OST** - James Horner. Adventurous, cosmic, with a touch of menace.

## 5. Art

For visual reference and for seeing how others have rendered the genre:

- **Frank Frazetta** - the visual lexicon of sword and sorcery. His Conan covers and Death Dealer paintings define what the genre _looks_ like.
- **Brom** - particularly his work for the Dark Sun setting. Decadent, brutal, post-apocalyptic; useful reference for the cyclic-history "you have inherited a fallen world" aesthetic in WSR.

---

The list is not exhaustive. Add what you love. The genre rewards wide reading, watching, listening, and looking.
