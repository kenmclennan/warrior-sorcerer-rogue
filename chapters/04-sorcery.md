# 4 Sorcery

This chapter covers becoming a sorcerer, casting spells, and summoning demons.

## 4.1 Becoming a Sorcerer

### 4.1.1 The Source of Sorcerous Power

Sorcerers do not sleep. Instead, each night they send their souls on a sojourn into the realms beyond the mortal world, where entities of immense age and power dwell. There, in spirit form, they seek out these beings to bargain with, to trick, or to steal from them the power to bend the mortal world to their will: what ordinary folk call sorcery.

The trance-like state they enter when crossing the threshold leaves them vulnerable and exposed. Sorcerers guard their resting places well.

When their souls return from these nightly sojourns their minds are afire with the painfully incomprehensible shapes, patterns, colours, and sounds of magic. The magic must be unleashed; it cannot be held for long. When it is gone, it leaves behind a terrible emptiness.

Knowingly or not, the sorcerer serves the oblique purposes of these higher powers in the realms of humankind.

### 4.1.2 Becoming a Sorcerer

To become a sorcerer, a character must have a POW score of 16 or higher and a sorcerous profession or background. See §2.5 for the professions that qualify. A non-sorcerous character who later pursues sorcery may meet these requirements during play with the GM's agreement.

Starting sorcerers know a number of spell levels equal to half their INT (round up). See §4.5 for talisman rules.

At character creation, a sorcerer-archetype PC may trade vitality for power: every 2 points permanently reduced from any combination of STR, CON, or CHA grants 1 POW. POW cannot be raised above 21 by this trade, and the trade is only available during character creation.

## 4.2 The Talisman

Every sorcerer possesses a **talisman** that acts as a focus and a conduit for their magic. Each talisman is unique to its sorcerer. Common forms include:

- **A ring** carved or cast in bronze, silver, or stranger metal; often bearing a sigil only the sorcerer can read.
- **An amulet** - a stone, a tooth, a fragment of a fallen star - worn on a thong against the skin.
- **A staff** banded with the sorcerer's chosen alloys, struck into the ground when the trance begins.
- **A small carrying object** - a sealed phial, a knotted cord, an inscribed bone, a tooth in a setting - chosen for portability and concealment.

A sorcerer makes their talisman once, at the beginning of their practice. Mechanically this is assumed to have happened before play begins (see §2.10.5).

Without a talisman a sorcerer cannot sojourn into the realms beyond to gain new magic, nor cast the magic they already possess. A sorcerer may use another's talisman only after a week-long period of attunement, and may be attuned to no more than one talisman at a time. Recovering a lost talisman restores all sorcerous abilities immediately on contact.

A sorcerer whose talisman is destroyed may make a new one, but the process is complex, time-consuming, and expensive, involving some sort of quest. The player and the GM should work together to determine the details.

## 4.3 Sojourning

A sorcerer can hold spell levels ready in their mind equal to their INT. These are the workings they have negotiated for in their most recent trance and may cast immediately. Spells beyond this limit are known but not ready - to cast them, the sorcerer must trance again.

**Sojourning.** Each night the sorcerer sits in a trance-like state, their soul cast into strange dimensions. They return at dawn with a mind full of spells - up to their INT in spell levels, chosen from their known spell list. This trance replaces ordinary sleep. While in trance the sorcerer is **unaware of the physical world**: they do not see, hear, or feel, and an attacker can act on the body with impunity. Returning from the trance to wakefulness takes a full round of confusion (acts last in the round, no parry or dodge).

A sorcerer prevented from trancing for a full night cannot refresh their spells. If they still hold uncast spells from a previous trance, they must succeed on an Idea roll or lose 1D4 of them at random.

**Swapping a spell during the day.** A sorcerer may sojourn briefly between nights to swap a single ready spell for another from their known list. This requires uninterrupted stillness for a number of turns equal to the spell's level. To force the crossing in such a short space of time, the sorcerer must rely on rare and exotic herbs, elixirs, or drugs.

## 4.4 Casting Spells

A sorcerer who has their talisman, knows the spell, has the PP, and has time to cast succeeds automatically - unless they are interrupted during the casting or the target resists.

### 4.4.1 Power Point Cost

Every spell costs PP equal to its level, paid at the moment of casting. A sorcerer cannot spend more PP than they possess, and PP do not regenerate above the sorcerer's POW score.

Some powerful spells demand permanent POW in addition to PP. Others require special herbs, potions, words, or other components. The GM may rule that additional requirements apply, or none at all. If a spell demands a sacrifice of permanent POW, the sorcerer's PP maximum drops with the POW; if their POW later increases, the PP maximum rises with it.

See §5.5 for PP totals and recovery.

### 4.4.2 Time to Cast

An instantaneous spell resolves on the caster's Initiative Rank in the round it is cast (§6.1). A sorcerer who intends to cast may roll their initiative with **1D10 + INT** instead of the usual **1D10 + DEX**, reflecting the mental quickness of the working. The cast itself is the sorcerer's combat action for the round; they may not also attack or move beyond a Short Move. Multi-round workings (rituals, demon summoning) are noted in the spell entry and cannot be performed mid-combat.

### 4.4.3 Interruption During Casting

If the sorcerer takes damage or a sudden shock while casting, make a Difficult Idea roll (INT/2, round up). On a failure, the working collapses and committed PP are lost. Knocked unconscious: automatic failure.

### 4.4.4 Spell Duration

Default duration is the caster's POW in combat rounds, unless the entry states otherwise. Instantaneous spells have no duration. A sorcerer may end a spell early by will at no cost.

### 4.4.5 Resistance and Contested Spells

Targeted spells must overcome the target's resistance via a resistance roll (§5.3.5): the sorcerer's current PP (after paying the cost) vs. the target's POW. On a failure, the spell is blocked; PP are still spent. Winning a resistance roll against a target with equal or higher POW earns an experience checkmark toward improving POW (§5.7.5).

## 4.5 The Disruptive Impact of Iron

Unenchanted iron is inimical to sorcerous power.

**Rule:** Each 1 ENC of unenchanted iron carried imposes **-5% on all sorcery rolls**, including Resistance Table rolls made as part of a spell's effect.

**What counts:** Iron and steel weapons, armour, chains, manacles, and significant iron-bearing fittings (iron-reinforced items borne on the body). Steel is carburised iron; the sorcerous current finds the metal regardless of carbon content. Small fittings - buckles, rivets - on primarily leather or bronze items do not count unless the GM rules the proportion significant.

**What does not count:** Bronze, copper, leather, bone, wood, stone, and all other non-ferrous materials. Enchanted iron or steel (consecrated through proper sorcerous ritual) does not impose the penalty.

**Practical effect:** A sorcerer carrying an iron sword (ENC 1), iron armour (ENC 9), and an iron helm (ENC 1) carries 11 ENC of iron - sorcery rolls suffer -55%. Most sorcerers travel in bronze or leather and leave the heavy martial kit to their companions.

## 4.6 Learning New Spells

Learning a new spell requires extensive exploration during the nightly sojourn. The search takes 1D6 weeks (the GM rolls in secret). After that time has elapsed the sorcerer makes an INT x3 roll and consults the table below:

| INT Roll         | Result                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Critical Success | The spell is learned. In addition, 1D3 points of POW are permanently gained. |
| Success          | The spell is learned.                                                        |
| Failure          | The spell is not learned.                                                    |
| Fumble           | The spell is not learned. 1D3 points of POW are permanently lost.            |

### Animate Dead

**Cost:** 3 PP per body
**Cast Time:** 10 minutes per body (ritual; requires the corpse and a drop of the sorcerer's blood)
**Duration:** POW hours, or until destroyed

The sorcerer raises one humanoid corpse as an animated servant. Only the bodies of humans and human-like creatures (orcs, goblins, beastfolk, and similar) may be raised, and only those whose SIZ does not exceed the sorcerer's POW. Dragons, giants, elephants, monstrous beasts, and other massive or alien forms cannot be animated by this working. The body retains its physical capacities (STR, SIZ, and any equipment it died wearing) but loses all else: DEX is halved, INT and POW are 0, the body does not speak, eat, drink, sleep, or fatigue. It follows simple verbal commands - "follow," "kill the one I point at," "guard this door," "carry this." It cannot make tactical decisions or interpret nuanced orders.

An animated dead has HP equal to one-quarter of its CON+SIZ when alive (round up), no armour beyond what it physically wears, and treats all attacks as Brawl 25% / 1D4+dm, or as the weapon it held when it died at 25% (untrained reflex - the muscles remember the grip but not the craft). Critical effects do not apply - the body cannot bleed, cannot be stunned, cannot suffer pain; reaching 0 HP destroys the animation. Holy water, blessed weapons, or sorcerous fire deal double damage at the GM's option.

A sorcerer may have up to POW/3 (round down) animated dead under their command at one time. Exceeding the limit causes the oldest to fall inert.

**The cost of the dead.** Communities almost universally revile necromancy. An animated corpse identified by a community will produce immediate hostility, and the recognition may come from a single relative seeing the body's face. Animate Dead is not a discreet working.

### Auguries

**Cost:** 3 PP base; the sorcerer may spend any number of additional PP to push for a clearer answer
**Range:** Self
**Cast Time:** 1 hour (a ritual; requires a fire, a focus, and the sorcerer alone in quiet)
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer asks the unknown a single specific question and works the ritual of their tradition: bones cast on a hide, entrails read from a sacrificed beast, smoke patterns watched from a brazier of cedarwood, ripples studied on still water, knucklebones rattled and thrown. The form is the sorcerer's; the powers consulted are those they have known.

The question must be one that can in principle be answered. _"Where is my brother?"_ _"Who killed the merchant?"_ _"What lies beneath the temple?"_ _"Will I survive this venture?"_ The question must be specific enough to be answerable by a fact, a person, a path, or a thing.

**The roll.** The sorcerer pays 3 PP to seat the working and may spend any further PP they have to push the vision. The GM rolls D100 in secret against a chance of **(total PP spent) × 5%**. The player does not see the roll. The quality of the vision returned is determined by the result tier:

| Result                                        | Vision quality                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Critical (matching dice within the chance)    | A clear, direct, true answer. A name, a face, a path, a fact.                                                                             |
| Success                                       | A true answer, partial or framed in symbol. Enough to advance the question; not enough to resolve it.                                     |
| Failure                                       | Silence, or a vision the sorcerer cannot interpret, or a warning about something else entirely. The PP are spent.                         |
| Fumble (00 or matching dice above the chance) | A misleading answer that the sorcerer believes true. The vision is wrong, or attracts the attention of something better left undisturbed. |

The GM determines the form of any answer; the sorcerer does not get to choose how the vision speaks. A warning may serve the sorcerer better than a direct answer, depending on what the GM knows about the situation.

A sorcerer may not cast Auguries on the same question more than once per lunar month; the powers do not repeat themselves. Asking the same question twice returns silence.

**Greater questions** - questions of cosmic scope, questions about the deep past, questions about gods or the powers themselves - require additional cost. The GM may demand permanent POW, a sacrifice, a long journey to a specific place, or all three. The line is the GM's to draw.

### Fury

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Touch (self or other)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer induces a furious, berserk rage in the target. If the target is unwilling, the sorcerer makes a PP resistance roll (§5.3.5); willing recipients and the sorcerer themselves do not roll. On a success, the recipient fights unceasingly for the duration against any single target of their choice.

While the rage holds:

- The recipient gains one additional attack per round, made at the lowest Initiative Rank of the round. The spell does not add to attack skill or to damage.
- Their instinct for self-preservation is gone: -30% to all Dodge and Parry rolls.
- They ignore unconsciousness from any major wound. A fatal wound still kills them, but they continue to attack until the end of the round in which the wound was struck, dying on the lowest Initiative Rank of that round.
- They can act only in ways related to immediate combat - no sorcery, no healing, no fleeing, no negotiation.

### Bird's Eye

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Sight (one bird visible to the sorcerer at the moment of casting)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer takes control of one bird's field of vision and sees through its eyes. The bird must be within eyesight when the spell is cast; once cast, the spell continues for its duration even if the bird flies out of sight. The sorcerer may steer the bird's gaze - turning its head, drawing its attention to a specific direction - but the bird's flight is its own. If no bird is in sight when the sorcerer casts, the spell does not take hold and the PP are spent.

While the working holds, the sorcerer's body stands still and unseeing.

### Bolster the Soul

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target's POW characteristic is increased by +3 per level: +3 at level 1, +6 at level 2, +9 at level 3. The augmentation does not increase the target's PP pool or contribute to training or any permanent characteristic improvement, but the boosted POW does aid the Luck roll and any POW vs POW resistance roll - including the binding roll for a summoned demon. The point of POW spent to bind a demon must be the sorcerer's real POW, not the temporary points conferred by this spell.

**The permanence roll.** When Bolster the Soul is cast at level 3 for the full +9 POW, roll D100. On a result of 00, the effect becomes permanent: those 9 points are added to the target's POW for good. The target may never again be the subject of this spell, and they must permanently subtract 9 points from their other characteristics in any combination, adjusting any values calculated from those characteristics.

### Brazier of Power

**Cost:** 4 PP + 1 permanent POW per casting (see capacity below)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 4 hours ritual
**Duration:** Permanent

The sorcerer binds permanent vital force into a physical object - a brazier, stone, sealed vessel, or carved monolith - creating a PP reservoir. The first casting attunes the focus and gives it a capacity equal to the sorcerer's POW (after the 1-point sacrifice). The sorcerer may store PP in the focus at any time up to that capacity. To extend the capacity, the sorcerer may cast the spell again on the same focus, paying another 4 PP and 1 permanent POW; capacity grows by the sorcerer's current POW each time.

The sorcerer must be in physical contact with the focus to draw on its stored PP. When drawing, the brazier's stored points are always spent before the sorcerer's own. Only the sorcerer who attuned the focus can use its PP directly.

Brazier of Power is incompatible with Chain of Being - the two workings cannot share a focus or be active on the same sorcerer's reserve at the same time.

If the focus is moved from its consecrated place or destroyed, the attunement is lost; the permanent POW invested cannot be recovered, and a new focus must be made from scratch. If the sorcerer dies, the stored PP dissipates over POW hours.

### Chain of Being

**Cost:** 4 PP from the sorcerer (initial casting); further PP drawn from the chain as needed
**Range:** Touch (unbroken contact)
**Cast Time:** 1 round (all participants must be present and willing)
**Duration:** Until released

The sorcerer becomes the focal point of a PP chain, linking up to eight willing participants (the sorcerer plus seven others) in an unbroken touch-chain - typically holding hands in a circle. Only the sorcerer need know this spell. The 4 PP cost of the chain itself must come from the sorcerer.

Once the chain is established, PP spent on subsequent workings cast through it are drawn as uniformly as possible from each participant, starting with the sorcerer. The circle must be maintained while PP are being spent.

If any participant drops to 0 PP, the chain breaks, any working in progress collapses, and the PP committed to that working are lost.

If the chain is used to summon and bind a demon, elemental, or other entity, **ownership is shared among all participants**. They must agree on what the bound entity is commanded to do; if they cannot agree, it does nothing. When the entity's service must be negotiated or bargained for, only the sorcerer makes the relevant skill rolls and offers the terms - the others stand witness.

Chain of Being is incompatible with Brazier of Power: a sorcerer may not draw on a brazier while a chain is active, and a chain cannot be established with a participant whose own brazier is being drawn upon.

### Cloak of Night

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch, or Sight (caster may target self or another)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Enhances the target's Hide skill: level 1 = +20%; level 2 = +40%; level 3 = +60%; level 4 = +80%. At level 4, the target becomes nearly invisible in dim light and completely invisible in darkness, audible only by breath and footfall.

### Crushing Aura

**Cost:** 3 PP to cast; 1 PP per round to maintain (paid at the start of each round)
**Range:** Self
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** While maintained

The air around the sorcerer becomes thick and resistant - a shimmering steely haze visible to anyone who looks for it. The haze fills the space roughly 3 metres around the sorcerer in all directions.

While the spell holds, any creature within the haze suffers:

- **Movement halved.** Closing to melee with the sorcerer takes twice the usual rounds.
- **-20% to attack rolls** made against the sorcerer.
- **A Stamina roll (CON × 3)** at the end of each round spent within the haze. On a failure, 1D3 damage as the haze grinds against them. The damage ignores worn armour but is reduced by sorcerous protection (Shadow-Wrap and similar). On a success, no damage that round.

The sorcerer is unaffected by their own aura - they move and act normally within it, and may cast other spells while sustaining Crushing Aura.

The aura does not block missiles. Arrows, javelins, and thrown weapons pass through unaffected. The haze is for things that press themselves close, not things that fly.

The aura ends if the sorcerer falls unconscious, fails to pay the round's maintenance, or wills it dispelled.

### Curse of Sorcery

**Cost:** 4 PP
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Until the caster dies

The target must fail a POW resistance roll (§5.3.5). On a failure, the sorcerer endows a part of the target's body with some gruesome or demonic quality of the caster's choice: extra warm to the touch, slimy, always wet, scaly, extra hairy, warty, mottled in unnatural colours, or similar. With a second successful resistance roll (consuming no additional PP, declared at the moment of casting), the affected body part takes on the shape of a tentacle, a crab leg, a bird's wing, an insect feeler, or similar - the appearance is cosmetic only and confers no bonuses or abilities.

If the affected area is visible to others, the target loses 1D3 CHA while the curse holds.

Only one Curse of Sorcery may afflict a single target at a time. The curse ends when the caster dies.

### The Cursed Token

**Cost:** 4 PP
**Range:** Touch (the sorcerer must shape the token in their own hand)
**Cast Time:** 10 minutes
**Duration:** Until triggered, or 1 lunar month, whichever comes first

The sorcerer crafts a small inert object - a black jade ball, a polished disc, a carved bone - and inscribes into the working the true name or a personal trace (hair, blood, nail-paring) of a single intended victim. The token then sits, quiet and ordinary, until two conditions are met:

1. The named victim touches the token with bare skin.
2. The sorcerer is still alive.

When both conditions are satisfied, the token transforms in the victim's hand into a small venomous creature - a spider, a viper, a scorpion - which immediately strikes the victim. The bite or sting does 1D6 damage and forces a Stamina roll against POT 12 venom (§7.2). On a failure, the victim suffers the full POT in HP damage. On a success, half POT.

The token does nothing in any other hand. A wary attendant who picks it up first feels only a smooth stone or a worked bone. Only the named target triggers the working.

A sorcerer may have at most one Cursed Token active at a time. Crafting a second cancels the first.

The transformed creature dies within a round of striking and crumbles to ash. The token itself is destroyed in the working.

Delivery is the sorcerer's problem: the token must be carried to the victim, and the victim must touch it. The Sorcerer's Gaze on a servant or messenger is one classical solution.

### Enervate

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Sight
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer attacks the target's vital force directly. Compare the sorcerer's current PP against the target's current PP on the Resistance Table (§5.3.5). On a success, the target loses 1D6 PP; the drained points are not transferred to the sorcerer but regenerate normally over rest. On a failure, the spell has no effect and the target loses no PP.

A target driven to 0 PP collapses unconscious and cannot act until they recover at least 1 PP.

### Far Sight

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Sight
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer's vision sharpens: things in their line of sight appear twice as close as they actually are. While the spell holds, each additional casting on the same working cumulatively doubles the magnification - four times closer, eight times, sixteen times - allowing for ever greater clarity at distance. Useful for scouts watching for dust on the horizon, archers picking out a face among the spearmen, and watchers at the signal fires.

### The Mirror's Gaze

**Cost:** 2 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Special (see below)
**Cast Time:** Level 1: 1 round; Level 2: 10 minutes; Level 3: 1 hour (ritual)
**Duration:** POW rounds (Level 1); POW minutes (Level 2-3)

The sorcerer scrys a distant location, seeing and hearing as if standing there invisibly. The scrying point cannot move during the working and cannot pass through closed doors, walls, or other physical barriers - though it may peer through windows, gaps, and openings.

Range scales with level:

- **Level 1:** line of sight up to POW × 100 metres - over a wall, into the next room, across a battlefield.
- **Level 2:** any location within one day's travel that the sorcerer has personally visited.
- **Level 3:** any location the sorcerer has visited, regardless of distance; or any location of a person the sorcerer has bound by name and blood (a sworn enemy, a blood relative, a sworn ally).

The Mirror's Gaze requires a reflective focus: a bowl of still water, a polished obsidian disc, a mirror of silvered iron. The sorcerer must work in a quiet, dim space. Disrupting the focus or the sorcerer's concentration ends the spell; any damage taken automatically breaks the working.

Targets of The Mirror's Gaze at level 3 may sense the scrying with a Difficult Sense or Insight roll. A sorcerer being scryed may detect it with Witch Sight.

### Hammer of the Gods

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Cast on a single blunt weapon - staff, club, mace, hammer, maul - the spell adds +1 to each damage roll per level, up to the maximum damage possible for that weapon. The bonus applies only to blows struck with the blunt portion of the weapon: if cast on an edged or pointed weapon, it grants nothing unless the wielder strikes with the hilt or haft. The wielder's damage modifier is rolled separately and is unaffected.

### Hell's Razor

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Cast on a single slashing weapon with one or more cutting edges - sword, axe, glaive, falchion - the spell adds +1 to each damage roll per level, up to the maximum damage possible for that weapon. The bonus applies only to cutting blows: if cast on a blunt or impaling weapon, it grants nothing unless the wielder strikes with the edge. The wielder's damage modifier is rolled separately and is unaffected.

### Sorcery's Sharp Flame

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Cast on a single impaling weapon - spear, dagger, rapier, javelin, or similar - the spell adds +1 to each damage roll per level, up to the maximum damage possible for that weapon. The wielder's damage modifier is rolled separately and is unaffected.

The spell does not work on bows or slings, but does work on their projectiles: arrows and sling-stones may be enchanted individually. If a damage roll reaches the weapon's maximum, the tip of the weapon glows red-hot at the moment of impact and may ignite a flammable target, inflicting an additional 1D6 fire damage (see §7.4).

### Talons of the Beast

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Cast on a target rather than a weapon. The spell adds +1 to each damage roll per level for that target's Brawl or Grapple attacks, or for any small hand-weapon that augments the hand (cestus, knuckle-irons, push-dagger). The wielder's damage modifier is rolled separately and is unaffected.

Talons of the Beast does not stack with itself, and the bonus is not applied twice when the spell is active on both the target and an appropriate hand-weapon they wield. ### Honeyed Whisper

**Cost:** 2 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Earshot (the target must hear the sorcerer speak)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target must fail a POW resistance roll or perceive the sorcerer as a trusted friend for the duration. Effect scales with level:

- **Level 1:** general goodwill. The target will not initiate hostile action and is favourably disposed to the sorcerer's presence.
- **Level 2:** trusting suggestibility. The target will follow simple, non-suicidal requests phrased as friendly suggestions.
- **Level 3:** devoted alliance. The target will defend the sorcerer at risk to themself, treat them as long-trusted, and lie or scheme on their behalf.

The target retains their own goals and personality - the spell shapes their perception of the sorcerer, not their other relationships. Requests that would betray a Passion at 70% or higher, kill an immediate family member, or contradict a deeply-held loyalty permit a new POW resistance roll to shake the effect free.

When the spell ends, the target remembers their actions and feels disturbed by them. All future social interactions between the sorcerer and that target suffer a permanent -30% penalty.

### Inescapable Bonds

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Until broken (see below)

The target is seized and held as if bound by invisible chains. They cannot significantly move any limb, finger, or tongue while the spell holds, and so cannot move, strike, gesture, cast, or speak. They may still see and hear.

Inescapable Bonds affects only living beings - humans, beasts, demons in fleshly form, and similar. It has no effect on undead, animated constructs (raised dead, animated statues, bound spirits in vessels), or inanimate objects.

Once per round, the target may attempt to break the bonds by rolling their STR against the sorcerer's current PP on the Resistance Table. On a success, the bonds shatter and the spell ends. On a failure, they may try again next round.

### Inhuman Plasticity

The target's SIZ is increased or decreased by +3 per level: ±3 at level 1, ±6 at level 2, ±9 at level 3 (the caster chooses growth or reduction at the moment of casting). The change in SIZ may temporarily increase or decrease damage modifier and HP. Any HP lost while this spell is in effect are first removed from the temporary HP gained. The spell may also affect the target's effective MOV at the GM's discretion. The minimum allowable SIZ is 1; further reductions are ignored.

The target's clothes and armour change SIZ with them for the duration. Their weapons do not.

The spell can be cast on insects, birds, and other small or non-human creatures.

**The permanence roll.** When Inhuman Plasticity is cast at level 3 for the full ±9 SIZ, roll D100. On a result of 00, the effect becomes permanent: the 9 points are added to or subtracted from the target's SIZ for good. ### Leap of the Shadow Cat

Enhances the target's Jump skill by +20% per level (cumulative): level 1 = +20%, level 2 = +40%, level 3 = +60%, level 4 = +80%. The distance the target can jump also increases by +1 metre per level.

### Lethe Touch

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** Touch (bare skin)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

They retain general impressions of where they were and who they were with, but specific details are lost: the route they were shown, the words spoken, the object handed over, the secret they confessed.

The lost memory may be partially recovered later. A Difficult Idea roll triggered by encountering strong sensory cues (the same room, the same person, a smell or song from that time) restores a fragment of the forgotten interval. Full restoration requires sorcerous intervention - typically a successful Undo Sorcery.

The spell does not erase physical evidence. The target may find the dagger they accepted but will not remember accepting it; they will rationalise the gap, or they will not.

**Range:** Touch

**Duration:** Instantaneous

Heals 1D3 HP in the sorcerer or a touched target, up to the target's HP maximum. Mend the Mortal Frame may be cast only once per individual wound, but it stacks freely with First Aid and may be used to follow up a successful First Aid treatment.

### Midnight

**Duration:** POW rounds

Creates an irregular cloud of absolute, unnatural darkness, roughly four metres across in any dimension. No light source within the cloud functions; sorcerous light not originating inside the cloud may penetrate at the GM's discretion. ### Moonrise

### The Oracle Mask

**Cost:** 2 PP
**Range:** Sight (the sorcerer must see the projection point at the moment of casting)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW minutes

The sorcerer projects their voice from a chosen point within sight - an idol, a corpse, an empty doorway, a thrown stone, an apparently empty corner. While the spell holds, words the sorcerer mutters under their breath are heard aloud at the projection point and can be heard normally by anyone within earshot of that point. Listeners cannot tell the sound is sorcery; they hear what their ears report.

The sorcerer may shape the voice freely:

- Their own voice
- A voice of another sex, age, or accent
- A voice approximating a specific person they have heard speak (a close friend or lover of that person may make a Difficult Perform roll to recognise the imitation as false)
- The voice of no one at all - a flat, sourceless utterance audible to all

The projection point need not have a mouth. A wooden idol, a corpse with sealed lips, a fountain, a shadow - any of these can speak. If the point is destroyed during the duration, the spell ends.

The Oracle Mask grants no command power and no compulsion. Listeners respond as they would to any voice: they may believe it, disbelieve it, look for the source, run, attack, or kneel. The spell is _the voice_ - what it does to the listeners depends on what is said and who hears it.

The sorcerer must remain within sight of the projection point to sustain the spell. Breaking line of sight ends it immediately.

### Plague Wind

**Cost:** 4 PP to seat the working; while the working holds, the sorcerer's **maximum PP is reduced** by the value below
**Range:** Sight (at the moment of casting)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** As long as the sorcerer maintains the working

The sorcerer calls a wasting pestilence from where it lies dormant in old Stygian tombs and seats it in a single named victim. The target makes a Stamina roll against POT 14 disease (§7.1). On a success, the working slides off; the 4 PP are spent. On a failure, the target contracts the plague.

**The disease.** Plague Wind is a CON-attacking disease (§7.1.1, Chills type). Under the sorcerer's maintenance the disease never recedes: each day the victim makes a new Stamina roll, but successes only prevent that day's severity advance - consecutive successes do not throw off the disease. Failed rolls advance severity through the §7.1 ladder (mild → acute → severe → terminal), with CON loss accruing at the severity rate. A victim brought to 0 CON dies; they cannot be raised by Mend the Mortal Frame, and the corpse cannot be raised by Animate Dead.

**The outbreak.** The sorcerer is not infecting one person - they are seating the plague. The outbreak has a **Scale** that grows over time. Each evening the GM rolls D100; on a result over **25**, the outbreak grows by one tier:

| Scale | Reach                    | PP maximum reduction |
| ----- | ------------------------ | -------------------- |
| I     | The original victim      | -1 PP max            |
| II    | Household (3-10)         | -2 PP max            |
| III   | Ward (~50)               | -5 PP max            |
| IV    | District (hundreds)      | -10 PP max           |
| V     | Town (thousands)         | -20 PP max           |
| VI    | City (tens of thousands) | -40 PP max           |

Dense urban environments, poor sanitation, refugees pressed by siege, and warm dry weather add +10% to the grow roll at GM discretion. Isolation, cold, clean water, and active quarantine subtract -10%.

**The maintenance cost.** While the working holds, the sorcerer's PP maximum is reduced by the value for the current Scale. The reserved PP cannot be spent on casting, Fate Point spends, or anything else - the sorcerer is feeding the working with their own vital current. As the outbreak grows, the reservation grows with it. A sorcerer whose effective maximum drops to 0 or below loses the working immediately and falls unconscious; the outbreak begins to recede by ordinary §7.1 disease rules.

A sorcerer's Brazier of Power may absorb the maintenance reduction in part or whole; the Brazier's stored PP is reserved instead of the sorcerer's own. This is how mid-tier sorcerers reach Scale IV, and how master sorcerers with deep braziers reach Scale V. Scale VI requires either a vast brazier of decades or a coven of sorcerers linked by Chain of Being.

**The sorcerer may end the working at will** at any time. When maintenance lapses - by choice, by death, or by exhaustion - the outbreak progresses by ordinary §7.1 disease rules and most of the infected will recover.

**Counters.** Killing the sorcerer ends the working immediately and the outbreak begins to recede. Undo Sorcery at level 3 or higher ends the working in a single infected target (but does not stop the disease in others already infected, who continue under ordinary §7.1 progression).

### Refutation

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Self
**Cast Time:** 1 round (cast in the same round as, or immediately after, the working it is to protect)
**Duration:** As long as the protected working persists, or until expended

Refutation is a defensive layer placed on the caster's own active workings. When cast, Refutation must be assigned to a specific spell the caster has placed - a curse, a binding, an enchantment, a sustained effect - and remains attached to that working for as long as the working persists. A sorcerer may have multiple Refutations active simultaneously, each guarding a different working.

When another sorcerer attempts to dispel the protected working with Undo Sorcery, the two spells contest first. Compare the level of Refutation against the level of Undo Sorcery on the Resistance Table. If Refutation prevails, the Undo Sorcery fails and its PP are lost; Refutation continues to defend the working. If Undo Sorcery prevails, Refutation is expended (its PP are lost), and the Undo Sorcery resolves normally.

Refutation defends only against Undo Sorcery. It does nothing against the working being unmade by direct means: the curse-victim being healed by other sorcery or natural recovery, the bound demon being slain, the enchanted object being destroyed.

### Relentless Vitality

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target's CON is increased by +3 per level: +3 at level 1, +6 at level 2, +9 at level 3. The boosted CON temporarily increases HP, the Stamina (CON x5) roll, and the target's resistance to poison and disease.

Damage taken while Relentless Vitality is in effect is first removed from the temporary HP gained. It is impossible for the target to die from HP loss while the spell holds, but a target at 0 or fewer HP is unconscious and cannot act. Further attacks continue to accrue negative HP and may inflict major wounds or lost limbs. When the spell ends, the target dies immediately if their HP is not restored to a positive total by First Aid, Mend the Mortal Frame, or other means. Any major wounds or lost limbs taken during the spell remain unless separately treated.

**The permanence roll.** When Relentless Vitality is cast at level 3 for the full +9 CON, roll D100. On a result of 00, the effect becomes permanent: those 9 points are added to the target's CON for good. The target may never again be the subject of this spell, and they must permanently subtract 9 points from their other characteristics in any combination, adjusting any values calculated from those characteristics.

### Rend the Heart

**Cost:** 10 PP + 1 permanent POW
**Range:** Sight
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer holds out a hand toward a target in line of sight, opens it as if pulling on something with the fingers, and tries to tear the target's heart through their breastbone.

The target makes a POW resistance roll (§5.3.5) - the sorcerer's current PP against the target's POW.

**On a failure**, the target's chest bursts outward in a shower of blood; their heart tears free of the ribs and flies through the air to the sorcerer's open palm "as a bit of steel leaps to the magnet" (Howard). The target takes **4D6 damage**, treats the wound as a critical hit per §A1 if they survive, and is at -10% to all rolls for the next round even if they live. A target reduced to 0 HP or below dies on the spot.

**On a success**, the target feels an awful constriction in their chest. They take 1D3 damage from the scrape of the working, and a thin trickle of blood may show at the corner of their mouth. They know - and any witnesses know - what the sorcerer just attempted.

**The cost is not a casual one.** 10 PP is more than most sorcerers carry; a sorcerer who casts this exhausts their pool to near-zero, and the 1 permanent POW is gone for good. A sorcerer who casts Rend the Heart twice in a campaign has materially diminished themselves. This is signature working - what the dread sorcerer does to demonstrate they are not to be approached, and what they pay for the privilege.

**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Wraps the sorcerer or a touched target in sorcerous protection functioning as armour: +1 AP per level (max +4 AP at level 4). Stacks with worn armour. Appears as a faint darkening of the air around the target.

### Silent Step

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Enhances the target's Move Quietly skill by +20% per level (cumulative): level 1 = +20%, level 2 = +40%, level 3 = +60%, level 4 = +80%. Footfalls fall on the air; hinges do not creak under the touched hand; even the rustle of leather and cloth quiets to nothing.

### Skin of the Beast

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** Self
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer transforms into a single specific animal form, chosen at character creation when the spell is first learned. Common totems: wolf, eagle, serpent, great cat, raven, otter. The form is the sorcerer's totem - a sorcerer who has learned Skin of the Beast as the wolf cannot take eagle form without learning the spell anew with a different focus and a new ritual.

While transformed:

- The sorcerer's physical characteristics (STR, CON, SIZ, DEX) become those of the animal type (typical specimen, not exceptional).
- The sorcerer retains their own INT and POW.
- They have full physical capabilities of the form - a wolf bites, an eagle flies, a serpent strikes and constricts.
- They cannot speak, cast sorcery, or use tools.
- Their clothing and equipment transform into the animal's hide or feathers and reform when the spell ends.

Learning Skin of the Beast for the first time in a campaign typically involves a brief ritual incorporating the totem animal's blood, hide, or sacred ground at the GM's discretion.

### Slumber of the Lotus

**Cost:** 2 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Sight
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** 1D6 rounds (level 1), 1D6 minutes (level 2), or 1D6 hours (level 3)

The target must fail a POW resistance roll or fall into supernatural sleep for the rolled duration.

The sleeper does not wake to ordinary noise, conversation in the room, or being moved gently. They wake immediately if struck for any damage, vigorously shaken, or doused with cold water. The sorcerer cannot maintain the slumber through repeated castings; once awoken, the target is immune to further Slumber of the Lotus from the same sorcerer for 24 hours.

The spell's name comes from the Hyperborean lotus said to grow only in graveyards, but no actual lotus is required. The sorcerer's gestures and whispered words are enough.

### Smothering Mist

**Cost:** 3 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Sight (the sorcerer must see the area where the mist will form)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds, or until dispersed

A luminous green mist forms at a point in the sorcerer's line of sight and rolls outward in a sluggish, serpentine cloud. The mist is heavier than air; it slides through bars, under doors, through cracks and keyholes. Open windows, strong winds, large fires, and Veil-of-the-Ancient-style wards can shape or block it, at the GM's discretion.

The cloud's reach scales with level:

- **Level 1:** roughly a small room (4 metres across).
- **Level 2:** a large chamber or a short corridor (10 metres across).
- **Level 3:** a great hall or a city alley (20 metres across).

Any breathing creature wholly within the mist at the end of a round must make a Stamina roll (CON × 5). On a failure, they fall to their knees retching and lose 1D6 HP. On a second consecutive failure (next round, still in the mist), they fall unconscious. On a third, they die.

A character who holds their breath delays the resistance roll entirely until they next breathe. A character at one of the cloud's edges may make a Difficult Stamina roll to fight through the mist toward open air.

Creatures that do not breathe - the bound dead, certain demons - ignore the mist entirely.

### The Sorcerer's Gaze

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** The sorcerer's gaze must meet the target's eyes (typically within a few metres)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** A single command, then ends

The sorcerer locks eyes with the target. The target makes a POW resistance roll (§5.3.5) - the sorcerer's current PP against the target's POW. On a success, the target shakes off the working and is immune to further Sorcerer's Gaze from the same caster for 24 hours.

On a failure, the sorcerer may speak a single command of no more than one sentence. The target obeys to the best of their ability, and only that command. The spell then ends.

**The command may direct the target to:**

- Surrender a weapon, drop an object, hand over a key
- Open a door, leave a room, kneel, stand still
- Speak truthfully about a single matter
- Stop attacking, lower their guard, fetch a specific person

**The command may not direct the target to harm themselves.** This is absolute. A command that would cause the target to take self-harming action - turn the weapon on themselves, walk into an obvious lethal hazard, drink something they know is poison, attack a foe they recognise as a killer - simply fails. The target stares back at the sorcerer, the spell expended, no compulsion taking hold.

A command that would cause the target to betray a Passion at 70% or higher (revealing their captain's secret, abandoning a sworn duty, killing a friend) permits a new POW resistance roll at -20% to refuse.

A target who breaks the Gaze - closing their eyes, looking away, being struck blind - immediately fails any partially-resolved command and is freed.

Witnesses who see the Gaze unmistakably know sorcery is being worked. The target's stillness and the sorcerer's stare are theatrical and obvious.

### Soul Walk

**Cost:** 4 PP
**Range:** Any location the sorcerer has personally visited, regardless of distance; or any location of a person the sorcerer has bound by name and blood (a sworn enemy, a blood relative, a sworn ally)
**Cast Time:** 10 minutes
**Duration:** POW minutes

The sorcerer's body falls into deep trance - indistinguishable from death in casual examination, breathing only once a minute, heart slowed to the slowest of beats. The spirit travels to the chosen distant location and manifests there as a translucent figure of the sorcerer themselves: visible, audible, recognisable. The projection wears whatever the sorcerer was wearing when the trance began.

While projected, the sorcerer can:

- Speak and be heard normally
- See, hear, and observe the surroundings
- Move freely about the distant location
- Pass through walls, doors, and physical barriers
- Frighten, intimidate, command, threaten, bargain, or beguile

While projected, the sorcerer **cannot**:

- Touch, strike, hold, or pick up any physical object
- Cast spells through the projection
- Be touched, struck, or restrained by any mortal means at the projection site

The body, meanwhile, lies wherever it was when the trance began. While projected, the sorcerer is unaware of the physical world around the body and cannot perceive bodily senses. Anything done to the body during the projection happens with the full vulnerability of the nightly trance (§4.3). A killing blow to the body ends the projection instantly and the sorcerer dies. **The sorcerer cannot return early to defend the body** - the working binds for its full duration unless the body itself is destroyed.

Returning to the body at the end of the duration is automatic; the spirit snaps back at the speed of thought, and the sorcerer wakes after a full round of disorientation (acts last in the round, no parry or dodge).

Witnesses at the projection site who succeed at a Sense or Insight roll recognise that the figure is sorcerous projection, not flesh. Iron weapons passing through the projected figure cause it no harm, but make a sound like a struck bell - audible at considerable distance.

### Stamp of Doom

**Cost:** 6 PP
**Range:** Sight (the sorcerer must see the point where the working will fall)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous (the destruction is permanent; the working itself ends with the cast)

The sorcerer raises a foot and stamps. Where the gesture lands - chosen by the sorcerer, anywhere within sight - the earth answers: a fissure opens, a cliff-face calves away, a wall splits down its length, a roof beam cracks and falls. The working is one of those particular pieces of stone or stonework, not the whole mountain.

The sorcerer may target one of:

- **A length of cliff or ledge** up to 10 metres long. Anyone standing on the affected stone makes a Difficult Agility roll (DEX × 5, halved) to leap clear. Failure: the character falls with the stone, taking damage per §7.7.1 (Falling).
- **A stone wall, stone roof, or stone bridge** up to 4 metres across. Anyone beneath or upon makes a Difficult Agility roll to dodge the collapse. Failure: 4D6 falling-masonry damage.
- **A fissure in solid ground** roughly 5 metres long and 2 metres wide. Anyone standing on the line makes a Difficult Agility roll to leap clear. Failure: they fall into the fissure (depth per the terrain - typically a few metres in normal ground, but in mountain country possibly far more).

The working affects natural stone, worked masonry, packed earth, and similar. It does not affect metal, wood beams, sorcerously enchanted stone (a warded wall resists), or living flesh - the spell ruptures terrain, not bodies.

Witnesses who survive the collapse recognise the gesture and the stamp for what they are. This is sorcery, not natural geology.

### Still the Mind

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Sight
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target must fail a POW resistance roll or be unable to cast spells, plan coherently, or perform any task requiring concentration. They may still fight and move on instinct. At the start of each round after the first, the target may attempt a new POW resistance roll to shake the effect free.

### The Sundering Hand

**Cost:** 2 PP
**Range:** Touch (the sorcerer must lay an open hand on the barrier)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer presses an open hand to a lock, hinge, latch, bar, chain, gate, door, or similar mechanical barrier. The barrier ruptures: wood splinters, bronze warps, iron bends, chains snap. A prison door buckles inward; a portcullis crumples; a strongbox flies open.

The working affects a single point of mechanism or fastening - the lock, the hinges, the bar, the chain - which is the weak point the sorcerer targets. A single Sundering Hand can break:

- A lock or padlock of any mortal craftsmanship
- The hinges or bolt of a single door, gate, or window
- A length of chain, fetter, or shackle
- The seal on a sealed tomb or vault
- A grate, grille, or window-bar

The working does **not** affect:

- Living flesh, bone, or armour worn on a person
- Stone, masonry, or earth (use Stamp of Doom)
- Sorcerously enchanted barriers - the Sundering Hand fails against wards and warded locks, the PP are spent, and the sorcerer's hand may ache or smoulder briefly
- Doors so massive that the failure of one mechanism does not open them (a great temple door braced from within by twenty men, a city gate sealed with multiple bars)

The working makes noise. The bronze bolt twisting sings like a struck bell; the wood splinters explosively; the chain snaps with a sound like a whip-crack. Witnesses in adjacent rooms hear it.

### Summon Demon

**Cost:** 4 PP for the invocation itself; the bargain and binding that follow have their own prices (see below)
**Range:** Self / Sight (the demon manifests at the prepared circle)
**Cast Time:** 4 hours minimum (preparation and invocation)
**Duration:** Until released or binding broken

Summoning is an extended ritual followed by a negotiation. The sorcerer prepares a circle, invokes a demon by the appropriate formula, and calls it into physical presence. The demon arrives unbound, having agreed to nothing - that negotiation follows. The demon's appearance, demands, and behaviour are the GM's domain (§11); the steps below cover the sorcerer's working.

**Preparation.** Before invoking a demon, the sorcerer must:

- **Prepare a summoning circle** - a physical boundary drawn with appropriate materials (chalk, ash, blood, ground bone) and inscribed with binding formulae. Takes one hour per spell level of the Summon Demon spell (minimum 4 hours). Cannot be improvised under pressure.
- **Research the target** - know the demon's nature (possessing spirit, flesh-eater, chaos-entity), its approximate power (Witch Sight helps), and its true name if available. Summoning without a true name is possible, but the demon arrives without the obligation a name implies.
- **Gather materials** - §11 describes what a specific demon prefers. Preparing without the appropriate materials imposes penalties on the binding roll.

**The invocation.** The sorcerer spends 4 PP and completes the minimum 4-hour working. The demon appears at the circle's edge; whether it enters is its own choice. With a true name, the demon is compelled to appear and hear the sorcerer's offer. Without a name, it may simply not come (the GM rolls in secret). PP contributed via Chain of Being may pay the summoning cost and the subsequent binding.

**The bargain.** The demon has desires. A lesser demon may want blood, PP, a body to wear, or a task done. A greater demon wants permanent POW, a future service, or a third party delivered. Both parties state their offer and demand; either may refuse, counter-offer, or accept. The GM may call for rolls on persuasion, willpower, or detecting deception. The bargain, once agreed, binds both parties in the demon's own fashion.

**The binding.** To secure the demon's service beyond a one-time deal, it must be bound. Binding costs 1 permanent POW, and the demon must fail a POW resistance roll against the sorcerer's current PP total. If the demon succeeds, it is not bound - and is usually unhappy.

Three forms of binding exist:

- _Flesh binding_ - bound to the sorcerer's service directly; follows commands; cannot harm the sorcerer.
- _Object binding_ - bound into a weapon, amulet, or container; power accessible through the object; cannot act independently.
- _Place binding_ - bound to a location; cannot leave but has great power within it.

Once bound, the demon's true name is surrendered to the sorcerer, allowing recall and dismissal at will (1 PP each).

**Release and breaking.** A binding ends in one of four ways:

- **The sorcerer releases the demon.** Conscious act; costs 1 PP; the true name loses binding power.
- **The demon is destroyed.** Binding ends immediately; the permanent POW cost is not recovered.
- **The sorcerer dies.** Active bindings end; object-bound and place-bound demons may persist.
- **Another sorcerer learns the true name and rebinds.** The demon now serves the new binder.

What the demon does when freed depends on the entity's nature, the terms of the original bargain, and how it was treated. §11 has guidance.

### Suppleness of the Serpent

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target's DEX is increased by +3 per level: +3 at level 1, +6 at level 2, +9 at level 3. Each level also increases the target's Dodge skill by +6% for the duration. The boosted DEX temporarily increases the Agility (DEX x5) roll and any DEX-derived skill category modifiers.

**The permanence roll.** When Suppleness of the Serpent is cast at level 3 for the full +9 DEX, roll D100. On a result of 00, the effect becomes permanent: those 9 points are added to the target's DEX for good. The target may never again be the subject of this spell, and they must permanently subtract 9 points from their other characteristics in any combination, adjusting any values calculated from those characteristics.

### Unbreakable Bulwark

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Cast on a single shield, this spell adds +1 AP per level (max +4 AP at level 4) to the shield's protection value for the duration. Stacks freely with any other armour the wielder wears. The spell may also be cast on any object being used as an improvised shield - a cooking pot, a heavy plank, a tavern table - for the same effect, though the GM may rule that an improvised shield does not survive heavy use even with the spell active.

### Undo Sorcery

**Cost:** 1 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Touch
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer ends or weakens an active spell or sorcerous effect. The target spell must be present and identifiable: a curse on a person, a lingering ward, a binding still in force, a transformation in progress, an enchantment on an object. The caster must know what kind of spell they are dispelling, though they need not know the spell themselves.

**Resolving the dispel.** Subtract the level of Undo Sorcery from the level of the target spell. If Undo Sorcery's level equals or exceeds the target, the target spell ends. If Undo Sorcery's level is lower, the target spell weakens by that amount but otherwise continues - a Curse of Sorcery struck by Undo Sorcery 2 would lose two levels of effect but remain in force.

If the original caster has placed a Refutation on the working, that contest resolves first (see Refutation).

**What Undo Sorcery can break:** active spell effects still running, persistent curses, bindings (a bound demon may be released - the GM should adjudicate carefully), enchantments on objects.

**What it cannot break:** spells that have already taken effect and ended; permanent characteristic loss; destroyed objects, limbs, or lives.

### Vampire's Kiss

**Cost:** 2 PP per level (max 3)
**Range:** Touch (the sorcerer must touch both the target and the beneficiary)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

Drains vitality from a touched target and transfers it to the sorcerer or a third party in contact with them. The target makes a resistance roll (§5.3.5) - the sorcerer's current PP against the target's POW. On a success, the spell fails entirely and no HP are drained or restored. On a failure, drain HP from the target and restore the same number of HP to the beneficiary:

- **Level 1:** drain 1D6 HP, restore 1D6 HP
- **Level 2:** drain 2D6 HP, restore 2D6 HP
- **Level 3:** drain 3D6 HP, restore 3D6 HP

The beneficiary cannot be healed above their maximum HP; any excess is lost.

### Veil of the Ancient

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** Touch (the sorcerer must walk the perimeter as they cast)
**Cast Time:** As long as it takes to walk the perimeter
**Duration:** Until triggered

As the spell is cast, the sorcerer walks and defines a line of up to 15 metres in length, or a circle of up to 15 metres in diameter. Once the working is set, no further effort is required to maintain it - the veil waits silently for an intruder, however long that takes. A little-travelled path or sealed sanctum might hold its veil for years before anyone trips it.

The first time any creature the sorcerer has not exempted - any enemy, any stranger, anyone unexpected - crosses the line or enters the circle, the sorcerer becomes immediately and silently aware of the breach. The veil does not stop the intruder, sound an audible alarm, or harm them: it only tells the sorcerer they have been crossed.

The veil is visible to Witch Sight and similar workings. Undo Sorcery at level 3 or higher unmakes it.

### Vermin's Eye

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Sight (one rat, mouse, or similar vermin visible to the sorcerer at the moment of casting)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer takes control of one rat, mouse, or similar vermin's field of vision and sees through its eyes. The animal must be within eyesight when the spell is cast; once cast, the spell continues for its duration even if the vermin scurries out of sight. The sorcerer may steer the vermin's gaze - turning its head, drawing its attention to a specific direction - but the animal's path is its own. If no vermin is in sight when the sorcerer casts, the spell does not take hold and the PP are spent.

While the working holds, the sorcerer's body stands still and unseeing.

### Whisper Across Distance

**Cost:** 2 PP per level (max 4)
**Range:** Scales with level (see below)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** A brief mental exchange

The sorcerer speaks one short sentence per level directly into the mind of a chosen target. The target must be someone the sorcerer has met in person and clearly recognises. They hear the sorcerer's voice in their own thoughts and may reply mentally during the exchange - the conversation lasts only a few seconds of subjective time.

Range scales with level:

- Level 1: line of sight (cross a battlefield, signal a confederate across a hall).
- Level 2: within POW kilometres (signal an ally across a city).
- Level 3: within POW x 100 kilometres (signal a contact across a region).
- Level 4: any distance on the same world (signal across a continent or sea).

The target may refuse the contact - choosing to ignore the voice in their head requires a Difficult POW roll. A target ignoring the call still knows it came from the sorcerer. The exchange is private; only the sorcerer and target hear it.

### Witch Sight

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Range:** Self (100 metres of sight)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer's vision opens to the sorcerous current. While the spell holds, the caster perceives:

- **The relative POW of any creature in sight**, expressed in five bands compared to the caster's own POW: _much stronger_ (more than double); _stronger_ (up to double); _equal_ (within one or two points); _weaker_ (down to half); or _much weaker_ (less than half).
- **Invisible beings, elementals, and spirits** that share the same plane, including the emanations of bound demons.
- **Sorcerous trails** where sorcerers, demons, elementals, or other sorcerous entities have recently passed - faint to the trained eye, fading over hours.

Witch Sight is almost always used before a summoning. If the caster has Witch Sight active when casting Summon Demon, the GM should roll the demon's POW openly and announce the result - the sorcerer can decide whether they are about to call something they cannot bind. Without Witch Sight, summoning is a leap in the dark.

If an artifact or object holds a bound demon, elemental, or other sentient entity, Witch Sight allows the bearer to mentally converse with the entity, provided it is willing. The process takes a full day of focused study.

Witch Sight is cancelled by Undo Sorcery at equal or greater level.

### Wolf's Ear

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Range:** Self
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer's hearing becomes supernaturally precise: whispered conversation at 100 metres, individual footsteps in a crowd, heartbeats and breathing in adjacent rooms. Imposes -20% to tasks requiring visual concentration. At the GM's option, sudden loud noises cause 1D6 PP loss.
