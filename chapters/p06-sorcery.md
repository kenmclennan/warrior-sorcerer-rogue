# P6. Sorcery

> _"He was a sorcerer and men feared him and he was doomed and he knew it and the knowledge had become a kind of pleasure."_
> _- Michael Moorcock_

This chapter is for sorcerer characters. It covers what you must do to become a sorcerer, how spells work, what every casting costs you, what iron does to your power, how grimoires work, and the full spell list. The GM Book chapter G4 covers the other side of all this: what patrons actually want, what demons look like when they arrive, and what happens when a sorcerer crosses too far into the dark.

If you are not playing a sorcerer, you do not need this chapter - but your companions are reading it.

---

## 1. Becoming a Sorcerer

### 1.1 What a Sorcerer Is

A sorcerer is a character who has learned to reach into the sorcerous current - the ancient flow of power that runs beneath the world, older than any civilization, colder than any ocean. It is not a gift. It is not a birthright. It is knowledge, acquired at cost, from a source that was not interested in sharing.

Sorcerers in this world are rare. Most people who study the arcane arts learn only enough to know they cannot safely go further. A true sorcerer has gone further anyway.

### 1.2 Requirements

To become a sorcerer, a character must meet all three of the following conditions at character creation. A warrior or rogue who later pursues sorcery may meet these requirements during play with the GM's agreement.

**Power (POW 16 or higher).** The sorcerous current demands a minimum vital force. A character with POW 15 or below can own a grimoire, can even read it, but cannot draw power through the text. The spells simply will not come. This threshold is not negotiable.

**A sorcerer's profession or background.** The character must have pursued sorcery through a recognised path: formal apprenticeship under an established sorcerer, years alone in a dark library, a pact exchanged in a place that should not exist, or initiation into a small and secretive order. Characters from purely martial or criminal backgrounds who have never been near sorcerous instruction cannot qualify, however high their POW. See P2 §5 for the professions that qualify.

**A grimoire.** Every sorcerer begins with at least one grimoire containing their starting spells. Without access to written sorcerous knowledge, no learning is possible. Starting sorcerers know a number of spell levels equal to half their INT (round up), inscribed in their grimoire and memorised for immediate use. See §5 for grimoire rules.

### 1.3 Learning Methods

How a sorcerer acquired their knowledge shapes who they are as a character. These are not mechanical distinctions - each learning method produces the same sorcerer - but they are narrative ones. They shape the character's debts, their enemies, and the style of their casting signature.

**Apprenticeship.** The character studied under an established sorcerer - a court mage, a desert hermit, a witch of the marshes - for years. The master taught, demanded, withheld, and eventually the student took what they could carry and left. The apprentice's grimoire is usually a copy of part of the master's, made in secret or given reluctantly.

**The Dark Library.** The character found a collection of sorcerous texts - in a ruin, in a temple archive, in the possession of a recently dead scholar - and taught themselves. Slow, dangerous, prone to misunderstanding. The self-taught sorcerer's grimoire may be incomplete, annotated, or in languages they only partially understand.

**The Infernal Pact.** The character bargained for knowledge - with a demon, a patron entity, a chaos power, something that walked out of the old time and offered terms. What they offered in return is the question. Characters who take a patron compact at character creation have usually arrived here.

**Stolen Power.** The character was a warrior, thief, or scholar who encountered sorcery at close range - perhaps too close - and found it had changed them. Their grimoire was stolen, taken from a dead sorcerer's hands, or is a single tablet recovered from a ruin. They are self-taught from stolen texts and may not know the proper forms.

### 1.4 The Sorcerous Heritage Option

At character creation, a sorcerer-archetype PC may choose to have invested some part of their vitality into their arcane capacity. For every 2 points permanently reduced from any combination of STR, CON, or CHA at creation, the character gains 1 point of POW. This trade is irreversible - the sorcerer who sacrificed their health or beauty for the power to see through walls has already made the bargain.

This option is available only at character creation and only to sorcerer-archetype PCs. It cannot be chosen to push POW above 21.

The sorcerous heritage trade is visible to those who know what to look for: a slight hollowness about the eyes, a chill in the handshake, an unnatural stillness. These are not wounds. They are the signs of a transaction.

## 2. Casting Spells

### 2.1 Automatic Success

Sorcery does not fail through incompetence. A sorcerer who casts a spell they know, who has the Power Points to pay for it, who has the time and the quiet to cast it - the spell works. There is no skill roll to hit a casting target, no chance of fumbling the formula. Sorcery is knowledge. Either you know it or you don't.

Failure modes exist, but they come from outside the caster: interruption during casting, a target who resists, iron in the sorcerer's kit disrupting the current. They are not failures of the caster's technique.

This is why sorcery is feared. A warrior can fumble. A sorcerer who begins casting finishes - unless something stops them.

### 2.2 Power Point Cost

Every spell costs Power Points equal to its intensity rating, paid at the moment of casting. Intensity 1 costs 1 PP; intensity 3 costs 3 PP; and so on. The specific cost for each spell is listed in the spell entry.

Some spells have fixed intensity (they always cost the same amount). Others scale with intensity - casting at a higher level produces a stronger effect and costs proportionally more PP. Intensity scaling is noted in the spell entry.

PP are shared with Fate Point spends and critical-buy conversions (P4 §3 and §7). A sorcerer who spends PP on a killing blow deflection has that much less for spells. A sorcerer who spends PP on a devastating critical buy has drained the same pool. Every draw comes from the same well.

If a sorcerer lacks the PP to complete a casting, the spell fails and any PP already gathered for the working disperses. There is no partial effect.

See P4 §6 for Power Point totals and recovery.

### 2.3 Time to Cast

A spell takes one combat round to cast and takes effect during Phase Two of that round (see P5 §1.2), before melee actions in Phase Three. While casting, the sorcerer may do nothing else - no movement, no attacks, no other actions.

Spells that are described as requiring more than one round (ritual workings, demon summoning) are noted in their spell entry. These longer workings cannot be done mid-combat.

### 2.4 Memorised Spells and the Grimoire

A sorcerer can hold a number of spell levels in active memory equal to their INT characteristic. These are the spells they can cast immediately, without consulting their grimoire. Spells beyond this limit are stored in the grimoire only and require time to review before casting.

**Readying a spell from the grimoire:** requires a number of uninterrupted rounds equal to the spell's level. This cannot be done in combat unless the sorcerer is secure from all interruption.

**Readying costs nothing mechanically** beyond time. The sorcerer is simply refreshing their focus on a formula they have studied before.

Starting sorcerers typically memorise their most-used spells and keep others in reserve. The choice of what to memorise is a meaningful one - two sorcerers with identical spell lists will feel different based on what they have ready.

### 2.5 The Casting Signature

Every sorcerer's magic is distinctive. At character creation, define your casting signature: a specific combination of gestures, words, sounds, smells, or foci that accompanies your spells. One sorcerer's magic smells of hot copper and is accompanied by a subsonic hum. Another's fills the air with drifting ash and the sorcerer's shadow always falls the wrong way.

The signature is not a mechanical element - it does not affect casting. It is the visible edge of the sorcerer's relationship with the power they are drawing from, and it makes magic theatrical. Per design.md §2.2: visual drama is not optional. Magic should be seen.

A sorcerer can attempt to suppress their signature with effort, but the signs tend to leak through at moments of strong power use.

### 2.6 Interruption During Casting

If a sorcerer takes damage or is subjected to a sudden shock while casting, make a Difficult Idea roll (INT/2, round up). On a success, the casting holds. On a failure, the working collapses and the PP already committed are lost.

If the sorcerer is knocked unconscious while casting, the spell automatically fails and all PP for that working are lost.

This rule gives enemies a reason to target the sorcerer first. It also means that a sorcerer must trust their companions to keep the pressure off while they work.

### 2.7 Spell Duration

Unless the spell entry states otherwise, a spell's duration is the caster's POW in combat rounds. A sorcerer with POW 17 maintaining a spell maintains it for seventeen rounds - just under four minutes. Spells described as instantaneous (single-use effects like damage or transformation) have no duration; they happen and are done.

A sorcerer may end a spell early by an act of will, which costs nothing.

### 2.8 Resistance and Contested Spells

Many spells that target another creature must overcome that creature's innate resistance. The mechanic is the Resistance Table (P4 §4.4): the sorcerer's current PP total (after paying the casting cost) is the active characteristic; the target's POW is the passive characteristic.

On a successful resistance roll, the spell takes effect. On a failure, the spell is blocked. The PP are still spent.

Winning a POW-contest resistance roll against a target whose POW equals or exceeds your own earns an experience checkmark toward improving POW (P4 §8.5).

## 3. The Cost Trio

Sorcery has three costs, operating at different scales. They are not alternatives - all three are real and in play simultaneously. A sorcerer manages all three.

### 3.1 Power Points

The immediate cost. Every spell costs PP. This is the moment-to-moment accounting of sorcery: how much power you have, how much you're spending, how close you are to the bottom.

At zero PP the sorcerer collapses. Between full and zero, every spend is a real decision: this spell or that Fate Point spend; this critical buy or that working. The pool is not large. The demands on it are many.

See P4 §6 for the full Power Point rules.

### 3.2 Patron Score (Optional)

A sorcerer who has entered into a compact with a patron entity - a demon, an old power, a chaos lord, a dead god still hungry - maintains a Patron Score that tracks the state of the relationship. The score rises when the sorcerer serves the patron's interests; it is a number that means both "how much the patron has invested in me" and "how much I owe."

**Taking a patron is optional.** Not all sorcerers have one. Unbonded sorcerers pay their PP and take their Corruption and owe nothing to any named power. That is a different kind of sorcerer - more independent, but with no patron reserves to draw on in the dark moments.

**Starting Patron Score:** A sorcerer who begins with a patron compact starts at a Patron Score of 1D6+5 (roughly 6-11). The score grows over adventures as the sorcerer performs deeds that please the patron. It rarely decreases.

**Benefits of a Patron Score:**

- **PP Reserve.** When the sorcerer's own PP pool reaches 0, they may call on the patron for an emergency draw. They may draw up to 1/10 of their current Patron Score (round up) as emergency PP. This may be done up to three times per session. Each use increases the Patron Score by 1 (the debt deepens).
- **Patron Intercession.** When the sorcerer's situation is truly desperate, they may invoke the patron by true name as a full action. Make an allegiance roll (D100 vs. current Patron Score). On a Critical (matching dice within the Patron Score), the patron intervenes directly - a demon servant appears, a curse falls on enemies, a door opens that should not. The form of intervention is the GM's call. A successful Patron Intercession always increases the Patron Score by 2D6. The patron has spent something, and the debt has grown.

**The obligation:** A high Patron Score draws the patron's attention. The GM Book chapter G4 describes what patrons want, what they do when offended, and how the relationship escalates over a campaign. From the player's side: know that the patron is watching, that the score climbing means the strings are tightening, and that a Patron Score above 50 tends to come with increasingly specific demands.

**Earning Patron Score:** After any adventure in which the sorcerer performed actions that served the patron's interests, the GM may call for a patron test. Roll D100. If the roll is less than the current Patron Score, the score increases by 1D6. The GM Book defines what a given patron values - each patron has its own trigger list.

### 3.3 Corruption

The permanent cost. Every sorcerer has a Corruption track, expressed as a score from its starting value down toward zero. This is the part of you that sorcery eats.

**Starting Corruption:** A sorcerer's Corruption track begins at POW x 5. A sorcerer with POW 17 starts with a Corruption track of 85.

**The Corruption maximum cap:** Deep arcane knowledge lowers a sorcerer's maximum Corruption ceiling permanently. Each 10% of Knowledge (Blasphemous Lore) a sorcerer possesses reduces their Corruption maximum by 10 points. A sorcerer with Knowledge (Blasphemous Lore) 30% has a maximum Corruption of (starting value - 30). As they study darker texts, the ceiling drops. More experienced sorcerers are closer to the edge even when they have not yet fallen far. The knowledge itself eats capacity.

**Losing Corruption:** The table below lists the circumstances that cost Corruption. When a triggering event occurs, make a D100 roll against the sorcerer's current Corruption. On a success, the sorcerer sustains the lower loss. On a failure, they sustain the higher.

| Trigger                                       | Corruption Loss (success/failure) |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Casting a spell of intensity 3 or lower       | 0 / 1                             |
| Casting a spell of intensity 4 or higher      | 1 / 1D3                           |
| Summoning a lesser demon (player side)        | 1 / 1D6                           |
| Binding a demon (any type)                    | 1D3 / 1D6                         |
| Casting past PP exhaustion (see §3.1)         | 1D6 / 2D6                         |
| Patron Intercession (the patron manifesting)  | 1 / 1D3                           |
| Encountering a Greater Demon unbound          | 1D3 / 1D6                         |
| Direct contact with an Old Power              | 1D10 / 1D100                      |
| Suffering a sorcerous curse or transformation | 1D6 / 2D10                        |

**Desensitisation:** Repeated exposure to the same ordinary source of Corruption (encountering the same class of lesser demon, casting the same minor spells) eventually loses its impact. The GM may reduce or waive the Corruption cost for situations the sorcerer has faced many times before. Greater Demons and Old Powers never desensitise.

**Corruption thresholds:** The track has three important levels.

_At half starting value (the Breaking Point):_ if the sorcerer loses Corruption points equal to or greater than their Breaking Point (half starting value) in a single session, they suffer an **acute episode**. Roll 1D6 on the Acute Episode table and 1D8 on the Duration table.

| 1D6 | Acute Episode                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **Fugue Possession.** The sorcerer stares, unmoving, as something looks out through their eyes. They are present but not controlling their body. Duration applies.                                           |
| 2   | **Speaking in the Dead Tongue.** The sorcerer speaks only in a dead arcane language they may or may not know. They can still understand others. Duration applies.                                            |
| 3   | **Demonic Certainty.** The sorcerer becomes convinced they are being surveilled by a specific supernatural entity. They may still act but with paranoia-driven decisions. Duration applies.                  |
| 4   | **Offering Compulsion.** The sorcerer is gripped by the need to make an offering - of blood, of something precious, of something irreversible. They resist with a Difficult Willpower roll or are compelled. |
| 5   | **The Sorcerer's Mark.** A permanent physical mark appears - an arcane brand, a changed eye colour, scales along the wrist, a cold spot at the temples. This mark does not fade when the episode ends.       |
| 6   | **Ecstatic Clarity.** For the duration, the sorcerer casts all spells at no PP cost. They also cannot be reasoned with and will not stop.                                                                    |

| 1D8 | Duration                |
| --- | ----------------------- |
| 1   | One combat round        |
| 2   | Five minutes (one turn) |
| 3   | One hour                |
| 4   | Two hours               |
| 5   | Twelve hours            |
| 6   | One day                 |
| 7   | One week                |
| 8   | Two weeks               |

_At Corruption 25 or lower:_ the sorcerer is visibly marked by their practice. They cannot easily pass as an ordinary person. Strangers feel unease in their presence. Animals will not approach willingly. The casting signature has become involuntary - it appears even in ordinary moments.

_At Corruption 0:_ the sorcerer is lost. They are either retired from play as an NPC or - at the GM's discretion - become an entity under GM control that may still speak with their voice but no longer has their goals. Reaching zero Corruption is the end of the character's story. It is not death, but it is worse.

**Recovering Corruption:** Two paths exist.

- **Destroying the source.** A sorcerer who destroys or permanently banishes the demonic entity or dark force responsible for a Corruption loss may recover up to half the Corruption that source threatened. The GM sets the exact amount.
- **Ritual substances.** Certain alchemical and herbal preparations - the black lotus, the white-blossomed plant grown on a corpse, specific ritual fumes burned in a sealed room - can restore small amounts of Corruption. These are rare, expensive, and not available in most markets. The GM sets availability and cost.

## 4. Iron Disrupts Sorcery

Unenchanted iron is inimical to the sorcerous current. A sorcerer who carries iron feels its interference in every casting - a wrongness in the flow, a place where the formula catches on something cold and hard.

**Rule:** Each 1 ENC of unenchanted iron a sorcerer carries imposes a **-5% penalty on all sorcery rolls** - including Resistance Table rolls made as part of a spell's effect.

**What counts:** Iron weapons, iron armour, iron chains, iron manacles. Large iron fittings (iron-reinforced doors the sorcerer is carrying, iron straps on a chest borne on the back) count. Small iron fittings - buckles, rivets - on primarily leather or bronze items do not, unless the GM rules the proportion is significant.

**What does not count:** Bronze, copper, leather, bone, wood, stone, and all other materials carry no penalty. Enchanted iron - iron consecrated through proper sorcerous ritual, harmonised with the sorcerous current - does not impose the penalty.

**Practical:** A sorcerer who carries an iron sword (ENC 1) and an iron helm (ENC 1) suffers -10% on all sorcery rolls. A sorcerer in iron mail (ENC 9) suffers -45%. Most sorcerers travel in bronze or leather, keep iron items at arm's length, and leave the heavy martial kit to their companions. The iron-vs-sorcery tension is part of what makes sorcerer characters look different at the table.

See P7 §11 for the ENC system, P7 §5 for material tiers and iron pricing, and P7 §12.1 for the full iron-vs-sorcery rules in the equipment context.

## 5. Grimoires

A sorcerer's grimoire is the physical record of their knowledge. Without a grimoire, a sorcerer cannot learn new spells and cannot recover spells they have forgotten. The grimoire is a target for enemies and the source of the sorcerer's power: losing it is a catastrophe.

### 5.1 Grimoire Formats

Sorcerous knowledge can be recorded in any medium that can bear inscription and survive handling. The following formats are standard in the ancient world:

**Codex (bound manuscript).** Papyrus or parchment leaves bound between wooden or bone covers. Portable, moderately durable, can hold many spells. Most expensive.

**Scroll.** A papyrus or parchment roll, stored in a leather or ivory case. Good for a few spells; easier to conceal than a codex. Fragile if the case is lost.

**Clay Tablet.** Heavy, durable, fireproof. A tablet can hold one or two spells. Sorcerers who inscribe on tablets often have a collection; the tablets must be carried or stored.

**Carved Stone.** The format of old temple sorcery. A stone slab or carved monolith bearing a working in relief. Cannot be carried; the sorcerer must travel to the stone to study it. Effectively permanent.

**Prepared Hide.** Tanned leather or dried skin bearing inscription. Portable, somewhat durable. Has a reputation for being the format of sorcerers who practice outside cities and civilisation.

**Knotted Rope.** An old sorcerous notation system in which spells are encoded in the pattern of knots. Extremely compact, completely unreadable to anyone who does not know the system. The same length of rope can hold a dozen spells or a single complex working, depending on the knotter's skill.

**Scribed Bone.** Individual bones - human or animal - bearing carved notation. Compact and durable. Regarded with suspicion by most people; sorcerers who carry bone grimoires tend not to show them to strangers.

### 5.2 Learning New Spells

A sorcerer learns new spells from grimoires. The process requires:

- **Access to the grimoire.** The sorcerer must be able to read it, which requires literacy in the grimoire's language. If the sorcerer's Language or Literacy skill in that tongue is below INT x5, they must make a successful Language roll to comprehend the spell as written.
- **Study time.** Learning a spell from a grimoire takes one week per spell level. This cannot be hurried.
- **Materials.** Learning requires peace, reasonable light, and the materials to copy the spell into the sorcerer's own grimoire. Writing materials for a codex-format copy cost approximately 2-5 Staters per spell.

**Research:** A sorcerer with access to multiple grimoires on a topic, or who is working from original sorcerous principles, may research new spells that are not already written down. Research takes one month per spell level and requires a Knowledge (Occult) roll of Difficulty determined by the GM (at minimum Difficult for any spell of level 3 or higher). Failure does not cost the time, but it does mean the sorcerer has hit a wall and must find another approach.

**The INT memory limit:** A sorcerer can hold a number of spell levels in active memory equal to their INT characteristic. When a sorcerer learns a new spell that would push them past this limit, they must choose which memorised spell to let go to background (held in the grimoire only, not immediately accessible). This is not forgetting - the spell remains in the grimoire - but it is no longer ready to cast without the readying time.

### 5.3 Scribing Your Own Grimoire

A sorcerer who knows how to write may copy their memorised spells into a new grimoire. Copying a spell takes one day per spell level. A sorcerer who is making their own grimoire from scratch must have the tools appropriate to the format (ink and parchment for a codex, a kiln for clay tablets, etc.).

If the original grimoire is damaged or lost, the sorcerer retains their memorised spells - but once a memorised spell is displaced, it is gone unless re-copied from a surviving grimoire or relearned from another source.

A sorcerer who has multiple grimoires has security. It is also a vulnerability: multiple volumes must be protected and transported.

## 6. The Spell List

The following spells are available to sorcerer PCs. This is not the entire range of sorcerous knowledge in the world - ritual workings, patron-specific powers, and spells known only to individual sorcerers exist in the GM's domain. This list is what a sorcerer can learn and cast independently.

Spells are listed alphabetically. Each entry includes:

- **Cost:** PP per casting (or per intensity level, if scaling)
- **Cast Time:** time required to complete the working
- **Duration:** how long the effect lasts; "Instantaneous" means the effect happens and ends
- **Cost Notes:** any Patron Score or Corruption gain associated with this specific spell (in addition to the standard casting Corruption from the table in §3.3)

---

### Bind the Raging Heart

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer bends another's will, inducing a state of uncontrolled violence in which they cannot distinguish friend from foe. The target must fail a POW resistance roll (P4 §4.4) or enter a berserker state for the duration: they attack the nearest creature to them, cannot parry or dodge, and cannot stop fighting even when mortally wounded. They do not feel pain.

When the spell ends, the target collapses unless their wounds were minor.

_Something looked out through those eyes and it was not the man they had known. It was older, and it did not care which of them it killed first._

**Cost Notes:** Casting at intensity 3 or higher increases Corruption by 1 (in addition to standard casting cost).

---

### Bird's Eye

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 3)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer sees through the eyes of a bird within range (intensity 1: line of sight; intensity 2: within 1 km; intensity 3: within 5 km). The sorcerer's own body stands still and unseeing while the working holds. The bird is unaware it is being used.

At intensity 3, the sorcerer may leap from one bird to another within range with a Difficult Idea roll.

_The raven landed on the gate arch, and somewhere a mile away a man stared at nothing and smiled at what it saw._

---

### Brazier of Power

**Cost:** 1 permanent POW + 4 PP per level of capacity
**Cast Time:** 4 hours ritual
**Duration:** Permanent

The sorcerer binds a portion of their permanent vital force into a physical object - a brazier, a stone, a sealed vessel, a carved monolith - creating a reservoir of PP. The reservoir holds PP equal to twice the sorcerer's normal PP maximum. Once created, the brazier can be recharged: the sorcerer spends 1 PP per round transferring power from their body to the reservoir.

A sorcerer may draw from the brazier as if drawing from their own pool, but only while in physical contact with it or within POW x2 metres.

If the brazier is destroyed, the permanent POW investment is lost. If the sorcerer dies, the brazier's stored power dissipates over POW hours.

_The heart of any true sorcerer's sanctum is the focus: the object that holds what they have poured into it. Touch it and feel what they sacrificed._

**Cost Notes:** Costs 1 permanent POW. Losing the brazier cannot restore this cost. Gaining a new brazier requires the full creation cost again.

---

### Chain of Being

**Cost:** 4 PP (from the sorcerer), plus PP contributed by participants
**Cast Time:** 1 round (all participants must be present and willing)
**Duration:** Until released

The sorcerer becomes the focal point of a PP chain, drawing on the vital force of up to eight willing participants who touch the sorcerer or each other in an unbroken line. Participating characters contribute PP to the sorcerer's working as if they were the caster. Participants who drain to 0 PP through their contribution fall unconscious immediately.

The sorcerer uses the combined PP pool for the linked working (usually a demon summoning or great ritual). Once the chain is released, participants recover PP normally.

_In the old stories, great summoners had a circle of volunteers - servants, students, the devout, the afraid - and they drew from each in turn. The circle always had fewer at its end than at its beginning._

**Cost Notes:** Participants who contribute more than half their PP in a single session each suffer 1 Corruption.

---

### Cloak of Night

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Enhances the target's Stealth skill: intensity 1 = +20%; intensity 2 = +40%; intensity 3 = +60%; intensity 4 = +80%. At intensity 4, the target becomes nearly invisible in dim light and completely invisible in darkness, audible only by breath and footfall.

The effect is precisely what it sounds like: the shadow thickens around the target, as if the night recognises one of its own.

_She had been standing there the whole time. He simply had not been able to see her._

---

### Curse of the Withered Hand

**Cost:** 4 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Permanent until broken

The sorcerer marks a target with a grotesque physical disfigurement - a hand that turns to a claw, an eye that clouds and weeps black, hair that falls and does not grow back, skin that takes on the texture of old bark. The target must fail a POW resistance roll (P4 §4.4). On failure, the curse sets.

The disfigurement is permanent and visible. The target takes a -20% penalty to all social interactions in any community that knows what the mark means. The mark also alerts any sorcerer who uses Witch Sight.

The curse can only be broken by Undo Sorcery at equal or greater intensity, or by killing the caster.

_"I will not kill you. I will make you something people cross the street to avoid."_

**Cost Notes:** Casting this spell increases Corruption by 1D3 (in addition to standard casting cost). Every sorcerer who casts it knows what they are becoming.

---

### Flaying Blade

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer sheathes a blade in sorcerous force, making it supernaturally keen. The enhanced weapon gains: intensity 1 = +1D6 damage; intensity 2 = +2D6 damage; intensity 3 = +3D6 damage; intensity 4 = +4D6 damage. The weapon also ignores mundane armour - magical armour (see Sorcerous Shield below) is not bypassed.

The weapon glows faintly during the working and leaves wounds that do not clot normally. A character who takes a Major Wound from a Flaying Blade-enhanced weapon loses 1 HP per round from the wound until it is treated with a First Aid roll.

_The blade sang through the shield and the arm behind it and none of them understood why until it was over._

---

### Hammer of the Gods

**Cost:** 3 PP per intensity (max 3)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer wraps a crushing weapon (mace, hammer, club, maul) in sorcerous force, adding thunderous impact. intensity 1 = +2D6 damage and any target hit must make a CON x5 roll or be knocked prone; intensity 2 = +3D6 damage and the knockdown roll is Difficult; intensity 3 = +4D6 damage, the knockdown roll is Difficult, and any target knocked prone also loses their next action.

The weapon makes a deep resonant sound on impact, audible at long range.

_It struck the bronze gate and the gate was simply gone. Not broken. Gone._

---

### Hell's Razor

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

As Flaying Blade, but for a slashing or piercing weapon. The enhanced weapon gains: intensity 1 = +1D6 damage; intensity 2 = +2D6 damage; intensity 3 = +3D6 damage; intensity 4 = +4D6 damage. As with Flaying Blade, the weapon ignores mundane armour and wounds bleed until treated.

Unlike Flaying Blade (designed for bludgeoning weapons), Hell's Razor is the more commonly known version and appears in most grimoires. The two spells cannot both be active on the same weapon simultaneously.

_Every man in the courtyard had seen a sword drawn before. Not one of them had seen a sword like that._

---

### Inescapable Bonds

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The target is seized by invisible bonds of sorcerous force. They must fail a STR resistance roll (STR x5 vs. the sorcerer's current PP total x5) or be immobilised: unable to move, attack, cast, or take significant physical action. They may still speak and observe.

Each round, the target may attempt a STR resistance roll to break free, but the difficulty is Difficult (halved STR). Once free, the spell is spent.

_He was not chained. There was nothing to see. He simply could not move, no matter how hard he tried, and that was the worst part._

---

### Iron Ward

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Counter-magic. When cast before a specific spell or sorcerous effect, Iron Ward resists it. Intensity 1 resists spells of level 1-2; intensity 2 resists up to level 3; intensity 3 resists up to level 5; intensity 4 resists any sorcerous working of any level.

When two sorcerers are contesting with Iron Ward and a competing spell, compare PP pools after casting costs. The working with the higher remaining PP behind it prevails.

_She smiled when she saw what he was preparing. She had seen that spell before._

---

### Leap of the Shadow Cat

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Enhances the target's Athletics (jump and climb) and Acrobatics skills: intensity 1 = +20%; intensity 2 = +40%; intensity 3 = +60%; intensity 4 = +80%. At intensity 4 the target can traverse vertical surfaces at normal movement speed and their falls never exceed 3 metres of effective height.

_She went up the palace wall like she was born to it. Which she was not. Which made watching it deeply unsettling._

---

### Liken Shape

**Cost:** 4 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW x10 rounds (approximately one hour)

The sorcerer or a touched target assumes the appearance of a specific person or creature the sorcerer has observed closely. The change is visual only - voice and movement can be approximated but not replicated. Close acquaintances of the target may sense something wrong with a Difficult Perception roll.

Maintaining the shape requires the sorcerer's concentration; they cannot cast other spells while sustaining Liken Shape.

A sorcerer who uses this spell to impersonate someone and is later seen through suffers a -30% social penalty with anyone present for the revelation.

_He wore the general's face for three hours. The general never learned what had been decided in his name._

---

### Mend the Mortal Frame

**Cost:** 3 PP + 1D3 Corruption
**Cast Time:** 1 round (emergency) or 10 minutes (careful)
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer knits a wound in themselves or a touched target, burning something that does not grow back to restore what was lost. The working heals 1D6+POW/5 (round up) HP. If used on a Major Wound that is still fresh (within the same scene), the Major Wound may be restored to a severe but non-crippling injury at the GM's discretion - this is not guaranteed and requires a Difficult Medicine roll from a healer or a second casting at the GM's option.

This spell does not restore lost limbs, cure disease, or reverse Corruption. It closes wounds. Nothing more.

_He screamed when the sorcerer's hands touched the wound, and the scream cut short when the pain was gone. He was alive. He did not ask how. Smart men did not ask._

**Cost Notes:** Casting this spell always causes 1D3 Corruption regardless of the standard casting Corruption roll. Sorcerous healing is not without its cost - the power drawn to knit flesh comes from the same place everything else comes from. This spell is not a routine tool. It is an emergency measure.

### Midnight

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Creates an area of complete and unnatural darkness within a radius of POW metres. No light source within the area functions; sorcerous light sources that do not originate from within the area may penetrate at the GM's discretion. Creatures with no other means of perception are effectively blind.

The darkness has a quality beyond mere absence of light. It presses.

_The torch went out. They all went out. And then something moved in the dark that was not one of them._

---

### Moonrise

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Creates a floating light source roughly equivalent to full moonlight, illuminating a radius of POW x2 metres. The light is cold and slightly blue-white. It moves with the sorcerer unless anchored to an object.

At the GM's option, creatures of the deep dark (certain undead, certain demons) are uncomfortable in moonrise light and take a -10% penalty to actions while within it.

_The light was beautiful and wrong and it cast shadows that moved when it shouldn't._

---

### Night Ear

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer's hearing becomes supernaturally precise. They can hear a whispered conversation at 100 metres, can identify individual footsteps in a crowd by sound alone, and can detect heartbeats and breathing in adjacent rooms. They suffer -20% to tasks requiring visual concentration (the ears overwhelm the eyes).

At the GM's option, sudden loud noises while under Night Ear cause 1D6 PP loss as the sorcerer recoils from the assault.

_He had heard the conversation from the other side of the palace wall. He had heard every word. He wished he had not._

---

### Pox of the Sorcerer

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer drains vitality from a target, reducing their PP pool by 1D3 per round for the duration. The target must fail a POW resistance roll each round or lose the PP. PP reduced this way are not transferred to the sorcerer - they are simply gone, dissipated into the sorcerous current.

A target drained to 0 PP collapses, unconscious, as per the standard rules.

_He felt his strength leaving him with each breath, and could not tell if it was fear or something else, and eventually it did not matter._

### See the Unseen

**Cost:** 3 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer perceives the strength and nature of magical auras within POW x5 metres. They can see how much PP a creature has remaining (roughly), identify whether an object bears an active sorcerous working, and perceive whether a creature is a bound demon or has demonic essence. They can identify whether a sorcerer has a patron relationship and very roughly what kind of patron.

This spell is essential before attempting to summon and bind a demon - it reveals whether the demon's POW is beyond the sorcerer's ability to contain.

_"Do not try to bind that one," he said quietly. "You cannot see what I see. I can. Do not try."_

### Shadow-Wrap

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

The sorcerer wraps themselves or a touched target in sorcerous protection. The effect functions as armour: intensity 1 = AP 2; intensity 2 = AP 4; intensity 3 = AP 6; intensity 4 = AP 8. This protection stacks with worn armour. It does not protect against Flaying Blade or Hell's Razor (see those entries), which ignore mundane protection but do not bypass sorcerous armour.

Shadow-Wrap appears to observers as a faint darkening of the air around the protected target, as if they cast shadows that belong to somewhere else.

_The blade struck. It should have opened him from shoulder to hip. Instead the sorcerer turned, uninjured, and the attacker's sword was cold._

### Shape Thief

**Cost:** 4 PP + 1 Corruption
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

A more extreme version of Liken Shape. The sorcerer does not merely appear as their target - they take on aspects of the target's physical nature. Cast on a human target: the sorcerer gains their STR, DEX, and SIZ for the duration while appearing to be them. Cast on an animal: the sorcerer becomes that species of animal, with full physical capabilities, for the duration.

The sorcerer retains their own INT and POW throughout. They cannot cast other spells while maintaining Shape Thief.

_He had been the wolf for an hour. When he returned to his own skin, he was quiet for a long time, and then he was not quite the same man again._

**Cost Notes:** Each use of Shape Thief to take on a non-human form causes 1 Corruption (in addition to the standard casting cost). The soul leaves a mark when it has worn a different body.

---

### Still the Mind

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** 1D6 rounds

The sorcerer confounds a target's reasoning. The target must fail a POW resistance roll or be unable to cast spells, plan coherently, or perform any task requiring concentration for the duration. They may still fight and move on instinct, but they cannot do anything that requires thinking clearly.

A target who is attacked while under Still the Mind may make a new POW resistance roll at the start of each following round to shake free.

_He opened his mouth to say the words. Nothing came. He stood there, sword in hand, unable to remember what swords were for._

### Summon Demon

**Cost:** 4 PP (plus whatever the bargaining costs)
**Cast Time:** 4 hours minimum (preparation and invocation)
**Duration:** Until released or binding broken

The sorcerer prepares a summoning circle, invokes a demon by the appropriate formula, and calls it into physical presence. The demon arrives. It is not bound; it has not agreed to anything. That negotiation is what comes next.

The mechanical procedure for the player's side of a summoning is covered in §7. The demon's behaviour, personality, demands, and what happens when the binding is broken are described in G4.

_The circle held. That was the important thing. As long as the circle held, everything was still a negotiation._

**Cost Notes:** Summoning a lesser demon causes Corruption as per the §3.3 table. Summoning a Greater Demon costs 1D3 additional Corruption regardless of the Corruption roll result.

### Sureness of Hand

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 4)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Enhances the target's manual or ranged combat skill: intensity 1 = +20%; intensity 2 = +40%; intensity 3 = +60%; intensity 4 = +80%. At intensity 4, the target's hands do not shake even under intense stress, and they receive a +10% bonus to any action that requires precise fine manipulation (a sorcerer enhancing a rogue's picklocking or a crossbow user's shot).

_"Just stay calm," the sorcerer said, and her hands were suddenly steady, and she made the shot._

---

### Undying Strength

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 3)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

Massively augments the target's physical power: intensity 1 = +1D6 STR; intensity 2 = +2D6 STR; intensity 3 = +3D6 STR. The bonus applies to damage modifier, Brawn (strength-based) rolls, and any STR-minimum weapon requirements for the duration.

At intensity 3, the target can lift objects that would ordinarily require a crew. The augmentation is visible - veins stand out, the target's frame appears to expand, and their breath becomes laboured.

When the spell ends, the target takes 1D3 fatigue damage (applied to HP) as the body pays the arrears.

_He picked up the gate - not opened it, picked it up - and threw it into the canal._

### Vampire's Kiss

**Cost:** 2 PP per intensity (max 3)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer drains vitality from a touched target, healing themselves or a third party. Intensity 1: drain 1D6 HP from target, restore 1D6 HP to the beneficiary; intensity 2: drain 2D6 HP; intensity 3: drain 3D6 HP. The target must fail a CON resistance roll or suffer the drain. On a success, the drain is halved.

The beneficiary cannot be healed above their maximum HP. The drained HP are transferred, not destroyed - the target has simply lost something the beneficiary now has.

_She touched him and he felt cold all the way through, and the woman beside him breathed easier, and he understood, finally, what he was for._

**Cost Notes:** Each use of this spell causes 1 Corruption (in addition to standard casting cost). Taking life to sustain life is not a neutral act.

### Veil of the Ancient

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW x2 rounds

Creates a magical alarm on a defined perimeter - a room's walls, the edges of a campsite, the threshold of a door. Any creature larger than a cat that crosses the perimeter triggers a silent alarm that the sorcerer perceives as a physical sensation: a cold rush, a sound no one else can hear, a flash behind the eyes.

The alarm does not stop intruders. It tells the sorcerer someone is there.

_He slept well in bad places, always. He always knew when someone came._

### Vermin's Eye

**Cost:** 1 PP per intensity (max 3)
**Cast Time:** 1 round
**Duration:** POW rounds

As Bird's Eye, but the sorcerer perceives through the eyes of a rat, snake, insect colony, or similarly small or base creature. The range is shorter (intensity 1: close; intensity 2: within 200m; intensity 3: within 1 km) but the targets are ubiquitous.

At intensity 3, the sorcerer may share perception with multiple creatures simultaneously, receiving overlapping sensory information that is difficult to parse (Difficult Idea roll to act on specific information from a particular creature).

_The rat had been under the table for the entire meeting. The sorcerer, in her room a street away, had heard every word._

### Wisdom of Wounds

**Cost:** 1 PP
**Cast Time:** 1 minute (ten rounds of careful examination)
**Duration:** Instantaneous

The sorcerer examines a wound, a body, a patient in extremis, and reads what happened with sorcerous clarity. They determine the exact cause, timing, and weapon type of any wound; can tell if a death was caused by poison (and roughly which poison); and know with certainty how close to death a living patient is.

This is a diagnostic tool. It does not heal. It only knows.

_"Three different blades. Two different nights. And the poison is something I've only read about." He paused. "Someone has gone to considerable trouble."_

## 7. Demon Summoning: The Player's Side

Summoning a demon is not like casting a spell. It is an extended ritual and then a negotiation. What the demon actually looks like, demands, and does is documented in G4. This section covers what the sorcerer needs to know at the table.

### 7.1 Preparation

Before invoking a demon, the sorcerer must:

**Prepare a summoning circle.** The circle is a physical boundary drawn with appropriate materials (chalk, ash, blood, ground bone - the formula varies by tradition) and inscribed with binding formulae. Preparing a circle takes one hour per spell level of the Summon Demon spell used (minimum 4 hours). The circle must be in a location the sorcerer has prepared in advance; a circle cannot be improvised under pressure.

**Research the target.** The sorcerer should know something about the demon they are calling: its nature (possessing spirit, flesh-eater, chaos-entity), its approximate power (See the Unseen helps here), and its true name if available. Summoning without a true name is possible but the demon arrives without the obligation that a name implies.

**Gather materials.** Different demons have different tastes. The GM (per G4) will describe what a specific demon prefers to be offered. Common materials: burning specific woods, spilled blood, particular metals, specific prayers in dead languages. Preparing without the appropriate materials does not prevent summoning but imposes penalties on the binding roll.

### 7.2 The Invocation

The sorcerer spends 4 PP and begins the Summon Demon casting - a minimum 4-hour working. At the end of the working, the demon is called. It does not appear inside the circle; it appears at the circle's edge, and whether it enters is its own choice.

If the sorcerer has the demon's true name, the demon is compelled to appear and to hear the sorcerer's offer. Without the name, a demon may simply not come (GM rolls in secret; high-power demons often choose not to answer uninvited calls).

If the sorcerer had other characters contributing PP via Chain of Being, those PP are available to pay the initial summoning cost and the subsequent binding.

### 7.3 The Bargain

The demon has desires. A lesser demon might want blood, PP, a body to wear for a defined period, a task done. A greater demon wants something that costs the sorcerer more: permanent POW, a service of the demon's choice to be called in at a future time, a third party delivered into the demon's reach.

Both parties state their offer and their demand. The demon can refuse, counter-offer, or accept. The sorcerer can refuse, counter-offer, or accept. This is a roleplay encounter, not a mechanical one - though the GM may call for rolls to represent the sorcerer's persuasion, their willpower under the demon's pressure, or their ability to tell when the demon is lying.

The bargain, once agreed, binds both parties. What exactly "binds" means for a demon who has not yet been formally bound by sorcerous working is the demon's concern, not the sorcerer's. Demons with integrity exist. They are not numerous.

### 7.4 The Binding

If the sorcerer wants the demon to serve for an extended period (rather than simply fulfilling a one-time deal), they must bind it. A bound demon is constrained by the sorcerer's will and name.

**Binding costs:** 1 permanent POW per binding, plus the demon must fail a POW resistance roll against the sorcerer's current PP total. If the demon succeeds, it is not bound. This is a real risk. If the binding fails, the demon is still present and is usually unhappy.

**Types of binding:**

- _Flesh binding:_ the demon is bound to the sorcerer's service directly. It follows the sorcerer, executes commands, and cannot harm the sorcerer for the duration.
- _Object binding:_ the demon is bound into an object (a weapon, an amulet, a sealed container). The demon's power is accessible through the object; the demon cannot act independently of it.
- _Place binding:_ the demon is bound to a location. It cannot leave that location but has great power within it.

**True name:** Once bound, the demon's true name is surrendered to the sorcerer. Knowing the name lets the sorcerer call and dismiss the demon at will (costing 1 PP each time) for the duration of the binding.

### 7.5 Release and Breaking

A binding ends in one of four ways:

- **The sorcerer releases the demon.** A conscious act; costs 1 PP; the demon is freed and the true name loses its binding power.
- **The demon is destroyed.** The binding ends immediately. The permanent POW cost is not recovered.
- **The sorcerer dies.** Active bindings end; object-bound and place-bound demons may persist.
- **Another sorcerer learns the true name and rebinds.** The demon now serves the new binder. The original sorcerer loses the binding.

What the demon does when freed depends entirely on the nature of the entity, the terms of the original bargain, and whether the sorcerer treated it well. The GM Book has guidance on demon responses to release.
